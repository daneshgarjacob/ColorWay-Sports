Netflix carried the Field of Dreams game between the Phillies and the Twins, and it brought a brand-new MLB scorebug with it. It is the most modern-looking graphic in baseball right now, and after an inning of watching it, we like it a lot more than we expected to.

## What Netflix Actually Built

It is a single rounded panel with a soft translucent dark fill, and it stacks in four zones:

- **A batter bar** across the top in the batting team's colour, carrying the lineup spot, the name, and the average on the right. Philadelphia red for Schwarber.
- **A pitcher bar** underneath in blue, with the pitch count on the right.
- **Two team chips** down the left, each a rounded rectangle in the team's own colour holding the cap logo and the run total. Red for the Phillies, navy for the Twins.
- **A right-hand column** with the Netflix wordmark, the base diamonds, and a bottom row carrying the inning arrow, the count, and the outs.

## What It Gets Right

**The rounded chips are the whole idea.** Most scorebugs put team abbreviations in a shared bar and separate them with a divider line. Netflix gave each club its own coloured container, which does two things at once: it identifies the team by colour instead of by three letters, and it makes the run totals easier to scan because each number sits inside its own field. On a broadcast where you might tune in cold, colour beats abbreviation every time.

**It is genuinely clear in HD.** The type is heavy, the contrast against the translucent panel is high, and nothing is competing for the same space. Scorebugs fail when they get dense, and this one has air in it.

**Using team colour on the batter bar is smart.** You always know who is hitting without reading anything, because the bar changes colour with the half inning. That is one less thing your eye has to process.

**Nothing is skeuomorphic.** No fake metal, no bevels, no drop shadows pretending the graphic is a physical object. It looks like software, in a good way, and it will age better than the textured bugs a lot of regional networks still run.

## What We Would Change

**It has no connection to the game it is covering.** This is the Field of Dreams game, in a cornfield in Dyersville, Iowa, one of the most visually distinctive events baseball puts on. The scorebug looks exactly like it would for a Tuesday game in April. There was room for something, a small mark, a colour treatment, a typographic nod, and Netflix left it generic. For a one-off event broadcast that is a miss.

**We want challenges per team on it.** Showing each club's remaining challenges is the single most useful piece of information a modern baseball bug can carry, and this one has the space for it in the bottom row.

To be clear about tonight specifically: **the Field of Dreams game is not ABS-equipped**, so there are no ball and strike challenges to display and their absence is not a flaw in this broadcast. The point is what happens when Netflix carries a game that does have the system. The layout should have a home for it.

A B+ is a good grade from us. The construction is clean, the colour logic is correct, and it reads instantly at broadcast size, which is the entire job. It does not reach the A range because a scorebug for a marquee one-off should feel like it belongs to that broadcast, and this one would look identical in any ballpark in the league.

## The Bottom Line

Netflix walked into baseball and immediately produced a cleaner graphic than most networks that have been doing this for decades. The team chips are the best idea in it, the legibility is excellent, and the restraint is the reason both work. Give it a challenge counter and let it acknowledge the game it is covering, and this is an A. Grade: B+.
