**The Boston Bruins retire Patrice Bergeron's No. 37 on Tuesday, December 1, 2026.** The ceremony is at TD Garden before Boston plays the **Colorado Avalanche**. It starts at about **6:10 p.m. ET** and airs only on **NESN**, with puck drop at **8 p.m.** on NESN and TNT. Bergeron becomes the **14th player in Bruins history with a retired number**, and the second this year after Zdeno Chara's No. 33 went up in January.

## Quick Answers

- **When do the Bruins retire Bergeron's number?** Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at TD Garden.
- **Who are the Bruins playing?** The Colorado Avalanche.
- **What time is the ceremony?** About 6:10 p.m. ET. The Bruins ask fans to be in their seats by 6:00 p.m. All times are subject to change.
- **What time is the game?** 8 p.m. ET. The NHL moved the puck drop to 8 p.m. in September to make room for the ceremony.
- **What channel is it on?** The ceremony is on NESN only, starting at 6:00 p.m. The game is on NESN in New England and TNT nationally, with NESN360 streaming. Radio is 98.5 The Sports Hub.
- **Who is hosting?** Andrew Ference, Bergeron's teammate on the 2011 Cup team, who also hosted Chara's ceremony.
- **Is there a giveaway?** Yes. Every ticket holder gets a Bergeron bobblehead, handed out at the doors.
- **How many numbers have the Bruins retired?** Bergeron's 37 makes 14.

## Who Patrice Bergeron Is, In Numbers

Bergeron played his whole career in Boston and wore **No. 37 in every one of his 19 seasons**. The numbers below are from the Bruins' June 18 announcement and NHL.com.

- **Drafted:** 2nd round, 45th overall, by Boston in 2003. He debuted on October 8, 2003, at 18.
- **Seasons:** 19, from 2003-04 to 2022-23, all with the Bruins.
- **Regular season:** 1,294 games, 427 goals, 613 assists, **1,040 points**. He is the fourth Bruin to reach 1,000 points and ranks third in team history in games, goals and points.
- **Playoffs:** 170 games, 50 goals, 78 assists. He shares the NHL record for Game 7 appearances, 14, with Chara.
- **Stanley Cup:** 2011. He scored two goals in Game 7 of the Final in Vancouver, including the Cup winner. Boston also reached the Final in 2013 and 2019.
- **Selke Trophy:** a record **six**, in 2012, 2014, 2015, 2017, 2022 and 2023. He was a finalist 12 seasons in a row, also a record.
- **Faceoffs:** 15,182 wins, a 57.9% career rate, third in NHL history behind Sidney Crosby and Anze Kopitar.
- **Captain:** from January 7, 2021, until he retired. He won the Mark Messier Leadership Award in his first season wearing the C.
- **Other honors:** King Clancy Trophy (2013), three All-Star Games, and the Triple Gold Club: Olympic gold in 2010 and 2014, the 2004 World Championship and the 2011 Cup.
- **Retired:** July 25, 2023, the day after his 38th birthday, after a final season in which Boston set NHL records with 65 wins and 135 points.
- **Hall of Fame:** inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame on November 9, 2026, in Toronto, in his first year of eligibility. The class also included Carey Price, Pekka Rinne, Keith Tkachuk, Cindy Curley and builder Brian Burke.

A few things stand out. Boston went **14 years without retiring a number**, between Neely's 8 in 2004 and Middleton's 16 in 2018, then retired four in nine seasons: Middleton, O'Ree, Chara and Bergeron. 2026 is the first calendar year the Bruins have raised two numbers since Shore and Clapper in 1947. And Bergeron and Chara, the captains who carried the 2011 Cup team, go up **ten and a half months apart**. When Chara's 33 was raised on January 15, Bergeron was one of the five former teammates who carried the banner onto the ice, with David Krejci, Tuukka Rask, Dennis Seidenberg and Mark Recchi.

## Who Wore No. 37 Before Bergeron

No Bruins star wore it before him, which is part of why the number is so clearly his. Hockey-Reference lists 13 Bruins in 37 before him, starting in 1985-86: Dave Pasin, Paul Beraldo, Alain Cote, Moe Lemay, Paul Guay, Lou Crawford, Wes Walz, Ralph Barahona, Dominic Lavoie, Vincent Riendeau, Clayton Beddoes, Mattias Timander and **Lee Goren**, who wore it through 2002-03, the season before Bergeron arrived. None of them wore it for more than three seasons. Bergeron wore it for 19.

## What Goes Up On Dec. 1

The Bruins have not shown the No. 37 banner ahead of time, and we will not guess at its design. It joins the 13 retired numbers already hanging in the TD Garden rafters. Based on the team's release, the night follows the pattern of Chara's ceremony in January: the same host, alumni arriving on a gold carpet, and the banner raised before the game. We will add what the banner looks like, who carried it and what the Bruins wore once it goes up.

On the jersey side, our [Bruins uniform schedule](/stories/bruins-uniform-schedule-2026-27) has Boston **expected in its black home sweater** for the Avalanche game. The team has not confirmed it, and Boston's brown Varsity Sweater is not scheduled for that night.

## The Sweaters Bergeron Wore In 20 Years

Bergeron's career runs through more Bruins uniform changes than almost any player in team history. Full details are in our [Boston Bruins uniform history](/stories/boston-bruins-uniform-history).

- **2003-2007, the late FleetCenter set:** black and white Spoked-B sweaters, with the gold **"Pooh Bear"** bear-head third still in use through 2005-06, then a black vintage third based on the 1970s sweater.
- **2007-2017, Reebok and the "Men in Black":** Reebok sharpened the Spoked-B in 2007, and from 2008 to 2016 the third was the laceless black V-neck with the bear on the front. Boston won the 2011 Cup in this era, so the Cup photos are the 2007 Reebok set.
- **Four Winter Classics:** Boston's outdoor sweaters at Fenway in 2010 (gold), Gillette Stadium in 2016 (black, BOSTON over a bear), Notre Dame Stadium in 2019 (brown and gold) and Fenway again in 2023 (black with vintage white) all came during his career. We ranked them in [every NHL Winter Classic jersey ranked](/stories/every-nhl-winter-classic-jersey-ranked).
- **2017-2023, Adidas:** small changes to the home and road set, plus the all-black alternate with the gold block B (2019-2023).
- **Reverse Retros:** the gold remix of the 1981-95 home sweater in 2020-21, and the white Pooh Bear in 2022-23, his last season.

He retired before the Centennial set (2023-24), the 2025 refresh and this season's brown Varsity Sweater.

## Our Take

Our standard for retiring a number is simple: a Hall of Famer, or a player who changed the franchise. Bergeron is both, and it is not close. He is a first-ballot Hall of Famer, the best defensive forward the league has ever measured by its own award, a Cup winner who scored the Cup winner, and a captain who played 19 years in one number for one team. Of the 14 numbers in the rafters, his is one of the easiest calls on the list.

The only argument is about timing. Boston waited three years after his retirement and raised him ten months after Chara. Putting the two 2011 captains up in the same calendar year is the right way to close that team's story.
