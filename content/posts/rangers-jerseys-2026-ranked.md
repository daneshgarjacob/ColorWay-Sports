The Rangers have quietly built one of the more balanced closets in the American League. Five looks, no dead weight, and a color palette that actually gets used instead of sitting in a drawer. The interesting part is that the two best jerseys here are the ones doing the least: the road gray and the royal blue alternate both win on the strength of the full package rather than on any one trick. The lettering is where we have our reservations, and that is what keeps the home white at the bottom. Here is every Texas Rangers jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

The home white is the jersey Texas wears for most games at Globe Life Field: a clean white base with "Rangers" in blue script across the chest, a wordmark the franchise brought back in 2020 for the first time since 1993, with red, white, and blue sleeve trim and the blue cap with its white block "T." It is the backbone of the rotation and the look the team defaults to more than any other.

It grades a B-, and the script is the reason. We like the Rangers, and we like almost everything about how this uniform is built, but the lettering on the chest has never quite landed for us. The cursive treatment is the weakest piece of an otherwise well-made uniform, and because the home white is essentially a white canvas with that script sitting in the middle of it, there is nothing else on the jersey to carry the load. Everything above this on the list either brings a color that does the heavy lifting or pairs the wordmark with a package that works harder. A perfectly fine home uniform that simply asks its weakest element to do the most.

## 4. Tejas City Connect

Unveiled in April 2026 as a full reset of the program, "Tejas" replaced the old cream Peagle City Connect. It is a deep cochineal-red jersey with "Tejas" spelled across the chest, the Caddo word for "friend" that gave the state its name, paired with a matching red cap and white pants. A papel picado motif with the outline of Texas sits on the sleeve, with charro-inspired detailing throughout, leaning into the Mexican heritage woven through Dallas and Fort Worth. It owns Friday night home games.

It grades a B-, and it earns that on a principle we will defend every time: the Rangers built their City Connect out of their own colors. Red is a real Texas Rangers color, not a costume borrowed for a marketing cycle, and the way the red carries the whole jersey is genuinely satisfying. That is the thing most City Connects get wrong and this one gets right. Where it stalls for us is how completely it commits, with almost nothing else in the palette to break up all that red. It ties with the home white, but for the opposite reason: the white asks too little of its jersey, and the Tejas asks a single color to do everything.

The powder blue is the fan favorite and it owns Sunday home games. It runs the same template as the home white but swaps the base for a soft light blue, with the "Rangers" script and the trim in red, plus a powder-blue cap with a darker brim. It is a throwback to the baby-blue road sets Texas wore through the late 1970s and 1980s, brought back as part of the 2020 rebrand.

It grades a B+, and this is the one we genuinely enjoy. The soft blue base does exactly what the plain white could not: it gives the red trim something to play against and gives the whole uniform a personality before you have even read the chest. The club actually considered moving it off Sundays and thought better of it once fans made their preference clear, which tells you plenty. On a sunny afternoon at Globe Life Field it is the best-looking thing in the building, and it climbs above both B- looks on the strength of that color alone.

On the road, Texas wears the standard gray set with the "TEXAS" wordmark across the chest and the blue cap. It is the look the Rangers carry for most away games, and it is the quiet workhorse of the closet.

It grades an A-, and the pants are a big part of why. This is a uniform that works as a complete outfit rather than as a jersey with some legwear attached, and the gray trousers are cut and shaded exactly right, giving the whole thing a clean, professional line from cap to cleat. Plenty of road grays feel like an obligation. This one feels considered. The gray base also gives the wordmark a more forgiving backdrop than the stark white does at home, which is a real part of why this clears the home white by such a margin.

The royal blue alternate is the wild card and the best jersey Texas owns. It is a bright royal-blue top carrying the same "TEXAS" wordmark as the road grays, worn with standard gray road pants and the blue cap. There is no fixed day attached to it. It is the jersey the Rangers reach for when they want a jolt of color away from home, and it turns up fairly often across a full season without ever being scheduled.

It grades an A-, and it takes the top spot because it is the look we have always liked most. Royal blue is the color this franchise is actually built on, and seeing it as the base of a jersey rather than as trim on someone else's is the most confident the Rangers ever look. It carries the same wordmark as the road gray, but a saturated blue field does more for that lettering than gray or white ever could. The absence of a set schedule is the final piece: because it is the manager's call rather than a standing Tuesday, it keeps a little event energy every time it shows up. The best thing in the closet, and it is not the one with the biggest story attached.

## The Bottom Line on the Rangers Jersey Rankings

Texas has a closet with no genuine failures in it, which is more than most clubs can say. The problem, to the extent there is one, is the chest lettering: the script does not do enough work for us, and the two jerseys that lean hardest on it against a plain background are the two that finish last.

Everything above that solves the same problem in a different way. The powder blue brings a color that gives the red trim something to argue with. The road gray wins on a complete package, pants included, rather than on any single element. And the royal blue alternate simply puts the franchise's real color on the field and lets it carry the jersey, which is why it comes out on top.

The Tejas City Connect deserves credit for the thing most City Connects miss. It is built from the team's actual colors instead of an invented palette, and that alone puts it ahead of a lot of the league's attempts. It just commits so hard to red that there is nothing left to balance it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Rangers jersey for 2026?**

We rank the royal blue alternate No. 1 with an A-. It is a bright royal-blue top with the "TEXAS" wordmark, worn with gray road pants, and it has no fixed spot on the schedule, which keeps it feeling like an event. The road gray is a close second, also at an A-.

**How many uniforms do the Rangers have in 2026?**

Five: the home whites, the road grays, the powder blue alternate, the royal blue road alternate, and the Tejas City Connect.

**When do the Rangers wear the powder blue?**

Sunday home games at Globe Life Field. The club considered moving it to a different slot and kept it on Sundays because it tested as the more popular look with fans.

**When do the Rangers wear the Tejas City Connect in 2026?**

Friday night home games. The Tejas kit debuted on Friday, April 24 against the Athletics and has held the standing Friday-night home slot since.

**What happened to the Rangers' old City Connect?**

The cream "Peagle" City Connect was retired when the Tejas kit launched in April 2026. The Tejas jersey was a full reset of the program rather than a refresh.
