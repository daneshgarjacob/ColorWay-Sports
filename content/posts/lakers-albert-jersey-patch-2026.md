The Los Angeles Lakers have a new jersey patch, and it is the biggest change to the front of their uniform in five years. Starting with the 2026-27 season, the small advertising patch on the upper-left chest will read **Albert**, the personal-finance app, replacing the Bibigo mark that had been there since 2021. According to ESPN's Dave McMenamin, a league source pegged the deal at an annual value north of **$30 million**, which makes it one of the richer jersey-patch partnerships in the NBA.

## Who Is Albert, the New Lakers Patch Sponsor?

Albert is a personal-finance app, a "personal financial assistant" that automates saving, budgeting, and money management, and the company says it is trusted by more than 20 million people. It joins a wave of fintech and financial-services brands buying the most valuable few square inches in the NBA. The patch is small, but it lives on every broadcast, every highlight, and every jersey sold, which is exactly why the category keeps bidding these deals up. For Albert, landing on the Lakers, one of the most-watched teams on earth, is about as much national exposure as that little rectangle can buy.

## The Design: Why the Albert Patch Works

The reason this one lands is restraint. The Albert wordmark is set in a soft, rounded serif in Lakers purple, and on the gold Icon jersey it sits on a gold patch so the whole thing reads tonally, purple type on gold, the team's own two colors and nothing else. There is no competing logo color, no busy mark, no fourth color crashing the palette. That is the entire difference between a patch that looks like an ad and a patch that looks like part of the uniform.

The Lakers confirmed the patch will appear on all four of their uniform editions: the gold **Icon**, the purple **Statement**, the white **Association**, and the black **City** edition. Expect the wordmark to flip to gold or white on the darker jerseys so it stays legible, the same way the best patch programs adapt per uniform.

## Ranking the Lakers' Three Jersey Patches: Wish, Bibigo, Albert

The Lakers have now worn three jersey patches since the NBA introduced them in 2017, and it is a genuine glow-up.

- **Albert (2026-27 onward) — the best of the three.** Tonal, clean, and color-matched to the uniform. The first Lakers patch that looks intentional.
- **Bibigo (2021-2026) — the middle child.** The Korean food brand's blue-and-white mark never sat comfortably in a purple-and-gold world. It was fine, never right.
- **Wish (2017-2021) — the weakest.** The plain blue Wish wordmark was the definition of a sticker slapped on a legendary jersey, and it drew years of fan complaints for exactly that reason.

If you graded the three, Albert is comfortably an **A-**, Bibigo a **C+**, and Wish a **C-**. Getting the patch to disappear into the uniform is the whole job, and Albert is the first one to pull it off.

## The Bottom Line on the Lakers' New Albert Patch

A jersey patch is a small thing that fans notice every single game, and for nine years the Lakers wore ones that never quite fit. Albert changes that. It is a reported $30 million-plus win for the business side and, more importantly for us, the first Lakers patch that respects the most iconic uniform in basketball. Purple on gold, clean type, no clutter. We are on board. Bring it on for 2026-27.
