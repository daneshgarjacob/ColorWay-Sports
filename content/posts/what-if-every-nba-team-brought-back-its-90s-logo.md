**The 1990s gave the NBA a purple dinosaur, a dribbling hornet, a teal horse and a cartoon rocket.** Some of those logos are still the most-loved marks the league has ever had. Some of them aged badly. So let's play it out: what if every NBA team threw out its current primary logo and went back to the one it used in the '90s?

We went through all 30 teams. For each one: what the '90s logo was, what the team uses now, and our verdict on whether the switch would make it look better or worse. The verdicts are our opinions. The years and details are checked against team histories, NBA releases and newspaper archives.

This is a hypothetical. No team is switching back full time. And a few teams couldn't do it even if they wanted to, for reasons we explain at the end.

## Quick Answers

- **Better with the '90s logo (5):** Raptors, Spurs, Nuggets, Hornets, Mavericks.
- **Worse with the '90s logo (9):** Bucks, Hawks, Pistons, Cavaliers, Warriors, Rockets, Clippers, Nets, Kings.
- **Barely changes, or already went back (12):** Bulls, Celtics, Lakers, Heat, Knicks, Pacers, Suns, Trail Blazers, 76ers, Jazz, Magic, Timberwolves.
- **Can't really do it (4):** Thunder, Pelicans, Grizzlies, Wizards. Their '90s logos belong to a different city or a name the team dropped.
- **Our most controversial call:** the purple Bucks deer should stay retired.

## Why Old Logos Don't Just Come Back

Teams can't swap logos on a whim. A new NBA identity takes three to four years, because merchandise makers need lead time, and the original uniforms are licensed to Mitchell & Ness for Hardwood Classics throwbacks. The Orlando Magic's chief marketing officer walked us through that in our [Magic rebrand interview](/stories/how-nba-uniforms-are-made-orlando-magic-rebrand).

That's why the '90s mostly comes back through side doors: Classic Editions, City Editions, a stripe on the shorts. This story asks what would happen if the side door became the front door. For every team's current closet, see our [2026-27 NBA uniforms guide](/stories/nba-uniforms-2026-27).

The Raptors revealed their first logo on May 24, 1994, a red cartoon raptor with a basketball, with purple as a team color. It sold. By the end of 1994 the Raptors were seventh in the league in merchandise sales. Toronto dropped purple from the logo for the 2008-09 season and replaced the dinosaur entirely with a basketball torn by claw marks, introduced in December 2014 and used full time from 2015-16.

The dinosaur hasn't gone anywhere in the fans' minds. The Dino Dunk City Edition, a Vince Carter tribute with the cartoon raptor on the chest, is reported to be back this season, and a purple Statement jersey is reported for 2027-28, as we covered in [NBA new uniforms 2027-28](/stories/nba-new-uniforms-2027-28-raptors-wizards-pacers-spurs).

The Spurs logo has always been the word SPURS with a cowboy spur standing in for the U. From 1989 to 2002, the team added streaks of pink, orange and turquoise, the colors of San Antonio's Fiesta celebration, while the uniforms themselves stayed black, silver and white. The Fiesta colors left the logo in 2002 and the wordmark went from straight to arched.

**Verdict: better.** Silver and black is classy, and five titles came in it. But it's also the quietest color story in the league. The Fiesta colors are what San Antonio actually looks like, and every time the Spurs bring them back on a City Edition, fans line up for it. Put them back on the main logo. But the rainbow skyline is one of the best logos the NBA has ever had, and the team keeps bringing it back on City Editions for a reason. <p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin: 0.25em 0 1.5em;">Current logo. The 1988-2002 Hugo logo is described below.</p>


The original Hornets logo was a teal and purple cartoon hornet in white shoes and gloves, dribbling an orange basketball. It ran from the team's first season in 1988 until the franchise left for New Orleans in 2002. When the Bobcats took the Hornets name back for 2014-15, Charlotte also got the 1988-2002 history and records, and Hugo returned as the mascot. **Verdict: better, but closer than you'd think.** The current hornet is good. It's one of the better logos from the 2010s. <p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin: 0.25em 0 1.5em;">Current logo. The 1993-2006 purple and green deer is described below.</p>


The Bucks changed their logo and uniforms for the 1993-94 season, after the franchise's 25th anniversary, going to purple and green with a new deer. Former NBA creative director Tom O'Grady has said the colors came from a package he got in 1992 with a dark green cap and a purple shirt from Wimbledon. That look lasted until 2006. The current buck head and the cream and green colors arrived on April 13, 2015.

**Verdict: worse.** We know the purple deer has a cult following. But the 2015 buck is one of the best logos in the NBA right now: bold, simple and readable at any size, with an M for Milwaukee in the chest and a basketball hidden between the antlers. The Grizzlies started in 1995 as the Vancouver Grizzlies, in turquoise, bronze and red. Their primary logo was a brown grizzly holding a red basketball under the word "Vancouver." The team moved to Memphis in 2001, kept those uniforms with the new city name until 2004, and then switched to navy, Beale Street blue and gold. **Verdict: better-looking, but it says Vancouver.** The turquoise and red are great, and a Memphis version of the full-body bear would beat the current bear head. ### Washington Wizards

<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin: 0.25em 0 1.5em;">Current logo. The Bullets and 1997 Wizards logos are described below.</p>


For most of the '90s the team was the Washington Bullets, and from 1987 to 1997 the logo was the word "Bullets" with the two L's drawn as hands reaching for a basketball. In 1997 owner Abe Pollin changed the name to Wizards because he did not want the team associated with violence. The first Wizards logo was a wizard conjuring a basketball with a quarter moon, in blue, bronze and black. Washington went back to red, white and blue in 2011 and has used the monument ball as its primary since 2015. Team executives told The Athletic this month they're aiming for new uniforms for 2027-28, with a possible new logo on a longer timeline.

**Verdict: the Bullets logo can't come back, and the moon wizard shouldn't.** The hands logo is good design tied to a name the team retired on purpose. The 1997 wizard is the worst '90s logo in this story. [Wizards uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/wizards-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What was the Raptors' original logo?**

A red cartoon raptor with a basketball, revealed on May 24, 1994, with purple as one of the team colors. Purple was dropped from the logo for 2008-09, and the clawed-basketball logo replaced the dinosaur starting with the 2015-16 season.

**Why did the Raptors get rid of the dinosaur logo?**

Toronto dropped purple in 2008 and then rebranded around a black, silver and red look and a clawed basketball logo, introduced in December 2014. The dinosaur still shows up on City Edition jerseys, including the Dino Dunk design returning for 2026-27.

**When did the Spurs have the Fiesta-color logo?**

From 1989 to 2002. The SPURS wordmark with the spur in place of the U picked up pink, orange and turquoise streaks for those years, while the uniforms stayed black, silver and white.

**What was the Bucks' logo in the 1990s?**

A purple and green deer, introduced for the 1993-94 season and used until 2006. The current buck head and cream, green and blue colors were unveiled on April 13, 2015.

**What was the Hornets' original logo?**

Hugo, a teal and purple cartoon hornet in white shoes and gloves dribbling a basketball, used from 1988 to 2002. Charlotte got the Hornets name and the 1988-2002 history back for the 2014-15 season.

**Why don't the Pelicans have a '90s logo?**

The franchise played in Charlotte as the Hornets until 2002. When it became the Pelicans in 2013, the history and records of the 1988-2002 Hornets went to the current Charlotte Hornets.

**Can the Thunder use the SuperSonics logo?**

No. When the team moved to Oklahoma City in 2008, the settlement with Seattle left the SuperSonics name, logo and colors available to a future Seattle NBA team, subject to league approval.

**Why did the Washington Bullets change their name?**

Owner Abe Pollin changed the name to Wizards in 1997 because he did not want the team associated with violence. The change came with a new logo, new colors and the move to the new downtown arena.

**What was the Rockets' logo in 1995?**

After winning titles in 1994 and 1995 in red and yellow, the Rockets switched to navy and silver with a cartoon-style rocket orbiting a basketball. It lasted until the 2003 "R." The 2026-27 rebrand brought back red with Championship Yellow.

**Which NBA teams have already brought back their old logos?**

The Magic unveiled a modernized version of their 1989-2000 "streaking ball with stars" logo ahead of 2025-26. The Timberwolves' 2026-27 crest modernizes their original 1989 wolf head. The Pistons (2017) and 76ers (2009, refreshed 2015) both went back to their 1970s-90s ball logos, and the Jazz's purple J-note is a return to the logo they used from 1979 to 1996.

**Why can't NBA teams just bring back their old logos?**

A new identity takes three to four years because merchandise makers need lead time, and the production rights to the original uniforms are licensed to Mitchell & Ness for Hardwood Classics. That's why old looks usually return as Classic or City Editions first.

**What was the Nuggets' logo in the 1990s?**

The rainbow city skyline, used from 1981 until 1993, and then a navy snow-capped mountain over a gold western-style "Nuggets" with a maroon "Denver" ribbon, which led the team's identity until the pick-axe logo became primary in 2018-19.
