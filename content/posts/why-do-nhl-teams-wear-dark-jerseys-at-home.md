**In the NHL, the home team wears its dark sweater and the visitors wear white. It has been that way since the 2003-04 season, and it is the opposite of what the league did for the 33 seasons before that.** From 1970-71 through 2002-03, NHL home teams wore white and the visitors brought their colors to town.

So why do NHL teams wear dark jerseys at home now? The short answer is third jerseys and selling them. Nearly every third jersey was dark, teams wanted to wear them in front of their own fans, and a dark-at-home rule made that easy. Here is how the rule flipped back and forth, what the league said at the time, and the home teams that are wearing white anyway this season.

## Quick Answers

- **Why do NHL teams wear dark at home?** Since 2003-04, the rule has been dark at home and white on the road. ESPN reported in 2025 that the switch was made in part so home teams could wear their third jerseys in front of their fans. Most of those thirds were dark.
- **When did the NHL switch to dark at home?** The 2003-04 season. The league approved it in December 2002. It was the first season since 1969-70 that home teams wore dark.
- **Did NHL home teams used to wear white?** Yes. From 1970-71 through 2002-03, the home team wore white (the Kings and Seals wore yellow) and the visitors wore dark.
- **Why did the NHL go to white at home in 1970?** White sweaters were hard to keep clean on the road. NHL chief branding officer Brian Jennings told ESPN that white showed "a lot of stains and puck marks."
- **Do any NHL teams wear white at home now?** Yes, on certain nights. In 2026-27 the Islanders, Canadiens, Avalanche and Stars all have white Hometown Remix jerseys they wear at home, and the Flyers and Golden Knights have each worn their regular white at home once already.
- **Our take (opinion):** dark at home is right for hockey. A home crowd in team colors with the team in the same color is the best look in the sport. The league just needs more nights where the visitors get to wear their color too.

## Before 1970: The Home Team Wore Dark

<img src="/images/posts/nhl-daily-tracker/canadiens-home.jpg" alt="Montreal Canadiens red home sweater with the CH crest and blue chest stripe" style="display: block; width: 100%; max-width: 320px; margin: 1.25em auto 0.4em; border-radius: 10px;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin: 0.25em 0 1.5em;">The Canadiens' red, the kind of sweater home crowds saw before 1970 and see again now</p>

For most of the Original Six era, the home team wore its color and the visitors wore white. A City of Toronto Archives photo from Maple Leaf Gardens, taken between 1960 and 1962, shows exactly that: Johnny Bower in the Leafs' dark sweater, the Chicago Black Hawks in white. The uniform historians at NHLUniforms.com list 1955-56 as the season the NHL required dark sweaters for the home team and white for the visitors.

It was not always that tidy. Some histories say the league briefly did the opposite in 1951-52, home teams in white and visitors in color, so viewers could tell the teams apart on black-and-white television. We could not find a primary source for that one. Either way, by the late 1950s dark at home was the rule, and it stayed that way through 1969-70.

## 1970-71: The NHL Flips To White At Home

<img src="/images/posts/nhl-daily-tracker/flyers-road.jpg" alt="Philadelphia Flyers white sweater with the orange shoulders and black Flyers logo" style="display: block; width: 100%; max-width: 320px; margin: 1.25em auto 0.4em; border-radius: 10px;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin: 0.25em 0 1.5em;">The Flyers' white. From 1970 to 2003, this was the home look in Philadelphia</p>

Starting with the 1970-71 season, the NHL reversed it: home teams in white, visitors in dark. (The Los Angeles Kings and California Golden Seals wore yellow at home instead of white.)

The reason was practical. Brian Jennings, the NHL's chief branding officer, told ESPN's Greg Wyshynski in December 2025 that road teams moved to color in the 1970s because "white jerseys would show a lot of stains and puck marks." Paul Lukas, writing in The Village Voice in 2003, said the same thing: teams had trouble keeping white sweaters clean on road trips, so the league put the whites at home where the laundry was. Some accounts also credit Hockey Night in Canada, which wanted color broadcasts to show off the visitors' dark sweaters, but we have not found that in a primary source.

For 33 seasons, the white jersey was the home jersey. A generation of fans grew up seeing the Bruins in white at the Garden and every visiting team in its own color.

## 2003-04: The NHL Flips Back To Dark At Home

<img src="/images/posts/nhl-daily-tracker/avalanche-home.jpg" alt="Colorado Avalanche burgundy home sweater with blue shoulders and the A crest" style="display: block; width: 100%; max-width: 320px; margin: 1.25em auto 0.4em; border-radius: 10px;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin: 0.25em 0 1.5em;">Colorado's burgundy home sweater. Since 2003-04, this is what the home team wears</p>

The NHL's board of governors approved the switch in December 2002, and it took effect for the 2003-04 season. Home teams went back to dark and the road teams went back to white for the first time since 1969-70. It has stayed that way for more than 20 years, and the NHL rulebook still requires every club to wear "distinctive and contrasting uniforms for their home and road games" (Rule 9.1).

## The Reason: Third Jerseys And Selling Them

The NHL started its third jersey program in 1995-96. Within a few years many teams had one, and nearly all of them were dark. Under the white-at-home rule, that created two problems.

1. **The home look was white.** A team's best-selling colors were on its road jersey, worn in someone else's building most nights.
2. **The visitors had to pack twice.** When a home team wore a dark third, the visitor could not wear its dark road sweater, so it had to carry a white set too.

Flipping the rule fixed both. ESPN's 2025 report says the 2003-04 change was made "in part, so home teams could wear their third jerseys in front of their fans." At the time, the league put it more gently: Lukas quoted the NHL as saying it wanted "to give a new generation of fans the opportunity to see dark jerseys on home ice." Lukas, and plenty of fans since, read it as a merchandise move. The fans sitting in the arena are the ones most likely to buy the jersey, so you want them looking at the jersey you are selling.

## What Fans Lost

The old rule had one big upside. Under white at home, a Rangers fan at Madison Square Garden saw the Blackhawks in red one night, the Bruins in black the next and the North Stars in green after that. Every visitor showed up in its best color.

Now almost every visitor shows up in white. That is what the league has started trying to fix. In December 2025 the board of governors told the league it wanted more "color vs. color" games, where both teams wear dark, especially for rivalry nights and national TV games. Jennings told ESPN that 57% of that season's games had two teams with enough contrast to try it and 43% did not. White road jerseys are not going away, but the league is no longer treating dark-versus-white as the only option.

**Regular whites at home.** Two teams have already worn their standard white road sweater at home this season:

- **Philadelphia Flyers, October 17** vs. the Senators. The Flyers wore white and Ottawa wore black.
- **Vegas Golden Knights, October 30** vs. the Devils. Vegas wore white at T-Mobile Arena and New Jersey wore red. The Devils won 5-2.

## How The NBA, NFL And MLB Do It

- **NBA:** No home or road jersey at all since Nike took over in 2017-18. The home team picks from its set and the visitor picks a contrasting one. We dug into it in [why NBA teams don't wear white at home](/stories/why-nba-teams-dont-wear-white-at-home).
- **NFL:** The home team picks white or color, and most pick color. A few, led by the Cowboys and Dolphins, wear white at home on purpose. See [which NFL teams wear white at home](/stories/nfl-teams-that-wear-white-at-home-2026).
- **MLB:** Still the old way. Home teams wear white, visitors wear gray, with alternates mixed in.

We compare all four in [who decides which jersey teams wear](/stories/who-decides-which-uniforms-teams-wear-each-game).

## Our Take

Dark at home is the right rule for hockey. The best atmosphere in the sport is a full building in the home team's color with the team in the same color, and white-at-home never matched that. But the league gave up something real in 2003. The Bruins in black at the Forum, the Blackhawks in red at the Garden, the North Stars in green anywhere: that variety was part of watching a road game, and it is mostly gone. The color-vs.-color push is the right fix. Keep dark at home, and let the visitors bring their color on rivalry nights.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do NHL teams wear dark jerseys at home?**

Because the NHL changed its rule for the 2003-04 season. The switch was made in part so teams could wear their third jerseys, which were almost all dark, in front of their home fans, who are the most likely to buy them. The league said it wanted a new generation of fans to see dark jerseys on home ice.

**When did NHL home teams start wearing dark jerseys?**

The 2003-04 season. The NHL's board of governors approved the change in December 2002. Before 1970, home teams also wore dark.

**Did NHL teams used to wear white at home?**

Yes. From the 1970-71 season through 2002-03, NHL home teams wore white (the Kings and Seals wore yellow) and road teams wore their dark sweaters.

**Why did the NHL switch to white at home in 1970?**

White sweaters were hard to keep clean on long road trips. The NHL's Brian Jennings told ESPN in 2025 that white showed stains and puck marks, so the league moved the dark sweaters to the road.

**What was the NHL rule before 1970?**

Home teams wore dark and visitors wore white. NHLUniforms.com dates the dark-at-home rule to 1955-56. Some histories say the league had home teams in white for a short time in the early 1950s, but we have not found a primary source for it.

**Can an NHL team wear white at home?**

Yes, with the league's approval. The visitor then wears its dark sweater. Rule 9.1 only says the two teams' uniforms must contrast.

**Which NHL teams wear white at home in 2026-27?**

The Islanders (10 games), Canadiens (8), Avalanche (5) and Stars (3) wear white Hometown Remix jerseys at home. The Flyers (October 17 vs. Ottawa) and Golden Knights (October 30 vs. New Jersey) also wore their regular white at home once.

**What is a color vs. color game in the NHL?**

A game where neither team wears white. In December 2025 the board of governors asked the league for more of them, especially rivalry games and national TV games, and the NHL said 57% of that season's games had enough contrast to qualify.

**Do NHL road teams always wear white?**

Almost always. The exceptions are games where the home team wears white and color-vs.-color games, like the 2014 Winter Classic between the Red Wings and Maple Leafs, which the league now wants more of.

**When did the NHL start third jerseys?**

The NHL's third jersey program started in the 1995-96 season. Within a few years many teams had one, and most were dark.

**Do NBA teams wear white at home?**

Not by rule. The NBA dropped home and road jerseys in 2017-18. Our playoff tracker found home teams in white only about a third of the time, which we explain in [why NBA teams don't wear white at home](/stories/why-nba-teams-dont-wear-white-at-home).

**Does MLB still have home whites?**

Yes. MLB home teams wear white and road teams wear gray, with alternates and City Connect jerseys mixed in.
