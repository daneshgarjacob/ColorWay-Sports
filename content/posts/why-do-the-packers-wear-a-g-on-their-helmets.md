**The G on the Packers' helmet is younger than most people think.** Green Bay played more than 40 seasons without a G on the helmet. The G arrived in 1961, because Vince Lombardi wanted a logo, and it was sketched by a college art student who was working for the team's equipment manager. His first version was shaped like a football.

Sixty-five years later it is one of the best-known marks in sports, it has outlasted every uniform change in Green Bay, and it even stayed on the cream helmet the Packers wore for the first time this season. Here is where the G came from, what it stands for, when it changed shape, and why Georgia and Grambling wear one too.

## Quick Answers

- **Why do the Packers wear a G on their helmets?** Vince Lombardi wanted a logo for the helmet. The G, for Green Bay, was first worn in 1961.
- **What does the G stand for?** Green Bay. Packers team historian Cliff Christl wrote that the popular "greatness" story is "completely unfounded."
- **Who designed the Packers G?** John Gordon, an art student at St. Norbert College who was assisting equipment manager Gerald "Dad" Braisher. His sketch was approved within 24 hours, according to the Associated Press.
- **When did the Packers first wear the G?** August 5, 1961, in the team's annual intra-squad game at old City Stadium, according to Christl.
- **Did the Packers have a G when they played for the 1960 NFL title?** No. It was not on the helmet until 1961.
- **Was the G always an oval?** No. The original was shaped like a football. Christl dates the first change to the current oval to before the 1970 season.
- **Does Georgia pay the Packers for its G?** No. Georgia asked permission in 1964 and got it, and a Georgia athletics spokesman told the Atlanta Journal-Constitution the school does not pay the Packers.
- **Is the G on the cream Rivalries helmet?** Yes. The Packers' own photos from the October 11 game against the Bears show the G on the cream matte helmet. The interlocking GB logo is on the jersey chest.
- **Our take (opinion):** Green Bay should bring back the football-shaped G on a throwback helmet.

## No G Until 1961

For most of their history, the Packers' helmets said nothing. Christl, the team's historian, wrote on Packers.com in 2015 that there was no G on the helmet until 1961, which means it was not there when Green Bay played Philadelphia for the 1960 NFL championship, Lombardi's first title game.

## Lombardi Wanted A Logo

The request came from the top. According to the Associated Press obituary of John Gordon, published in October 2023, equipment manager Gerald "Dad" Braisher came back from a meeting with Vince Lombardi with an assignment: a logo, a G in the shape of a football.

Braisher handed it to his assistant. Gordon was an art student at St. Norbert College in De Pere, just outside Green Bay. He sketched the G, and the design was approved within 24 hours, the AP reported. Christl's 2015 history also credits the sketch to Gordon. Gordon went on to teach art in Green Bay and later as an adjunct professor at St. Norbert. He died in 2023 at 83.

Why a G and not a P for Packers? Christl's answer: "No doubt because Vince Lombardi wanted it that way."

## First Worn August 5, 1961

The G made its debut in a practice game, not a regular-season one. Christl dates the first appearance to the Packers' annual intra-squad game on August 5, 1961, at old City Stadium, the Packers' home field before the stadium now called Lambeau Field opened in 1957. That fall Green Bay won the 1961 NFL championship, the first of five NFL titles under Lombardi, and the G was on the helmet for all five, through the 1967 season.

## From A Football To An Oval

Lombardi asked for a G shaped like a football, and that is what Gordon drew. The G on the helmet today is an oval. Christl went through old photos and concluded that "the change – or at least the first change – was made prior to the 1970 season." He did not find a reason. His explanation is only that it "was a tumultuous time in Packers history": Lombardi had stepped down as coach after the 1967 season, and the team was between eras.

So the football-shaped G belongs to the Lombardi dynasty, and the oval belongs to everything after it.

## The G Stands For Green Bay, Not Greatness

If you have heard that the G stands for "greatness," you have heard a story that Christl traces to 2011. Christl traced it to former NFL running back Tiki Barber, who "concocted a story on media day" before Super Bowl XLV, the Packers' win over Pittsburgh. "Although completely unfounded," Christl wrote, "the story spread like wildfire on the Web." His conclusion, and the title of his column: the G stands for Green Bay.

## Why Georgia's G Looks Like The Packers' G

Georgia's red helmet carries a black and white G that looks a lot like Green Bay's, and it came three years later. Christl wrote that Georgia coach Vince Dooley proposed putting a G on the helmet, that it was designed by a commercial artist married to a newly hired assistant coach with no ties to the Packers, and that Georgia first wore it in 1964. Because it looked so much like Green Bay's, athletic director Joel Eaves "called for permission which was granted," Christl wrote. The Atlanta Journal-Constitution has identified the designer as Anne Donaldson, wife of assistant coach John Donaldson.

Georgia does not pay for it. Athletics spokesman Claude Felton told the Journal-Constitution's Q&A on the News column that the school does not pay the Packers to use the G.

## Grambling Has One Too

Grambling State's black helmet carries a gold G in the same family. In 2005 the Associated Press reported that the Packers hold the trademark on their G and say they "granted limited permission to use a similar mark" to Georgia in 1964 and to Grambling. Grambling had applied to register its G in 1997, and the application was rejected in 1998, the AP reported. Nobody the AP talked to could recall exactly how Grambling got permission. In March 2026, HBCU Gameday reported that Grambling had finally secured federal registration of its G.

## The G Goes Wherever The Packers Go

The G has gone onto other helmet colors, too. The white alternate helmet the Packers wore against Washington in September 2025 had a green G on white.

The newest test came this season. Green Bay's Rivalries uniform debuted against the Bears at Lambeau Field on Sunday, October 11, a 31-7 Packers win. The Packers' August 25 reveal described "a matching matte helmet with green and gold striping and a matte green facemask," with the interlocking GB logo "on the chest" of the jersey. The release did not say what would be on the helmet. The team's [game photos from October 11](https://www.packers.com/photos/photos-jordan-love-slings-packers-to-victory-vs-bears-2026) answer it: the cream matte helmet carries the oval G, green on cream with a gold outline, and the GB stays on the jersey.

## Our Take

The oval G is perfect, and nobody should touch it. But the football-shaped G is the one the Packers won five championships in, and it has not been the everyday G since the 1960s. A Lombardi-era throwback with the original football G would be the best alternate in the NFL, and it would tell the real story of the logo better than any article can.
