Nobody in baseball owns a color the way the Padres own brown. It is the whole identity, the thing that makes them instantly recognizable and impossible to confuse with anyone else, and it is the reason this closet is one of the most fun in the sport to rank. Five uniforms, a real range of looks, and a color story no other team can touch.

Here is the honest throughline, though: we love this set and we still think almost every piece of it is a little underpowered. The brown, the khaki, the camo are all handsome and all a touch too soft, like they are one notch of contrast away from being great. The one everybody raves about, the City Connect, is the one we have cooled on the most. Here is every Padres jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

## 5. City Connect

This is the one that will get us in trouble, because the navy Dia de los Muertos City Connect has a lot of fans, and we understand why in a vacuum. But rank it honestly against everything the Padres own and against what a great jersey actually is, and it just is not that good. The navy is the least Padres color imaginable, brown and gold are the identity and this jersey hides both, and stripped of the theme it is a fairly ordinary dark uniform that leans on its accents to carry it.

There is a real distinction worth drawing here. When we [ranked every City Connect in baseball](/stories/mlb-city-connect-2026-official-ranking) against one another, this one placed near the top, because the City Connect program is a low bar and this clears it cleanly. But grading it as a jersey in its own right, next to a century of real baseball uniforms, is a different test, and it does not pass that one nearly as well. It is a good City Connect and a mediocre jersey, and in a Padres closet built on one of the best color stories in the sport, a navy set that uses none of it lands last. C+.

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2076709517119889892"></a></blockquote>

The home white ties the City Connect at C+ and edges it only because it is at least honest about what it is. Brown and gold trim on a white base with the SD on the cap, clean and perfectly fine, but for a team with a color this distinctive, the white is the version where the least of that color gets to show. When brown is your superpower, a mostly-white uniform is the one that turns the volume all the way down.

It is not a bad jersey, it is just the Padres look with the least Padres in it. Every team needs a home white, and this is a competent one. It is also the last thing we would reach for out of this closet on a day when it mattered.

## 3. Khaki Road

  <div style="font-size: 3em; font-weight: 800; line-height: 1.2; color: #ffffff;">B-</div>
</div>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2079295524352741531"></a></blockquote>

The khaki road jersey is the Padres doing something almost nobody else in baseball does, which is wearing sand as a base color. It is a genuine nod to San Diego, the beach and the desert light, and it is a smarter road option than a plain gray would ever be. Brown script on khaki is warm and specific in a way road grays never are.

It gets a B- because the execution is a touch soft. The contrast between the sand base and the brown lettering is lower than it should be, so from distance the whole thing can go a little flat and washed out under bright sun. The idea is an A. The pop is what holds it back, and that is a recurring note with this uniform set.

## 2. Brown Alternate

  <div style="font-size: 3em; font-weight: 800; line-height: 1.2; color: #FFC425;">B</div>
</div>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2080292336941289966"></a></blockquote>

This is the one everybody thinks of, the brown-and-gold that is the entire reason the Padres look like the Padres. We love it. It is the most identifiable jersey the franchise has, no other team in baseball owns brown, and every time it is on the field it is doing something nobody else can do. On history and identity alone it is one of the most important uniforms in the sport, and it is the jersey that should define this team.

So why only a B, and why behind the camo? Because the jersey itself, as drawn, does not quite live up to the color it is built on. The brown-on-brown with gold trim is a little flat, the pop we keep wanting from this whole set is missing here too, and it leans more handsome than knockout. We are grading the uniform, not the idea, and the idea is an A+ that the execution only partly cashes in. Iconic, essential, and still a notch short of its own ceiling.

## 1. Camouflage Alternate

The best thing the Padres wear, and it is not particularly close. The camouflage set is a San Diego institution, a tribute to the city's deep military community that the team has worn in some form for decades, and no other franchise has tied a uniform to its city's identity this directly for this long. In a Navy town it is not a marketing gimmick, it is a genuine gesture, and that sincerity is exactly why it works where a stunt would not.

The only thing keeping it from an A is the note that runs through this whole closet: we wish it had a touch more pop. The camo pattern is handsome but muted, and a hair more contrast in the lettering or the trim would take it over the top. Even so, it clears everything else the Padres own. It means something, it looks like nobody else, and it is the one jersey in this set we would put on without a second thought. A-, and the top of the closet.

## The Bottom Line on the Padres Jersey Rankings

The Padres have the most distinctive color story in baseball and a closet that never quite turns it all the way up. The one honest critique that runs through almost everything here is contrast: the brown, the khaki, and the camo are all handsome, all specific, and all a little softer than they could be. A touch more pop and this is one of the two or three best uniform sets in the sport.

The camo wins because it means the most and still looks the best, the brown-and-gold does the identity work no other team can, and the celebrated City Connect is, honestly, the most overrated thing they wear. San Diego remains the most instantly recognizable club in the National League. Nobody else even gets to try brown, and we just wish they would lean on it even harder.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many jerseys do the Padres have in 2026?**

Five in active rotation: the home white, the khaki road, the brown alternate, the camouflage military tribute alternate, and the navy Dia de los Muertos City Connect.

**What is the best Padres jersey?**

We rank the camouflage alternate first and grade it an A-. It is a decades-long tribute to San Diego's military community and the one Padres look that both means something and outshines the rest of the closet.

**Why do the Padres wear brown?**

Brown has been the Padres' color since their expansion debut in 1969, and no other team in baseball owns it. After decades in navy and other colors, San Diego brought brown and gold back full-time in 2020. Our full explainer covers [why the Padres wear brown](/stories/why-padres-wear-brown).

**Is the Padres City Connect any good?**

We grade it a C+. It ranks well against other City Connects, but as a standalone jersey it is fairly ordinary: the navy uses none of the brown and gold that make the Padres distinctive, and it leans on its accents to carry it.
