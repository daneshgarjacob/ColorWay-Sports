Nobody in baseball owns a color the way the Padres own brown. It is the whole identity, the thing that makes them instantly recognizable and impossible to confuse with anyone else, and it is the reason this closet is one of the most fun in the sport to rank. Five uniforms, a real range of looks, and a color story no other team can touch.

The one surprise, and we say this as fans of the brown, is that the brown jersey is not the best thing they wear. It is close, and it is iconic, but two other looks in this closet edge it. Here is every Padres jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2076709517119889892"></a></blockquote>

The home white is the one Padres look that leaves us wanting. It is clean and it is fine, brown and gold trim on a white base with the SD on the cap, but for a team with a color this distinctive, the white is the version where the least of that color gets to show. When brown is your superpower, a mostly-white uniform is the one that turns the volume all the way down.

It lands at C+ for exactly that reason. There is nothing wrong with it, and every team needs a home white, but this is the Padres look with the least Padres in it. We would take any of the four jerseys above it on a given day.

## 4. Khaki Road

  <div style="font-size: 3em; font-weight: 800; line-height: 1.2; color: #ffffff;">B-</div>
</div>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2079295524352741531"></a></blockquote>

The khaki road jersey is the Padres doing something almost nobody else in baseball does, which is wearing sand as a base color. It is a genuine nod to San Diego, the beach and the desert light, and it is a smarter road option than a plain gray would ever be. Brown script on khaki is warm and specific in a way road grays never are.

It gets a B- because the execution is a touch soft. The contrast between the sand base and the brown lettering is lower than it should be, so from distance the whole thing can go a little flat and washed out under bright sun. The idea is an A. The pop is what holds it back, and that is a recurring note with this uniform set.

## 3. Brown Alternate

  <span style="font-size: 0.85em; text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: 0.1em; font-weight: 600; color: #FFC425;">ColorWay Sports Grade</span>
  <div style="font-size: 3em; font-weight: 800; line-height: 1.2; color: #FFC425;">B</div>
</div>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2080292336941289966"></a></blockquote>

This is the one everybody thinks of, the brown-and-gold that is the entire reason the Padres look like the Padres. We love it. It is the most identifiable jersey the franchise has, no other team in baseball owns brown, and every time it is on the field it is doing something nobody else can do. On history and identity alone it is one of the most important uniforms in the sport.

So why only a B? Because the jersey itself, as drawn, does not quite live up to the color it is built on. The brown-on-brown with gold trim is a little flat, the pop we keep wanting from this whole set is missing here too, and it leans more handsome than knockout. We are grading the uniform, not the idea, and the idea is an A+ that the execution only partly cashes in. Iconic, essential, and still a notch short of its own ceiling.

## 2. Camouflage Alternate

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Padres/status/2076451362020401393"></a></blockquote>

The camouflage set is a Padres institution, a tribute to San Diego's deep military community that the team has worn in some form for decades, and it is one of our favorite things they do. We love these. No other franchise has tied a uniform to its city's identity this directly for this long, and it never once feels like a gimmick, because in San Diego it genuinely is not one.

The only thing keeping it from an A is the same note that runs through this whole closet: we wish it had a little more pop. The camo pattern is handsome but muted, and a hair more contrast in the lettering or the trim would take it over the top. As is, it is a heartfelt, distinctive, genuinely great alternate, and comfortably the second-best thing the Padres wear.

## 1. City Connect

The best thing the Padres own, and one of the best City Connects Nike has made for anyone. This is the navy Dia de los Muertos set, and it is the rare City Connect where we honestly cannot find anything we would change. The hat looks incredible, the dark navy with the orange and cream accents reads premium instead of costumey, and the pants being a different color than the jersey keeps it from the monochrome trap that sinks half the program. It looks like a real uniform, not a marketing exercise.

It earns the A, and the top spot, by doing the one thing the rest of this closet keeps just missing: it pops. Where the brown, the khaki, and the camo all want a little more contrast, the City Connect has it in spades, the bright accents cutting hard against the navy from any seat in the park. And we say this as Dodger fans who are contractually obligated to dislike the Padres. It is a great uniform, full stop. (We had this at an A when we [ranked every City Connect in baseball](/stories/mlb-city-connect-2026-official-ranking), and a full season later we have not moved off it.)

## The Bottom Line on the Padres Jersey Rankings

The Padres have the most distinctive color story in baseball and a closet that ranges from a genuinely great City Connect down to a home white that mostly gets out of the way. The one honest critique that runs through almost all of it is contrast: the brown, the khaki, and the camo are all handsome, all specific, and all a little softer than they could be. A touch more pop and this is one of the two or three best uniform sets in the sport.

As it stands, the City Connect and the camo carry it, the brown-and-gold does the identity work no other team can, and San Diego remains the most instantly recognizable club in the National League. Nobody else even gets to try brown.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many jerseys do the Padres have in 2026?**

Five in active rotation: the home white, the khaki road, the brown alternate, the camouflage military tribute alternate, and the navy Dia de los Muertos City Connect.

**What is the best Padres jersey?**

We rank the City Connect first and grade it an A. The navy Dia de los Muertos set is one of the best City Connects in baseball, with orange and cream accents that give it the contrast the rest of the Padres closet slightly lacks.

**Why do the Padres wear brown?**

Brown has been the Padres' color since their expansion debut in 1969, and no other team in baseball owns it. After decades in navy and other colors, San Diego brought brown and gold back full-time in 2020. Our full explainer covers [why the Padres wear brown](/stories/why-padres-wear-brown).

**What is the Padres camouflage uniform?**

It is a long-running tribute to San Diego's large military community, one the Padres have worn in some form for decades. We grade the current version an A-.
