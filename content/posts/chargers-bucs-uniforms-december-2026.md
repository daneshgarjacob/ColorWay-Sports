Two teams announced their uniforms for the same December game on the same afternoon, and between them they produced one of the best-looking matchups of the 2026 NFL season. It is only July. The game is not for four and a half months. Both fan bases already know exactly what they are watching.

The Chargers are going to Tampa on December 6 in Charger Power gold. The Buccaneers are meeting them in the creamsicles. Gold against orange-on-white, in Florida, in December.

And buried in the Chargers' announcement is the detail that actually matters, the one thing that separates this from last year: **white pants.**

Look at the bottom right corner of the Chargers' graphic. There is a starburst badge, the kind you put on a cereal box, and it reads **"NOW WITH WHITE PANTS!"** The team is treating a pants change as headline news, and they are completely right to.

Charger Power arrived in 2025 as the first yellow jersey in franchise history, and it debuted the way far too many alternates debut now: gold jersey, gold pants, one continuous column of the same color from collar to shoe. Monochrome. And monochrome is the single most common mistake in modern football uniform design.

Here is the actual problem with it, beyond taste. A football uniform is built to be read at speed from two hundred feet away, and the thing that makes a player legible is the break at the waist. That horizontal line is what gives the body proportion on a wide camera, what separates the numbers from the legs, what lets you track eleven people moving at once. Paint the whole thing one color and you delete that line. You do not get a bold uniform. You get a silhouette, and a slightly shapeless one at that.

There is also the plain fact that gold is a lot of gold. Twenty-two ounces of it head to toe reads less like a football uniform and more like something you sleep in. The pants were the bigger issue than the jersey, though. Charger Power is a fine jersey, a real nod to the powder-blue-era palette, and the white helmet on top of it is genuinely good. It just needed something underneath it to push against.

White pants do exactly that. They give the gold a floor to sit on, they pull the white helmet down into the rest of the uniform so the lid stops floating, and they hand the whole look the proportion it was missing. Same jersey, one change, and it stops being a costume.

We want to be straight about where that lands it, though, because a fix is not the same as a great uniform. This is still a lot of gold, and gold is a hard color to build a football uniform around. It still does not have the effortlessness of the powder blue set, which is the standard this franchise set for itself and the bar everything else in the closet gets measured against. The white pants take Charger Power from a jersey we did not enjoy watching to one we are fine with, and that is real progress on a set that had nowhere to go but up. It is not the same as loving it.

### Now Do the Navy

The Chargers introduced two alternates in 2025, and the other one has the identical problem. "Super Chargers" is a navy jersey that debuted with navy pants, the same single-column construction, the same missing waistline.

If the gold gets white pants, the navy should too, and for the same reasons. Navy over white would give that set a hard, clean break, it would tie back to the white helmet the way the gold version now does, and it would leave the Chargers with something almost nobody in the league has: three separate alternate looks that each solve their own proportion problem instead of three variations on one flat idea.

The powder blue over white is already the best uniform in the NFL and it earns that partly by getting this exact thing right. The template exists inside the building. Use it twice more.

We would still take four uniforms from this team over three from almost anyone else, which is not something we would say about many franchises. The usual rule is that a closet should stop at three, because scarcity is what makes a look land. The Chargers get the exemption because the palette is genuinely theirs and because the ceiling is so high. Nobody else in football owns powder blue and gold. That is also exactly why the gold set landing at a B- stings a little: this is the one franchise that has no excuse for a merely fine uniform.

Tampa Bay is calling it "The '76 Jersey," and the badge on their graphic says **"BACK BY POPULAR DEMAND."** That is not marketing language for once. It is just what happened.

The Buccaneers wore this from their first season in 1976 until 1996, then buried it in 1997 for the pewter and red set they still wear. For most of the next two decades the NFL's one-helmet rule made a proper return impossible, because the creamsicle does not work without the white shell and Bucco Bruce winking on the side of it. When the league dropped that rule, this was the first uniform everyone asked for. It came back in 2023 and it has been the best day on Tampa's calendar ever since.

Everything about it is right. The base is white, so the orange gets to be the accent rather than the whole garment, which is the exact lesson the Chargers just learned about gold. The numbers are orange outlined in red, two warm colors sitting a shade apart, and that thin red keeps the orange from dissolving into the white. The helmet stripe runs red-orange-red. The facemask is orange. And the logo is a cartoon pirate with a dagger in his teeth, which should be ridiculous and instead is the most charming mark in professional football.

It is also, quietly, a masterclass in the thing this whole post is about. There is no version of the creamsicle that reads as one flat color. White jersey, white pants, but the orange and red do so much work at the collar, the shoulders, the numbers, and the helmet that the uniform has structure everywhere you look. All-white is the one monochrome that works, because white is a background rather than a statement, and Tampa fills it in properly.

Wearing it once or twice a season is exactly correct. This is a jersey that would lose something if it showed up every other week.

## Why This Matchup Actually Works

<img src="/images/posts/chargers-bucs-uniforms-december-2026/chargers-white-pants-announcement.jpg" alt="Los Angeles Chargers announcement graphic for the December 6 game at Tampa Bay, with a NOW WITH WHITE PANTS badge on the Charger Power gold uniform" style="width: 100%; max-width: 480px; display: block; margin: 0 auto; border-radius: 10px;" />
<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin-top: 0.5em;">Image via the Los Angeles Chargers</p>

Uniform matchups fail when both teams show up in the same value. Two dark jerseys and the broadcast turns to mud. Two whites and nothing separates anybody.

This one is built the right way without either team planning it. Tampa is in a white-based uniform with warm orange accents. Los Angeles is in a saturated gold with a white helmet and now white pants. The two whites do not collide because the Chargers' gold is carrying the weight up top while the Bucs' orange is carrying it in the details. You get warm against warm, which sounds like a problem and is not, because gold and creamsicle orange sit far enough apart in both value and saturation that every camera angle will hold them separate.

The kicker is that both of these are throwback-flavored looks pulled from the same stretch of football history, and neither one is a costume. The Chargers' gold and the Bucs' creamsicle both come from an era when teams picked a color and committed. Two teams doing that in the same stadium, in December, is going to look like a football card.

Best-looking game of the season, announced in July. Set a reminder.

## The Bottom Line on the December 6 Uniform Matchup

The Buccaneers' creamsicle is one of the five best uniforms in the NFL and it did not need to change a thing. That is the easy half of this.

The Chargers' half is more interesting, because it is a story about a uniform getting meaningfully better without getting good. The monochrome gold was the worst thing this franchise puts on the field. White pants fix the specific structural problem, and Charger Power goes from a set we would rather not see to one that is perfectly fine on a December Sunday. Fine is not the same as great, and next to the powder blue over white it is not close.

Now they should do the navy. A team with the best palette in football should not be leaving two of its three alternates half-finished.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What uniforms are the Chargers wearing against the Buccaneers on December 6?**

The Charger Power gold jersey, paired with **white pants** for the first time. The Chargers announced it themselves with a "NOW WITH WHITE PANTS" badge on their graphic. Previously the gold set was worn monochrome, gold jersey over gold pants.

**What are the Buccaneers wearing on December 6?**

The creamsicle throwback, which Tampa Bay is calling "The '76 Jersey." White jersey with orange numbers outlined in red, white pants, and the white helmet with the Bucco Bruce logo.

**What time is Chargers at Buccaneers on December 6?**

1:00 PM ET, which is 10:00 AM PT for anybody watching in Los Angeles.

**What is the Chargers' Charger Power uniform?**

A gold alternate the Chargers introduced in 2025, the first yellow jersey in franchise history, worn with a white helmet. It debuted with gold pants and gets white pants for the first time on December 6.

**Why did the Buccaneers stop wearing the creamsicle uniforms?**

Tampa Bay retired the look in 1997 when the franchise rebranded to pewter and red. The NFL's one-helmet rule then blocked a proper return for years, because the creamsicle needs the white shell and the Bucco Bruce logo. Once the league lifted that rule the uniform came back in 2023, and it has been a once-or-twice-a-season throwback since.

**What color pants are the Chargers wearing with the gold jersey?**

White. The December 6 game at Tampa Bay is the first time the Charger Power gold jersey is paired with white pants instead of gold ones.

**What grade do the Chargers Charger Power uniforms get?**

We grade the gold jersey with white pants a **B-**, up from a **C-** for the all-gold monochrome version. The white pants fix the proportion problem, but the set still sits well behind the powder blue over white, which we consider the best uniform in the NFL.

**Are the Buccaneers creamsicle jerseys coming back in 2026?**

Yes. Tampa Bay is wearing the creamsicle throwback, which they call "The '76 Jersey," at home against the Los Angeles Chargers on December 6.

**Are monochrome uniforms bad?**

In football, usually yes. The break at the waist is what gives a player proportion and legibility from a distance, and a single color from collar to shoe erases it. All-white is the exception, because white reads as a background rather than a statement.
