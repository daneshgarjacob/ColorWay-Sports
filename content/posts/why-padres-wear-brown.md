There is exactly one team in Major League Baseball that wears brown, and it is not close. The San Diego Padres own the color the way the Yankees own pinstripes, and the story of how they got it, lost it, and finally took it back is one of the best identity arcs in the sport.

## It Started on Day One in 1969

The Padres wore brown in the very first game in franchise history. When they took the field for their expansion debut in 1969, they did it in beige uniforms with brown caps, and brown has been part of the DNA ever since.

Why brown? The most-told version credits original owner C. Arnholt Smith, a man who reportedly liked the color so much that he painted his bank buildings, his shipyards, and his tuna cannery brown, wore brown suits, and even used brown-tinted letterhead. When the boss loves a color that much, the expansion team is going to wear it. Founding club president Buzzie Bavasi is also often credited with settling on brown as a base that matched the warm, earthy tone of San Diego itself.

## The Golden Era, and Then the Wilderness

Brown and gold carried the Padres through the best stretch of their early history, all the way to the 1984 National League pennant, and it is the era every longtime fan pictures first. Tony Gwynn came up in brown. It was weird, it was warm, and it was theirs, because nobody else wanted it.

Then they talked themselves out of it. Through the 1990s and 2000s the Padres chased navy blue, then navy and orange, then sand and various combinations, drifting through look after look in search of something more conventional. They got exactly that: for two decades San Diego went from the most distinctive team in baseball to one of the most forgettable, a franchise that had traded its one true advantage for the safe colors half the league already wore.

## The Comeback in 2020

Fans never stopped asking for the brown, because it was the one thing that had ever made the Padres unmistakable. The team dipped a toe back in with brown Friday home jerseys, saw how much the crowd loved it, and then went all the way: starting in 2020, San Diego made brown and gold the primary identity again, home and road.

It worked instantly. The Padres went right back to being the most visually recognizable team in the National League, and a generation of fans who had only known the navy era got to see what everyone had been talking about. Brown was never a mistake the Padres needed to correct. It was the correction.

## The Bottom Line

Brown is not a gimmick or a throwback the Padres wheel out for nostalgia nights. It is the franchise's actual identity, the one color no other team in baseball is willing to wear, and San Diego spent twenty lost years proving that walking away from it was the real error. The Padres are the brown team. After a long detour, they finally remember it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do the Padres wear brown?**

Brown has been part of the Padres since their 1969 expansion debut, chosen under original owner C. Arnholt Smith, who loved the color, and club president Buzzie Bavasi. No other MLB team wears brown, which makes it San Diego's most distinctive trait.

**When did the Padres bring back brown?**

Full-time in 2020. After spending the 1990s and 2000s in navy and other colors, the Padres reintroduced brown on Friday home jerseys and then made brown and gold the primary identity again for all home and road games starting in 2020.

**Are the Padres the only team that wears brown?**

Yes. The Padres are the only team in Major League Baseball with brown as a primary color, which is a big part of why the look is so recognizable.

**Did Tony Gwynn wear brown?**

Yes. Tony Gwynn came up with the Padres in the brown-and-gold era of the 1980s, the same look that carried the franchise to the 1984 National League pennant.
