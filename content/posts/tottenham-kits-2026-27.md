Tottenham's wardrobe is complete, and the last shirt in is the best one. The home and away launched through July, the purple third landed on August 11, and for once the most adventurous thing Spurs are wearing is also the thing we would pick first.

## The Home Shirt: B-

Lilywhite with a textured diagonal tonal stripe patterned directly into the fabric, and navy blue on the collar and cuffs. The diagonal treatment references the iconic mid-1980s Hummel shirts.

The reference is a good one. Those Hummel kits are among the best Tottenham have ever worn, and a modern club nodding to them is the right instinct. The problem is that the nod is so quiet you need to be told it is there, and once you strip it out you are left with a white shirt with navy trim, which is what Tottenham wear every single season.

ESPN ranked it 34th of every Premier League kit and described it as plain by design, and called the diagonal hash marks a reference to far superior Hummel kits. That is harsh but it is not wrong. This is not a bad shirt. It is an interchangeable one, and for a club with a genuinely distinctive archive to draw on, that is the disappointment.

**Grade: B-**

## The Away Shirt: B

Worn before it was sold. Tottenham posted its first outing on July 28, so it had already had a game in it before it reached general sale, which it now has across the full Elite, Stadium and long-sleeve range. It comes with custom shorts rather than reusing another set.

It is the better of the two Tottenham shirts, which is not a high bar this season, and the important thing is that it reads as a decision rather than a repeat. That alone puts it above the home kit.

**Grade: B**

## The Third Shirt: B+

Psychic Purple, with darker purple pinstripes edged in white running down the front, a navy fold-over polo collar with white piping, a tonal geometric print underneath it all, and a white Swoosh, crest and AIA mark. The pinstripes fade out around the sponsor so the logo sits clean. Purple shorts and black socks finish it. Released August 11 under the line "Built on Heritage, Driven by Belief," and Nike built it off the mid-1990s purple away shirts to mark thirty years with the club.

This is the best thing Tottenham have worn in years. Purple has real history here, the 1990s away kits are the ones supporters still ask for, and this shade is bold without tipping into garish. The pinstripes give a plain-coloured shirt some structure, the polo collar finishes it properly, and the white piping on the collar is a small detail that makes the whole neckline work.

A change shirt is supposed to change something. The home and away this season are careful to the point of anonymous, and this one is not. Supporters have already named it the Spurple, which tells you it has landed.

**Grade: B+**
