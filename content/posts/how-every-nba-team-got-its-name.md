NBA names are younger than baseball's and stranger in a different way. Half of them came out of fan contests, a few were literally pulled out of a hat, and a surprising number stopped making sense the moment the team moved. The Lakers left the Land of 10,000 Lakes for Los Angeles. The Jazz left New Orleans for Salt Lake City. The Grizzlies left British Columbia for Memphis. They all kept the names anyway.

So we went back to the start for all 30, using the teams' own histories, newspaper archives and state and city historical sources. Where the famous story is shaky, we say so.

This is part three of our team-name history series, after [How Every NFL Team Got Its Name](/stories/how-every-nfl-team-got-its-name) and [How Every MLB Team Got Its Name](/stories/how-every-mlb-team-got-its-name). The NHL is next.


The follow-up is out: [How Every NBA Team Got Its Colors](/stories/how-every-nba-team-got-its-colors), the story behind all 30 color schemes.

## Quick Facts: NBA Team Names

- **Oldest names still in use:** the Celtics, Knicks and Warriors, all from 1946. The Celtics and Knicks have never moved.
- **Newest names:** the Pelicans (2013) and the Hornets, back in Charlotte since 2014.
- **Pulled out of a hat:** the Knicks (staffers' names in a hat) and the Suns (one of more than 300 "Suns" entries drawn at random).
- **Named in fan contests:** Bucks, Suns, 76ers, Cavaliers, Trail Blazers, Clippers, Mavericks, Magic, Heat, Timberwolves, Hornets, Raptors, Nuggets, Rockets and Jazz.
- **Moved and kept a name that no longer fits:** Lakers, Jazz, Grizzlies and Clippers.
- **Forced to change by another team:** the Nuggets (Houston had the Rockets), the Kings (Kansas City baseball had the Royals) and the Thunder (Seattle kept the SuperSonics name).
- **Forced to change by the police:** the Grizzlies, after the Royal Canadian Mounted Police blocked "Mounties."
- **Changed for a social reason:** the Wizards, who dropped "Bullets" over gun violence.
- **Named after an insult:** the Hornets, from Lord Cornwallis's hornet's nest.
- **Named after a factory:** the Pistons, a company team from a Fort Wayne piston foundry.

## Atlantic Division

### Boston Celtics

The Celtics name came from New York. In the 1920s the Original Celtics, a barnstorming team from New York, were the most famous club in basketball. When Boston Garden-Arena Corporation president Walter Brown started a team in the new Basketball Association of America in 1946, he and publicist Howie McHugh kicked around Whirlwinds, Unicorns and Olympics. Brown picked Celtics. As the story goes, he said the name had "a great basketball tradition" and that "Boston is full of Irishmen," so the team would wear green. The Celtics have never changed their name or their city. [Boston Celtics uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/celtics-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

### Brooklyn Nets

The Nets started as the New Jersey Americans, an original ABA team in 1967-68 that played at the Teaneck Armory. Their first season ended in a forfeit: a one-game playoff was moved to Long Island's Commack Arena, and the league ruled the wet, patched floor unplayable. Owner Arthur Brown moved the team to Long Island for 1968-69 and renamed it the New York Nets, a name chosen to rhyme with the Mets and the Jets. The name followed the team back to New Jersey in 1977 and to Brooklyn in 2012. [Brooklyn Nets uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/nets-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

### New York Knicks

The name was pulled out of a hat. Madison Square Garden executive Fred Podesta later explained that founder Ned Irish, publicity man Lester Scott and a few staffers each dropped a name in, and "most of them said Knickerbockers, after Father Knickerbocker, the symbol of New York City." That symbol came from a hoax. In 1809 Washington Irving planted newspaper notices about a missing Dutch historian named Diedrich Knickerbocker to promote his satirical history of New York, and the name came to mean any old-stock New Yorker. The New York Times reported the team's name on July 24, 1946, and "Knicks" was in print within weeks. [New York Knicks uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/knicks-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

### Philadelphia 76ers

Only nine people suggested it. When Ike Richman and Irv Kosloff bought the Syracuse Nationals and moved them to Philadelphia in 1963, they ran a name contest that drew fewer than 4,000 entries. "Colonials" was the most common. The winner, announced August 6, 1963, was a West Collingswood, New Jersey, fan whose entry said no team had "paid tribute to the gallant men who forged this country's independence." The name honors 1776 and the Declaration of Independence, signed a few miles from where the team plays. A local columnist joked: if they play .500 ball, will they be the 38ers? ### Toronto Raptors

Toronto's expansion team was named at the height of dinosaur mania, less than a year after Jurassic Park came out. A fan contest produced finalists including the Bobcats and the Dragons, and rejected ideas included the "Canadian Eh's" and the "Toronto Mighty Dunks." The team unveiled the name and its dribbling-dinosaur logo in May 1994. "Raptors" is short for velociraptor, the movie's breakout villain, and the merchandise was expected to help pay off a $125 million expansion fee. ## Central Division

### Chicago Bulls

Founding owner Dick Klein first leaned toward the Matadors or the Toreadors. He wanted a one-syllable name to match Chicago's Bears, Cubs, Sox and Hawks. In Klein's telling, while he brainstormed with his family, his young son Mark said, "Dad, that's a bunch of bull!" Klein took it. Bulls also tied to Chicago's meatpacking history, since the team's first home, the Chicago Amphitheatre, sat next to the Union Stock Yards. The Bulls joined the NBA in 1966. ### Cleveland Cavaliers

One of the finalists was named after the owner's son. Owner Nick Mileti picked five finalists for his 1970 expansion team and explained each: "Presidents, because Ohio is the state of presidents; Towers, like the Terminal Tower; Foresters, since Cleveland is known as the Forest City; Jays, for my son Jimmy; and of course, the Cavaliers." Fans voted in a newspaper contest, and Cavaliers took more than a third of about 6,000 votes. The winning entry came from Jerry Tomko of Eastlake, who wrote that cavaliers were "daring, fearless men, whose life's pact was never surrender, no matter what the odds." ### Detroit Pistons

The Pistons were a factory team. Fred Zollner ran a foundry in Fort Wayne, Indiana, that made pistons for car, truck and locomotive engines, and his club played as the Fort Wayne Zollner Pistons in the National Basketball League. "Zollner" was dropped in 1948 when the team moved to the Basketball Association of America. In 1957 Zollner moved the club to Detroit, which had gone about a decade without pro basketball. In the Motor City, the name fit even better. [Detroit Pistons uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/pistons-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

### Indiana Pacers

A group of Indiana investors pooled their money to buy into the new ABA and announced the name on June 16, 1967. Co-founder Dick Tinkham said it reflected two Indiana traditions: harness racing pacers, horses bred to race at a pacing gait, and the pace car that leads the field at the Indianapolis 500. It also signaled that the team meant to set the pace. The Pacers kept the name when they joined the NBA in 1976. [Indiana Pacers uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/pacers-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

### Milwaukee Bucks

The fan who explained "Bucks" best won a car. Milwaukee's 1968 expansion team got more than 14,000 contest entries, and at least 45 people suggested "Bucks," so the judges picked a winner by reasoning. R.D. Trebilcox of Whitefish Bay won a new car for writing that bucks are "spirited, good jumpers, fast and agile." Rejected names included Skunks, Badgers, Beavers, Stags and Robins. Longtime broadcaster Eddie Doucette remembered seeing Skunks on the list and saying, "this stinks." The white-tailed deer is Wisconsin's state wildlife animal. ## Southeast Division

### Atlanta Hawks

The Hawks were born in Buffalo in 1946 and moved within weeks to Moline, Illinois, part of the "Tri-Cities" with Rock Island and Davenport, Iowa. There they became the Tri-Cities Blackhawks, after Black Hawk, the Sauk leader born near today's Rock Island, whose name was already on local hotels, clubs and teams. Owner Ben Kerner moved the club to Milwaukee in 1951 and shortened the name to Hawks. It kept the name in St. Louis in 1955 and Atlanta in 1968. ### Charlotte Hornets

The Hornets are named after a British general's insult. In 1780 Lord Cornwallis spent a frustrating stretch in Charlotte, harassed by outnumbered local Patriots, and reportedly called the town a hornet's nest. Charlotte adopted the insult, and a hornet's nest went on the city seal. When George Shinn won an NBA team in 1987, he planned to call it the Charlotte Spirit, but a name-the-team contest picked Hornets. The franchise moved to New Orleans in 2002 and took the name along. Charlotte got it back in 2014, along with the 1988-2002 history. [Charlotte Hornets uniform schedule 2026-27](/stories/hornets-uniform-schedule-2026-27)

### Memphis Grizzlies

The Grizzlies were supposed to be the Vancouver Mounties. The Royal Canadian Mounted Police objected to a basketball team using its famous nickname, so on August 11, 1994, the team became the Grizzlies instead, after the big bear native to British Columbia. The team moved to Memphis in 2001, nowhere near a wild grizzly. Management asked fans about a new name, and they "overwhelmingly told us how much they loved" the old one, said business president Andy Dolich. ### New Orleans Pelicans

New Orleans played as the Hornets after the Charlotte franchise moved there in 2002. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why are the Lakers called the Lakers?**

The team started in Minneapolis in 1947 and was named for Minnesota's nickname, the "Land of 10,000 Lakes." When the Lakers moved to Los Angeles in 1960, they kept the name, even though LA has very few lakes.

**Why is Utah called the Jazz?**

The franchise began in New Orleans in 1974, the birthplace of jazz. When the team moved to Salt Lake City in 1979, owner Sam Battistone kept the name and the Mardi Gras purple, green and gold.

**How did the Raptors get their name?**

Toronto held a fan contest in 1994, the year after Jurassic Park came out. Raptors beat finalists including the Bobcats and Dragons, and the team unveiled the name and its dinosaur logo in May 1994.

**Why are the Knicks called the Knicks?**

It's short for Knickerbockers, a nickname for New Yorkers that comes from Diedrich Knickerbocker, the made-up Dutch historian Washington Irving used for his 1809 book about the city. Garden staffers put names in a hat in 1946, and most of them said Knickerbockers.

**Why did the Bullets change their name to the Wizards?**

Owner Abe Pollin wanted to stop tying his team to gun violence and made the decision final after his friend, Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin, was assassinated in 1995. Fans picked Wizards in a phone vote, and the new name debuted in 1997-98.

**Why are the Pistons called the Pistons?**

Founder Fred Zollner owned a foundry in Fort Wayne, Indiana, that made engine pistons, and the team started as his company's club, the Fort Wayne Zollner Pistons. It moved to Detroit, the Motor City, in 1957 and kept the name.

**Why are the Memphis Grizzlies called the Grizzlies?**

The team started in Vancouver, where grizzly bears are native. It was supposed to be the Vancouver Mounties until the Royal Canadian Mounted Police objected. When the team moved to Memphis in 2001, fans asked to keep the name.

**Why are the 76ers called the 76ers?**

The name honors 1776, the year the Declaration of Independence was signed in Philadelphia. A fan's entry won a 1963 contest after the team moved from Syracuse, where it was the Nationals.

**Why are the Golden State Warriors not named after a city?**

In 1971 owner Franklin Mieuli moved the team to Oakland and planned to play some home games in San Diego, so a San Francisco name no longer fit. He chose "Golden State," California's official nickname.

## The Bottom Line

The NBA's names are a map of where the teams used to be. Lakes in Los Angeles, jazz in Utah, grizzlies in Memphis and pistons in a city that builds the engines. A lot of the league's look started with those names, from the Bucks' antlers to the Hornets' teal, and the season tips off Tuesday with every one of them on the floor.
