Baseball names are older and stranger than the other leagues'. Some of them were never picked at all. Newspaper writers named the Yankees, the Cubs, the Giants, the Tigers and the Dodgers, and the clubs just went along with it. The Pirates kept an insult. The Red Sox and White Sox took names their crosstown rivals had thrown away. And the Cardinals, Reds, Red Sox and White Sox are all named after the color of their socks or their trim, which makes baseball the one league where the uniform came first and the name came second.

So we went back to the start for all 30 MLB teams, using each club's own history, the Society for American Baseball Research's newspaper research and the Hall of Fame. Where the famous story and the record disagree, we say so.

This is part two of our team-name history series, after [How Every NFL Team Got Its Name](/stories/how-every-nfl-team-got-its-name). The NBA and NHL are next.


The follow-up is out: [How Every MLB Team Got Its Colors](/stories/how-every-mlb-team-got-its-colors), the story behind all 30 color schemes.

## Quick Facts: MLB Team Names

- **Oldest continuous nickname:** the Giants, since 1885. The Phillies are the oldest one-name, one-city franchise, since 1883.
- **Newest name:** the Guardians, official in late 2021 and first worn in 2022.
- **Named by newspaper writers:** Yankees, Cubs, Giants, Tigers, Dodgers, Pirates and Cardinals. Headline writers also shortened the White Sox.
- **Named in a fan contest:** Blue Jays, Mariners and Royals. The Marlins, Diamondbacks and Mets took fan suggestions, then the owner picked.
- **Named after a color or socks:** Reds, Red Sox, White Sox and Cardinals.
- **Moved cities and kept the name:** Dodgers, Giants, Braves and Athletics.
- **Moved and took a new name:** Orioles (once the Milwaukee Brewers and St. Louis Browns), Twins and Rangers (both once the Senators), Brewers (Seattle Pilots) and Nationals (Montreal Expos).
- **Named after an insult:** the Pirates. The Athletics' elephant came from one too.
- **Tried to change and gave up:** the Phillies, who pushed "Blue Jays" from 1944 to 1949, and the Reds, who were the "Redlegs" from 1953 to 1958.

## AL East

### Baltimore Orioles

The Orioles used to be the Milwaukee Brewers. The franchise started as the 1901 Milwaukee Brewers, moved to St. Louis in 1902 and played 52 seasons as the Browns, then moved to Baltimore in 1954 and took the city's favorite baseball name. Baltimore had already had Orioles clubs in the 1880s and 1890s. The bird's name is about color, not geography: early naturalist Mark Catesby recorded Marylanders calling it "the Baltimore-Bird" because the male's orange and black matched the colors of Lord Baltimore's coat of arms. That is why the Orioles wear orange and black. It has been Maryland's state bird since 1947. [Baltimore Orioles uniform schedule 2026](/stories/orioles-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Boston Red Sox

The Red Sox are named after socks another team threw away. For their first seven seasons Boston's American League club had no official nickname, and most people called them the Americans. In 1907 Boston's National League club, the team that became today's Braves, dropped its traditional red stockings. That December, owner John I. Taylor ordered new uniforms with bright red stockings and named his team the Red Sox. The name took hold in 1908. Red stockings were a Boston baseball tradition going back to the 1871 Boston Red Stockings, who are also the ancestors of today's Braves. ### New York Yankees

Newspaper headline writers named the Yankees. The team started in 1903 as the Highlanders, because it played at Hilltop Park, one of the highest points in Manhattan, and club president Joseph Gordon shared a name with Gordon's Highlanders, a famous British regiment. "Highlanders" was too long to fit in a headline. The first known "Yankees" in print was an April 7, 1904 headline in the New York Evening Journal. Both names ran side by side for years until 1913, when the club moved to the Polo Grounds and "Yankees" won out. No one ever formally announced it. ### Tampa Bay Rays

The first choice was the "Sting Rays," but a winter-league team in Hawaii already owned that name. Owner Vince Naimoli's group went with the Devil Rays when the franchise was awarded in March 1995. The name drew complaints right away, and the club held a call-in vote that month between "Devil Rays" and "Manta Rays." Devil Rays stayed. After the 2007 season, new owner Stuart Sternberg dropped the "Devil," and "Rays" now means a burst of Florida sunshine as well as the fish. The 2008 Rays went from last place to the American League pennant. They still wear the old Devil Rays look as a throwback, including in this year's ALCS. ### Toronto Blue Jays

The Blue Jays were named in a fan contest, and 154 people picked the winning name. Toronto's 1976 expansion club drew more than 30,000 entries with over 4,000 different names. A panel of 14 judges sent 10 finalists to the board, which chose "Blue Jays" in August 1976. "Toronto Blues" was a popular entry, but the University of Toronto already used it. Because the winning name came from 154 different fans, the club held a draw, and Etobicoke periodontist Dr. William Mills won season tickets and a week at spring training. Mills said brewery owner Labatt's involvement made blue the obvious color. [Toronto Blue Jays uniform schedule 2026](/stories/blue-jays-uniform-schedule-2026)

## AL Central

### Chicago White Sox

The White Sox took their name from the Cubs. "White Stockings" was the old name of Chicago's National League club. In 1900 Charles Comiskey moved his St. Paul Saints to Chicago's South Side and helped himself to it. His team was not allowed to put "Chicago" on its jerseys, but nothing said it could not borrow a nickname. White Sox historian Richard Lindberg called it "an ingenious marketing ploy." Newspaper editors shortened it to "White Sox" to fit headlines, and by 1903 that was the only version in use. [Chicago White Sox uniform schedule 2026](/stories/white-sox-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Cleveland Guardians

The Guardians are named after eight stone statues on a bridge. The Guardians of Traffic are 43-foot Art Deco figures on the pylons of Cleveland's Hope Memorial Bridge, which opened in 1932 as the Lorain-Carnegie Bridge, within sight of the ballpark. Its later name honors the family of Bob Hope, whose father was one of the stonemasons on the job. The team announced the change on July 23, 2021, after narrowing a list of nearly 1,200 names, surveying 40,000 fans and doing 140 hours of interviews. It became official that November, ending the name the team had used since 1915. Before that, Cleveland's American League clubs were the Bronchos and the Naps. ### Detroit Tigers

The first known "Tigers" in print was the Detroit Free Press of April 16, 1895, which called the minor-league club "Capt. Strouthers' Tigers" after its 23-year-old manager, Con Strouthers. The leading theory connects the name to the Detroit Light Guard, a local military unit known as the "Tigers" since the Civil War. George Stallings, who managed the team in 1896, later claimed the name came from the team's striped socks, but the Free Press headline came a year before he arrived. The name became official when Detroit joined the new American League in 1901. [Detroit Tigers uniform schedule 2026](/stories/tigers-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Kansas City Royals

The Royals were never named after kings. They are named after a livestock show. In 1968 the expansion club held a fan contest, and the winning entry, from Sanford Porte of Overland Park, Kansas, pointed to Missouri's livestock industry, Kansas City's huge cattle market and "the nationally known American Royal parade and pageant." The American Royal began in 1899 as a livestock show in Kansas City's stockyards and still runs today. Rejected entries included "Cowpokes" and "Mules." The name is often tied to the Negro Leagues' Kansas City Monarchs, but MLB.com calls that a misconception. [Kansas City Royals uniform schedule 2026](/stories/royals-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Minnesota Twins

The Twins are the original Washington Senators. When owner Calvin Griffith moved the club in 1960, he wanted to call it the "Twin Cities Twins" so he would not have to pick between rivals Minneapolis and St. Paul. He even stepped off the plane in a "TC" cap. The American League wanted a bigger place name, so Griffith settled on "Minnesota," which made the Twins the first MLB team named after a whole state instead of a city. The name was announced on November 26, 1960. The "TC" never left the cap. [Minnesota Twins uniform schedule 2026](/stories/twins-uniform-schedule-2026)

## AL West

### Houston Astros

Houston joined the National League in 1962 as the Colt .45s. The Colt firearms company wanted a share of the team's revenue, so owner Judge Roy Hofheinz went looking for a new name to go with his new stadium, the Astrodome, the world's first domed ballpark. He narrowed it to "Astros" or "Stars" and asked his friend, astronaut Alan Shepard, who picked Astros. Hofheinz announced it on December 1, 1964. Houston was already home to NASA's Manned Spacecraft Center, where Mission Control ran the Gemini flights, so the city was "Space City" before the team ever took the field. ### Los Angeles Angels

"Los Angeles" is Spanish for "the angels," so the team name is the city's name in English. The MLB club took it from the Pacific Coast League's Los Angeles Angels, who played from 1903 to 1957. Cowboy singer Gene Autry went to baseball's 1960 Winter Meetings to bid on the new team's radio rights and came home owning the team. MLB.com reports he paid Walter O'Malley, who owned the old minor-league name, $350,000 for it. The Angels have kept the nickname through four place names: Los Angeles, California, Anaheim and Los Angeles of Anaheim, before going back to plain Los Angeles in 2016. ### Athletics

The A's turned an insult into a logo. The name goes back to the Athletic Base Ball Club of Philadelphia in the 1860s, and Connie Mack's American League club took it in 1901. The next year, Giants manager John McGraw mocked the team as a "white elephant," and Mack adopted the elephant as its symbol. It is still on the sleeve today. The name has survived every move: Kansas City in 1955, Oakland in 1968 and West Sacramento in 2025. In late 2024 the club dropped "Oakland" and became simply the Athletics while it plays at Sutter Health Park ahead of a planned move to Las Vegas. ### Seattle Mariners

The man who named the Mariners never claimed his prize. Seattle's owners, a group that included actor Danny Kaye, held a name-the-team contest in 1976 that drew about 15,000 entries and more than 600 different names. Plenty of people suggested "Mariners." The club picked Roger Szmodis of Bellevue, Washington, because it liked his explanation best: "the natural association between the sea and Seattle and her people." His prize was a pair of 1977 season tickets and a road trip. He never claimed them, and according to the club's own history, it never found him. ### Atlanta Braves

The Braves are named after a New York political machine. In December 1911 the Boston club was bought by James Gaffney, a figure in Tammany Hall, the Democratic organization that ran New York politics. Tammany members were known as "braves," and SABR's research credits Gaffney's partner, John Montgomery Ward, with suggesting the name. Before that the club had been the Red Stockings, the Doves (after owners John and George Dovey) and, for one season, the Rustlers (after owner William Russell). Fans voted to rename them the Bees in 1936, and Braves came back in 1941. The team moved to Milwaukee in 1953 and Atlanta in 1966. A newspaper named the Cubs for being young. After many players jumped to the new American League, Chicago's National League club was left with a young roster, and "cub" meant young and inexperienced. On March 27, 1902, the Chicago Daily News wrote about "the new Cubs," and the paper liked how the short name fit in headlines. Earlier nicknames included the White Stockings, Colts and Orphans. Player-manager Frank Chance pushed to make "Cubs" official, and by 1908 a bear cub was on the uniform. [Chicago Cubs uniform schedule 2026](/stories/cubs-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Pittsburgh Pirates

The Pirates are named after an insult. In 1890 the Pittsburgh club, then called the Alleghenys, went 23-113 after its stars left for the short-lived Players' League. When that league folded, second baseman Lou Bierbauer did not re-sign with the Philadelphia Athletics, and Pittsburgh signed him for 1891. Philadelphia's side called the move "piratical," rival papers started calling Pittsburgh the Pirates, and the club kept the insult. The name stuck in the 1890s, was on the uniforms by 1912, and has never changed since. [Pittsburgh Pirates uniform schedule 2026](/stories/pirates-uniform-schedule-2026)

### St. Louis Cardinals

The Cardinals are named after a color, not the bird. In 1899 new owners Frank and Stanley Robison swapped the old brown uniforms for ones trimmed in cardinal red. The story goes that a St. Louis Republic columnist, Willie McHale, overheard a woman fan call them "a lovely shade of cardinal," and started calling the team the Cardinals. By 1900 the name was official. The birds did not show up until 1921, when the famous birds-on-the-bat logo arrived. Before that, the franchise was the Browns, for its brown stockings, and briefly the Perfectos in 1899. [St. Louis Cardinals uniform schedule 2026](/stories/cardinals-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Arizona Diamondbacks

The staff voted for Scorpions. Owner Jerry Colangelo wanted "Arizona" in the name so the team would represent the whole state, and fan suggestions were cut to five finalists: Coyotes, Diamondbacks, Phoenix, Rattlers and Scorpions. A design firm mocked up all five, and a secret staff ballot went "like 12-1" for Scorpions because the logo looked so sharp, team president Rich Dozer recalled. Colangelo had the final vote. He said scorpions gave him "the willies," and he picked Diamondbacks, a desert rattlesnake whose name also plays on the baseball diamond. ### Colorado Rockies

Denver fans wanted the Denver Bears, after the city's longtime minor-league team, and polls in both daily papers showed it. Denver's NHL team had been the Colorado Rockies from 1976 to 1982 before it moved away and became the New Jersey Devils. The baseball Rockies were awarded in 1991 and began play in 1993. ### Los Angeles Dodgers

The Dodgers are named after Brooklyn's trolleys. In the early 1890s Brooklyn's streetcars switched from horses to electricity and got much faster and more dangerous, and people from Brooklyn became known as "trolley dodgers." By April 1895 the Brooklyn Times reported the ballclub was "now known as the trolley dodgers." It was one of many nicknames for the team, along with the Bridegrooms, after six players married around 1888, the Superbas and the Robins, after manager Wilbert Robinson. "Dodgers" became official in 1932, went on the uniform in 1933 and moved to Los Angeles in 1958. [Los Angeles Dodgers uniform schedule 2026](/stories/dodgers-uniform-schedule-2026)

### San Diego Padres

The name is older than the MLB team. In 1936 the Pacific Coast League's Hollywood Stars moved to San Diego, and a local committee chose "Padres" to honor the first California mission, which Father Junípero Serra founded in San Diego in 1769. "Padre" is Spanish for father or priest. A teenage Ted Williams signed with those minor-league Padres in 1936 for $150 a month. The expansion club kept the name when it joined the National League in 1969, and the Swinging Friar mascot carries on the same theme. ### San Francisco Giants

The famous origin story is probably a myth. Legend says New York manager Jim Mutrie named the team after a comeback win in 1885, shouting "My big fellows, my giants!" SABR's research points to a newspaper instead: an unsigned April 14, 1885 game story in the New York World called the team the Giants, and within weeks everyone did. Researchers have found no account from the time of Mutrie coining it, and he made the claim more than 30 years later. Before that, the club was usually called the Gothams. "Giants" is the longest continuous nickname in the majors, and it moved to San Francisco in 1958. [San Francisco Giants uniform schedule 2026](/stories/giants-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why are the Dodgers called the Dodgers?**

It is short for "trolley dodgers," a nickname for Brooklyn residents who had to dodge the city's fast electric streetcars in the 1890s. Newspapers attached it to the Brooklyn ballclub by 1895. The team made "Dodgers" official in 1932 and brought it to Los Angeles in 1958.

**Why are the Yankees called the Yankees?**

Newspaper headline writers created it. The team started in 1903 as the Highlanders, a name too long for headlines, and the New York Evening Journal printed "Yankees" in a headline on April 7, 1904. "Yankees" fully replaced "Highlanders" by 1913.

**How did the Cleveland Guardians get their name?**

It comes from the Guardians of Traffic, eight Art Deco figures on Cleveland's Hope Memorial Bridge near the ballpark. The team picked it from nearly 1,200 options after surveying 40,000 fans and announced it July 23, 2021.

**Why are the Pittsburgh Pirates called the Pirates?**

It started as an insult. In 1891 Pittsburgh signed second baseman Lou Bierbauer, whom the Philadelphia Athletics expected back, and Philadelphia's side called the move "piratical." The club kept the name.

**Why are the Cubs called the Cubs?**

The Chicago Daily News started calling the young team the Cubs in 1902, because its roster was full of inexperienced players after many veterans jumped to the new American League.

**Are the St. Louis Cardinals named after the bird?**

No. They are named after the color. In 1899 the team added cardinal red trim, a columnist reportedly heard a fan call it "a lovely shade of cardinal," and the name stuck by 1900. The birds-on-the-bat logo came in 1921.

**Why did the Astros change their name from the Colt .45s?**

The Colt firearms company wanted a share of the team's revenue, and owner Roy Hofheinz wanted a space-age name for the new Astrodome. He announced "Astros" on December 1, 1964.

**Why are the Kansas City Royals called the Royals?**

The name honors the American Royal, a livestock show and parade held in Kansas City since 1899, not royalty. A fan's entry in the club's 1968 naming contest made the case.

**Which MLB team names came from fan contests?**

The Blue Jays, Mariners and Royals were named through fan contests. The Marlins, Diamondbacks and Mets took fan suggestions, but the owner made the final call, and in Miami's case the fans' favorite, the Manatees, lost.

## The Bottom Line

Baseball's names mostly were not designed. They were handed down from socks, newspapers, rival leagues and political clubs, and then kept for more than a century. That is why so many MLB uniforms still look the way they do: the Red Sox and White Sox still wear the stockings in their names, the Cardinals still wear the red, and the A's still wear an elephant that started as a joke.
