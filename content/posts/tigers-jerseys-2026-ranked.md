Detroit has always been one of the most conservative uniform franchises in baseball, an identity built on a single letter, and for decades the wardrobe barely changed. Then 2026 happened: the Tigers rolled out orange for the first time in 126 years plus a matching navy road alternate, giving one of the sport's most traditional closets a real jolt of color. Here is every Detroit Tigers jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

On the road, Detroit wears the standard gray set with the script "Detroit" arched across the chest in navy, the wordmark the club has worn on its travel grays since the mid-1990s, paired with the navy cap. It is the unfussy road look that lets the home club's colors carry the visual load, and there is nothing wrong with it.

It grades a B and lands at No. 5 simply because it is the most anonymous piece in the closet. A road gray does not need to be flashy to do its job, and this one does its job cleanly, it just has no signature detail to push it past the looks above it. Solid, forgettable, exactly what a travel gray is supposed to be.

Unveiled in the same February 2026 reveal as the orange, the navy alternate is the road companion to it. It runs the script "Detroit" across a navy base but flips the palette to orange lettering with white trim, using the same 1984-inspired template, complete with the V-neck collar, cuff striping, and roundel sleeve patch. The Tigers wear it for select road games rather than on a fixed day.

It grades a B+. This is a genuinely sharp dark top, the orange-on-navy reads great and the throwback detailing gives it more character than a plain alternate, and it adds a welcome jolt of color to the road schedule. The only thing keeping it from climbing higher is that it has no calendar hook, it is the discretionary look you get when the team feels like it, which keeps it from building the kind of identity the jerseys above it have.

Detroit's "Motor City" City Connect, introduced in 2024, is back for a third season with a smaller footprint, moving from Fridays to select Monday home games now that the orange owns the Friday slot. The design is a love letter to the city's car culture: a navy base with electric-blue tire-tread detailing beneath white "Motor City" lettering, racing stripes at the cuffs and down the pants, a road-sign "313" sleeve patch, a faux VIN on the cap, and tiger eyes hidden on the beltline and under the brim.

It grades an A-. This is one of the most thematically dense City Connects MLB has approved, and unlike a lot of the program's entries, every element actually ties back to something real about Detroit. It is a little busy by nature, which is the only thing keeping it out of the top two, but pulling it back to an occasional Monday is smart, it keeps a genuinely creative uniform from wearing out its welcome.

This is the headline. For the first time in 126 years, the Tigers are wearing orange in regular-season games. The orange alternate keeps the classic Old English D on the upper-left chest, rendered in navy trimmed in white so it pops against the bright base, and pairs with white pants and a new navy cap with an orange brim. The V-neck collar and three-color sleeve striping pull straight from the 1984 champions, and the roundel sleeve patch nods to the franchise's mid-century tiger-head logos. It is reserved for Friday home games at Comerica Park.

It grades an A-, and the grade is as much about the boldness as the design. For a club this traditional to break out orange at all is an event, and they did it the right way, anchoring the loudest jersey they have ever worn to the one piece that makes them the Tigers. It is a genuine thrill to see on the field, and giving it a standing Friday slot is exactly how you turn a new look into a weekly reason to show up.

The home white is the crown, and it is not particularly close. A clean white jersey with the navy Old English D on the left chest, navy piping, and the navy cap with the white D, it is the single most recognizable identity in baseball. The Old English D has appeared on Detroit's home uniform in some form every season since 1905, and the team guards it closely, because there is nothing here to reinvent.

It grades an A as the standard every other Tigers look is measured against. The new orange is thrilling and the City Connect is clever, but this is the jersey that makes all of them mean something, the anchor the whole wardrobe orbits. Some franchises are still searching for an identity; Detroit has had a perfect one for over a century and has the good sense to leave it alone.

## The Bottom Line

Detroit's 2026 closet is the rare mix of untouchable tradition and genuine new energy. The Old English D home white is still the crown and one of the best uniforms in the sport, but the historic orange alternate and its navy road companion finally give one of baseball's most conservative franchises some color to play with, and the Motor City City Connect remains one of the most creative in the league. There is not a bad jersey here, and for a team that used to change nothing, that is a real statement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many uniforms do the Tigers have in 2026?**

Five: the home whites, the road grays, the brand-new orange home alternate, the new navy road alternate, and the Motor City City Connect.

**When do the Tigers wear the orange jersey?**

The orange alternate is reserved for Friday home games at Comerica Park. It marks the first time in 126 years the Tigers have worn orange in a regular-season game, and it keeps the classic Old English D on the chest.

**When do the Tigers wear the Motor City City Connect in 2026?**

Select Monday home games. The Motor City City Connect moved off its old Friday slot for 2026, worn more sparingly now that the orange alternate owns Fridays.

**What is the best Tigers jersey for 2026?**

We rank the home white No. 1 with an A. The navy Old English D on a clean white base is the most recognizable identity in baseball and has anchored the franchise since 1905. The new orange alternate is the most exciting piece and lands at No. 2.
