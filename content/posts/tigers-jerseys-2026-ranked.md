Detroit has always been one of the most conservative uniform franchises in baseball, an identity built on a single letter. Then 2026 happened: the Tigers rolled out orange for the first time in 126 years plus a matching navy road alternate, and those two new looks immediately jumped to the top of the closet. The City Connect, on the other hand, is the one we could do without. Here is every Detroit Tigers jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

Detroit's "Motor City" City Connect, introduced in 2024, is the one that misses for us. It is a navy base with electric-blue tire-tread detailing beneath white "Motor City" lettering, racing stripes at the cuffs and down the pants, a road-sign "313" sleeve patch, a faux VIN on the cap, and tiger eyes hidden on the beltline and under the brim. The Detroit car-culture concept is a good idea in theory, but the execution piles gimmick on gimmick.

It grades a C. There is so much going on, tire tread, racing stripes, VINs, hidden eyes, that it stops reading like a baseball uniform and starts reading like a theme, and none of it plays off the classic identity that makes the Tigers the Tigers. Tellingly, the team pulled it back to occasional Monday home games for 2026 and handed the Friday slot to the orange, which feels like a quiet admission that this one was never a keeper.

On the road, Detroit wears the standard gray set with the script "Detroit" arched across the chest in navy, the wordmark the club has worn on its travel grays since the mid-1990s, paired with the navy cap. It is understated, but it is a genuinely good-looking road gray.

It grades a B+. The arched navy script gives it more character than the average travel gray, and there is a quiet confidence to how clean it is, no gimmicks, no clutter, just a sharp classic wordmark on gray. This is how a road uniform is supposed to look, and it comfortably clears the bar the City Connect could not.

The home white is a clean white jersey with the navy Old English D on the left chest, navy piping, and the navy cap with the white D. The Old English D has appeared on Detroit's home uniform in some form every season since 1905, and it is one of the most recognizable marks in the sport.

It grades an A-. This is a rock-solid classic and there is nothing to fix, but it is the look you respect more than you get fired up about. In a closet that now has the historic orange and the excellent navy alternate bringing real color, the whites are the dependable backbone rather than the showpiece. A very good home uniform that just gets edged out by the two looks above it.

Unveiled in the same February 2026 reveal as the orange, the navy alternate is a knockout. It runs the script "Detroit" across a navy base in orange lettering with white trim, using the 1984-inspired template, complete with the V-neck collar, cuff striping, and roundel sleeve patch. The Tigers wear it for select road games.

It grades an A. The orange-on-navy is rich and confident, and the throwback detailing gives it a genuine sense of occasion instead of feeling like a bolt-on alternate. This is one of the best jerseys the Tigers have ever put on the road, the kind of dark top that makes you hope they wear it more than "select" games. A real winner.

The orange home alternate takes the crown. For the first time in 126 years, the Tigers are wearing orange in regular-season games, and they nailed it. The orange base keeps the classic Old English D on the upper-left chest, rendered in navy trimmed in white so it pops, and pairs with white pants and a new navy cap with an orange brim. The V-neck collar and three-color sleeve striping pull straight from the 1984 champions, and it is reserved for Friday home games at Comerica Park.

It grades an A, and it is the best thing in the closet. For a franchise this traditional to break out orange at all is an event, and they did it exactly right, going bold on the color while anchoring the whole thing to the one mark that makes them unmistakably Detroit. It is a thrill to see on the field, and tying it to a standing Friday slot turns it into a weekly reason to show up. New, historic, and genuinely great.

## The Bottom Line

The 2026 reveal reshaped the Tigers' closet. The historic orange alternate is now the best jersey they own, with the excellent navy road alternate right behind it, both proving that one of baseball's most conservative franchises can do color when it commits. The road gray remains a sharp classic and the Old English D home white is the dependable backbone. The lone miss is the busy Motor City City Connect, and the team seems to agree, quietly shrinking its role for 2026.

Five: the home whites, the road grays, the brand-new orange home alternate, the new navy road alternate, and the Motor City City Connect.

**When do the Tigers wear the Motor City City Connect in 2026?**
