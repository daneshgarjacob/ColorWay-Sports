The Panthers pulled the wraps off a new batch of Bank of America Stadium renderings this week, and the price tag went up with them. Carolina now says the renovation is a **$1.3 billion** project, and the imagery finally shows what that number is buying: a reworked exterior crowned by a glowing halo over Uptown Charlotte, wide open social decks, and a stadium meant to feel like a year-round destination rather than a ten-Sundays-a-year building.

## What the New Renderings Show

The headline is the exterior. The renderings reimagine the outside of the stadium with upgraded materials and a **signature illuminated crown**, a lighting element that reads clearly against the Charlotte skyline and gives the building the kind of nighttime identity the Queen City has never really had at its stadium. It is the single most striking idea in the set, and it is the one that will end up on postcards.

Inside, the theme is openness. Carolina is promising expanded indoor-outdoor gathering spaces on every level, headlined by a **500-level social patio** with sweeping views of Uptown. There are larger and more dynamic scoreboards and video displays, a full range of upgraded seating options, more premium offerings, and technology aimed at a more connected, personalized game day. The through-line is a stadium that wants you standing, moving, and hanging out, not just sitting in a bowl.

## The Money and the Timeline

The jump to $1.3 billion comes from an additional **$500 million in private funding** from Tepper Sports & Entertainment, on top of the City of Charlotte's **$650 million** public contribution. That is a real escalation from where this project started, and it reflects a bigger vision than a straight refresh.

The work is phased. Interior renovations are already underway in 2026, the fan-facing enhancements are scheduled to begin in 2027, and the whole thing is targeted for completion in **2030**. So this is a long build, and these renderings are the promise rather than the product.

## The Bigger Charlotte Play

The renderings also sit inside a broader plan the Panthers are calling the "Charlotte corridor," an effort to turn the blocks around the stadium into a year-round draw. The most notable piece is a **4,400-seat music venue**, the kind of mid-size room a city this size has lacked, designed to keep the district busy on nights the Panthers are not playing. Read together, the stadium and the corridor are less about football Sundays and more about making the area a weekend destination in every season.

## The Bottom Line

The illuminated crown is the image that will carry this reveal, and it should. It gives Bank of America Stadium a real skyline identity for the first time. But the more interesting story is the scope: a $1.3 billion budget, a 2030 finish line, and a plan that treats the stadium as the anchor of a district rather than a standalone building. Carolina is not renovating a stadium so much as rebuilding a corner of its city around one.
