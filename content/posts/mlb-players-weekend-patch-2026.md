If you turned on a Major League Baseball game this weekend and noticed a small patch on the side of every cap, on both teams, in every ballpark, you are not imagining it. That is the **Players' Weekend patch**, and all 30 clubs are wearing it from **Friday, August 21 through Sunday, August 23, 2026**.

Here is the short answer, and then the longer one: the patch is the Players' Weekend logo, it sits on the side of the cap, every player, coach and manager in the league is wearing it, and it is the only thing about the uniform that changes. No nickname jerseys, no special uniforms. Just the patch.

## What the Patch on MLB Caps Actually Is

The patch is the official **Players' Weekend logo**, applied to the side panel of each team's regular game cap. It is a league-wide mark rather than a team-specific one, so it looks the same on a Dodgers cap as it does on an Orioles cap. Teams keep their normal caps underneath it.

That is the entire on-field uniform change. Every club is wearing its usual jersey rotation this weekend, right down to City Connects and alternates. On Friday night alone, ten teams wore City Connect jerseys, and every one of those jerseys was paired with a normal team cap carrying the Players' Weekend patch. If your team looked different this weekend, that was the regular uniform schedule doing its thing, not Players' Weekend.

## Why It Is Just a Patch Now, and Not Nickname Jerseys

This is the part that trips people up, because Players' Weekend used to look completely different.

From **2017 to 2019**, Players' Weekend was one of the loudest uniform events on the sports calendar. Teams wore brightly colored, Little League style pullover jerseys with players' **nicknames on the back** instead of surnames, paired with matching caps and, in 2019, an all-black and all-white set that most fans remember for being nearly impossible to read on television. Nobody was neutral about them.

The event then went away, and when it **returned in 2024**, MLB brought it back without the alternate uniforms. Since then it has been a cap-patch event, and 2026 follows that same template. It is comfortably the tamest version of Players' Weekend there has ever been, which is either a relief or a disappointment depending on how you felt about the nicknames.

One consequence of the patch-only format: it is genuinely league-wide. The Yankees, who famously sat out the nickname jerseys, wear the patch like everyone else, because a league-wide cap patch does not ask a club to change its uniform identity. That is exactly [the kind of compromise the Yankees will accept](/stories/yankees-uniform-schedule-2026) when a full alternate is off the table.

## The Real Customization Moved to the Gear

The uniforms went back to normal, but the personalization did not disappear. It moved off the jersey and onto everything a player holds or wears on their feet.

**Custom bats.** This is the centerpiece now. More than **400 players** are swinging custom bat designs this weekend, built through Victus and Marucci, with artwork honoring hometowns and home countries, family members, alma maters, old nicknames and charitable causes. It is the largest participation the program has had. Julio Rodriguez went Spider-Man, and Jordan Walker brought two: an Iron Man bat and a Home Run Derby champion bat.

**Custom cleats.** Players can wear personalized cleats across all three days, which is where a lot of the visual creativity that used to live on the jerseys ended up.

**Branded caps and batting practice tees.** Players' Weekend branded caps and BP shirts appear before games, which is why pregame footage looks more special than the game itself does.

**Custom batting gloves.** As part of MLB's Latino cultural platform, custom batting gloves are being worn this weekend under a dedicated banner.

After the weekend, the custom bats and cleats go up on **MLB Auctions**, with proceeds going to charities the players choose themselves.

## The Three Daily Themes

MLB built the 2026 weekend around three themes, one per day, carried across in-stadium features, broadcasts and social:

- **Off-Field Interests**, spotlighting what players care about away from baseball.
- **Causes**, focused on charitable and community work.
- **Appreciation**, celebrating the people who helped players reach the majors.

The themes drive the storytelling and the bat and cleat designs. They do not change anything about the uniforms. The Off-Field Interests day is where you get things like Pete Crow-Armstrong's locker listing his three favorite albums.

## How This Compares to MLB's Other Patch and Cap Weekends

Players' Weekend is one of several league-wide uniform moments on the calendar, and they are easy to mix up when your team suddenly looks different:

- **Armed Forces Day weekend**, mid-May, is the olive green cap weekend.
- **Memorial Day** is a red poppy patch on the jersey, not a cap change.
- **Fourth of July** is the Stars and Stripes cap moment. In 2026 it also carried [the USA 250 patch that MLB, the NBA and the NFL all shared](/stories/usa-250-patch-mlb-nba-nfl-2026).
- **Hall of Fame Weekend**, late July, is when [all 30 teams wear throwback caps](/stories/mlb-throwback-caps-hall-of-fame-weekend-2026).
- **Players' Weekend**, late August, is the cap patch.

The pattern is worth knowing: when caps change league-wide, it is usually Armed Forces Day, the Fourth or Hall of Fame Weekend. When a small patch shows up on the side of the cap and nothing else moves, it is Players' Weekend.
