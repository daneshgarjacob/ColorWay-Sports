The Minnesota Timberwolves new uniforms have leaked in three colorways ahead of the team's official June 7, 2026 reveal — a white primary, a blue primary, and a black alternate. The throwback look we called in our [June 7 reveal prediction post](/stories/timberwolves-new-uniforms-june-7-2026-throwback-prediction) lands exactly as projected: the brighter blue and green color palette from the 1989 expansion identity, the wolf head crest design language, and the tree print side panel returning on the black alternate. The rebrand is a full identity reset to the look that built the Timberwolves brand in the Kevin Garnett era, and the leaked set delivers on every signal the team has been sending since the new logos surfaced earlier this year. We grade the rebrand A+.

## The White Home Jersey

<div style="margin: 2em 0; text-align: center;">
  <img src="/images/posts/timberwolves-2026-27-new-jerseys-leaked/white.jpg" alt="Minnesota Timberwolves new white home jersey 2026-27 with the throwback wolf head crest and the brighter blue and green color trim returning from the 1989 expansion identity, the primary home uniform for the Wolves 2026-27 NBA season" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</div>

The new Timberwolves white home jersey returns to the brighter blue and green trim that defined the franchise's original identity. The cleaner white base reads clean on broadcast against any road opponent and the throwback color story replaces the muted navy and forest green of the current Wolves set with the brighter, more saturated palette that built the brand. The wordmark and trim work do the heavy lifting on the white version, the absence of the tree print side panel keeping the home jersey clean and the color story focused on the brighter blue and green that fans have been asking for. The white jersey is the textbook NBA home primary done right, the cleanest broadcast canvas the Wolves have run since the original Hardwood Classic era.

## The Blue Road Jersey

<div style="margin: 2em 0; text-align: center;">
  <img src="/images/posts/timberwolves-2026-27-new-jerseys-leaked/blue.jpg" alt="Minnesota Timberwolves new blue road jersey 2026-27 with the throwback wolf head crest, WOLVES wordmark, and brighter blue base from the 1989 expansion identity returning as the primary road uniform for the Wolves 2026-27 NBA season" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</div>

The new Timberwolves blue road jersey leans into the brighter shade of the throwback blue that defined the Kevin Garnett-era WOLVES wordmark uniform, the saturated blue base anchoring the road identity with the green trim and white outline detail that made the 1995-96 Mitchell and Ness throwback one of the most recognizable jerseys of the early NBA throwback boom. The blue version is the road jersey the franchise should have been running for the past decade and the brighter saturation reads stronger on broadcast than the navy-and-grey direction of the current Wolves road look. The tree print is not on the blue jersey either, the side panel detail held in reserve for the black alternate, but the blue base on its own carries enough of the throwback identity to stand alone as the road primary.

## The Black Alternate (With the Tree Print)

<div style="margin: 2em 0; text-align: center;">
  <img src="/images/posts/timberwolves-2026-27-new-jerseys-leaked/black.jpg" alt="Minnesota Timberwolves new black alternate jersey 2026-27 with the tree print side panel returning from the original Hardwood Classic uniform, the wolf head crest and brighter blue and green trim completing the most distinctive alternate look in the Wolves 2026-27 NBA rebrand" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</div>

The black alternate is where the rebrand goes the hardest. The tree print side panel pattern lifts directly from the original Hardwood Classic uniform, the silhouette-tree motif that has lived in the Timberwolves brand archive since the 1989 expansion era now returning as the defining design element of the new black alternate. The black base lets the tree print read as the strongest single design element on the jersey and the brighter blue and green trim ties the alternate back to the rest of the new set. We wish the tree print had also landed on the white and blue primaries — the side panel pattern is the strongest piece of design language in the entire rebrand and putting it on every jersey would have unified the look across all three colorways. But the asymmetry has its own logic. Holding the tree print to the black alternate makes the black jersey feel like a true alternate with its own visual identity rather than a recolor of the primary, and the standalone tree print frame elevates the black version into one of the most distinctive alternates in the league.

## The Rebrand Grade

The Timberwolves rebrand delivers on every signal the franchise has been sending since the leaked logos surfaced earlier this year. The brighter blue and green palette replaces the muted navy and forest green direction that has dragged on the brand for the past decade. The throwback design language pulls directly from the franchise's strongest identity period without copying the past one-for-one. The tree print side panel return is the single biggest design win of the rebrand, the Hardwood Classic signature returning as a primary alternate-jersey element. The white-blue-black colorway trio gives the team a complete primary-road-alternate set rather than a single new jersey drop, signaling a full identity reset rather than a partial rebrand. The only nitpick on the entire set is the tree print holding to the black alternate only, the kind of small design call that could have unified the new set even harder, but the asymmetry makes the black alternate the standout piece in a way putting trees on every jersey could not have replicated.
