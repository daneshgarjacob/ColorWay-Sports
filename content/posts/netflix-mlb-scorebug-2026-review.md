Netflix carried the 2026 Field of Dreams game between the Phillies and the Twins, and it brought a **redesigned scorebug** with it. This is not the graphic it debuted with in March. It is the most modern-looking bug in baseball right now, and after an inning of watching it, we like it more than we expected to.

## The New Bug, Broken Down

<img src="/images/posts/netflix-mlb-scorebug-2026/scorebug.jpg" alt="The redesigned Netflix MLB scorebug during the 2026 Field of Dreams game, showing the Schwarber batter bar in Phillies red, the pitcher bar in blue, the Phillies and Twins colour chips with the score, the Netflix wordmark, the base diamonds, the inning, the count and the outs" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 10px;" />
<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 0.75em; color: #999; margin-top: 0.5em;">The redesigned bug during the Field of Dreams broadcast</p>

One rounded translucent panel, stacked in four zones:

- **A batter bar** across the top in the batting team's colour, carrying the lineup spot, the name, and the average. Philadelphia red for Schwarber.
- **A pitcher bar** underneath in blue, with the pitch count on the right.
- **Two team chips** down the left, each a rounded rectangle in the club's own colour holding the cap logo and the run total.
- **A right-hand column** with the Netflix wordmark, the base diamonds, and a bottom row carrying the inning arrow, the count, and the outs.

The debut bug was already rounded and app-like, which made sense for a platform that lives on a phone and a TV. The redesign keeps that instinct and changes three real things:

- **The Netflix mark moved.** It sat top right with a "Live" tag in March. It now lives in the right-hand column beside the bases, which frees the top of the panel entirely for the batter.
- **Team abbreviations became colour chips.** This is the biggest change and the best one. Each club gets its own rounded container in its own colour instead of sharing a bar split by a divider.
- **The batter and pitcher got dedicated bars.** March packed that information more tightly. The new version gives each a full-width row.

## What It Gets Right

**The rounded chips are the whole idea.** You identify the team by colour rather than by reading three letters, and each run total sits inside its own field, so scanning the score takes no effort. On a broadcast where viewers tune in cold, colour beats abbreviation every time.

**It is genuinely clear in HD.** Heavy type, high contrast against the translucent panel, and nothing competing for the same space. Scorebugs fail when they get dense, and this one has air in it.

**Colour on the batter bar is smart.** The bar flips with the half inning, so you always know who is hitting without reading anything.

**Nothing is skeuomorphic.** No fake metal, no bevels, no shadows pretending the graphic is a physical object. It looks like software, and it will age better than the textured bugs many regional networks still run.

## What We Would Change

**It has no connection to the game it is covering.** This is the Field of Dreams, in a cornfield in Dyersville, Iowa, one of the most visually distinctive events baseball stages. The bug looks exactly as it would for a Tuesday game in April. There was room for a small mark, a colour treatment, a typographic nod, and Netflix left it generic. For a one-off event broadcast that is a miss.

**We want challenges per team on it.** Each club's remaining challenges is the single most useful thing a modern baseball bug can carry, and the bottom row has space for it.

To be precise about tonight: **the Field of Dreams game is not ABS-equipped**, so there were no ball and strike challenges to display and their absence is not a fault of this broadcast. The point is what happens when Netflix carries a game that does have the system. The layout should have a home ready for it.

The March version earned a B+ from us and the redesign holds it, which is not a knock. The construction is cleaner, the colour logic is better, and it reads instantly at broadcast size, which is the entire job. It does not reach the A range because a scorebug for a marquee one-off should feel like it belongs to that broadcast, and this one would look identical in any ballpark in the league.

## The Bottom Line

Netflix walked into baseball and produced a cleaner graphic than most networks that have been doing this for decades, then made it better five months later. The team chips are the best idea in it, the legibility is excellent, and the restraint is why both work. Give it a challenge counter and let it acknowledge the game it is covering, and this is an A. Grade: B+.
