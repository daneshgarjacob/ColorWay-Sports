Dortmund are the most consistent club in our European ranking, with both graded kits landing at exactly the same level. Their purple third was one of the first two in Europe to launch and turned out to be one of the more interesting shirts of the season, and the all-black away that arrived in August keeps the set at the same level.

## What Does Borussia Dortmund's 2026/27 Home Kit Look Like?

Yellow with a tonal graphic across the front and lower body drawn from the steel framework of the Minister Stein colliery and the Dortmunder U-Tower.

Simple with a pop, which is the whole brief for a Dortmund home shirt.

What separates this from the other concept-driven kits that struggled this season is that the reference is specific to the city rather than being generic heritage filler. The Minister Stein colliery and the U-Tower are real Dortmund landmarks, and the steel framework translates into a pattern that reads as texture on yellow instead of fighting it.

Yellow is a difficult base. It goes flat under floodlights and it shows every graphic decision you make. Keeping the pattern tonal was the right restraint.

**Grade: B+**

## What Does Borussia Dortmund's 2026/27 Third Kit Look Like?

Purple with neon yellow collar trim and yellow PUMA logos, released July 30 under the line "Shine different."

It reads a little like a goalkeeper shirt, which is usually a criticism and here somehow is not.

Dortmund went somewhere genuinely unusual. Purple has no history at the club and no obvious justification, and normally that is exactly what we mark a shirt down for. The difference is that this does not pretend otherwise. It is not dressed up as a heritage nod or a hidden pattern with a story. It is just a good purple shirt with excellent neon yellow trim.

It was one of the first two third kits in Europe to launch, and one of the first being this interesting set the bar for the rest.

**Grade: B+**

## What Does Borussia Dortmund's 2026/27 Away Kit Look Like?

Black, with a tonal charcoal lightning-bolt pattern running across the body, a buttoned placket collar with white inserts at the neck, white trim on the cuffs, and the crest and PUMA mark in bright yellow. Released August 11 under the line "Wherever we go."

A black away shirt is the safest call in football, so it has to earn its place, and this one does. The bolts give a dark shirt some movement without turning it into a graphic, the white at the neck and cuffs keep it from reading as a training top, and the yellow crest does all the identity work on its own, which is exactly what a Dortmund crest is for.

It is not as brave as the purple third and it was never going to be. As black change kits go it is one of the better ones this season, and it slots into the wardrobe without dragging anything down.

**Grade: B**

## Dortmund's Third Was One Of The First Two In Europe

Worth putting Dortmund's third shirt in context. When it launched on July 30, exactly two of Europe's fifteen biggest clubs had released a third kit for 2026/27: Dortmund and Arsenal. Twelve of the fifteen have now, with only Chelsea, Inter and Paris Saint-Germain still to come.

That made Dortmund's one of the earliest looks at what clubs were doing with the slot where they have the most freedom, and the answer here was a colour with no connection to the club at all. On the evidence, that was the right gamble, and most of the thirds that followed were more cautious.

## Who Makes Borussia Dortmund's Kits?

PUMA, who have supplied Borussia Dortmund since 2012.
