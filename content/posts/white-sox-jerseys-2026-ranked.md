The Chicago White Sox have had a miserable few years on the field, but the closet has never been the problem. Since the 1991 rebrand that traded red and blue for black and silver, this has been one of the cleanest, most disciplined visual identities in baseball. The monochrome palette does not have a weak entry, and the team is smart enough to mostly leave it alone.

The trouble starts when they reach outside it. Three of the four uniforms they have actually worn this season are black, white, silver, and gray, exactly as they should be. The fourth is red, and it is the whole argument of this list.

A quick note on the count. The White Sox technically still carry the charcoal "Southside" kit, their original City Connect, as an available alternate. But they have not put it on the field once in 2026 by our count, so we are ranking the four uniforms that have actually shown up rather than a jersey that has stayed on the hanger. If it makes an appearance, we will slot it in.

Here is every White Sox jersey they have worn in 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

## 4. Red City Connect

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/whitesox/status/2075788964028448943"></a></blockquote>

The 2025 Bulls crossover is a clever piece of marketing and a bad White Sox uniform, and the gap between those two things is the whole problem. Both franchises belong to Jerry Reinsdorf, so the jersey mixes red Bulls pinstripes with black Sox pinstripes, adds a winged-sock sleeve patch from the 1950s, and shipped with two different caps. As a thing to sell, it works. People buy it.

As something to put on the field, it fails the one test that matters for this franchise. The entire point of the White Sox look, the thing that made it iconic in 1991 and kept it iconic for thirty-five years, is that it is black and silver and nothing else. Red is a Bulls color. Dropping it onto a Sox jersey does not read as bold, it reads as borrowed, and the second the team takes the field in it they stop looking like the White Sox and start looking like a promotional night, which is exactly what it is.

Nothing about it belongs. The red fights the black cap, the whole thing photographs like a costume, and a franchise with one of the cleanest identities in the sport spent its City Connect slot looking like the other tenant of the United Center. It is the worst uniform the White Sox own by a wide margin, and it is the only one on this list that does not deserve to exist.

## 3. Road Gray

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/whitesox/status/2077196050704572615"></a></blockquote>

Most road grays exist to disappear. This one is smooth enough to notice. "CHICAGO" arched across the chest in the same black Old English Gothic lettering as the home jersey, trimmed in silver, worn with the black cap, and the whole thing just moves. The Gothic wordmark is the difference, because black-on-gray with a silver edge reads sharp under any road-park lighting while most road sets go flat and anonymous.

There is a quiet confidence to it that a lot of gray uniforms never find. Nothing is trying too hard, nothing is missing, and the monochrome palette that makes the home looks great does the same work on the road. The one small wrinkle for 2026 is the blue-on-gray CME Group sleeve patch, the single spot of off-palette color, and it is minor enough that it never breaks the look. This is one of the best road grays in baseball, and it earns the grade on smoothness alone.

## 2. Home White Pinstripes

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/whitesox/status/2079670689884197363"></a></blockquote>

The longest-running uniform in White Sox history, and the one every fan pictures first. A clean white jersey laced with black pinstripes, "Sox" arched across the chest in the custom Old English Gothic script with silver trim, the black "SOX" cap on top. It has been the home look since 1991, and in thirty-five years the team has barely touched it, because there is nothing to touch.

What makes it great is the lettering. That interlocking Gothic "Sox" is one of the two or three best wordmarks in baseball, distinct enough that it functions as a logo on its own, and the black-on-white-with-silver-trim treatment lets it carry the entire jersey without a single other flourish. This is restraint as a design philosophy, and it is why the White Sox look sharp in an era when half the league is chasing gimmicks.

It ties the road gray on the grade and lands just behind it in the order only because one uniform in this closet takes the same idea and pushes it all the way. The whites are close to perfect. The next one is perfect.

## 1. Black Alternate

The best thing the White Sox wear and one of the best dark uniforms in all of baseball. A black jersey with the diagonal "SOX" script across the chest in silver, the black cap, most often paired with the white pinstriped pants. It is the purest possible statement of the identity this franchise built in 1991, and more than three decades later nothing about it has aged.

Black is the hardest base color to make look good, because it flattens on camera and swallows detail, and this jersey solves the problem the only way that works: with almost nothing on it. The silver diagonal script is the entire design, it sits perfectly against the black, and the contrast reads clean from the upper deck and clean on television. Pairing the black top with the pinstriped pants instead of solid black is the detail that keeps it from going too heavy, and it is the kind of decision that separates a real uniform program from a merch drop.

There is no story attached to it, no anniversary, no campaign, no reason it exists beyond looking outstanding. That is the highest compliment you can pay a uniform. It is the White Sox at their most White Sox, and it is untouchable.

## The Bottom Line on the White Sox Jersey Rankings

The White Sox own one of the best pure identities in baseball, and the ranking proves it by accident: the top three are all black, white, and silver, and they are all excellent, because the franchise figured out its look in 1991 and had the discipline to stop fiddling with it. The black alternate is the crown jewel, a top-five dark uniform in the sport with nothing on it but a silver script and total confidence, and the home whites and road grays are right behind it.

The red City Connect is the one miss, and it is not a small one. It earns the only F on any of these lists for the clearest reason we have handed out: every other jersey looks like the White Sox, and that one looks like the Bulls came to visit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many jerseys do the White Sox have in 2026?**

They have worn four this season: the home white pinstripes, the road grays, the black alternate, and the red City Connect. The team technically still carries the charcoal "Southside" kit, its original City Connect, as an alternate, but it has not appeared in 2026, so we did not rank it.

**What is the best White Sox jersey?**

We rank the black alternate first and grade it an A. The black jersey with the silver diagonal "SOX" script is one of the best dark uniforms in baseball, and it is the purest expression of the black-and-silver identity the team adopted in 1991.

**Why do the White Sox have a red City Connect?**

It is a 2025 crossover with the NBA's Chicago Bulls, who share an owner with the White Sox. It mixes red Bulls pinstripes with black Sox pinstripes and is worn on specific promotional dates. We grade it an F and rank it last, because red is not part of the White Sox's black-and-silver identity and the uniform looks like a promotional night rather than the team.

**What do the White Sox wear at home?**

Most home games are the white pinstripes with the Gothic "Sox" script. Weekend and night home games often feature the black alternate, and the red City Connect and Southside charcoal come out on selected dates.

**Are the White Sox pinstripes black or navy?**

Black. The White Sox have used a black-and-silver monochrome palette since their 1991 rebrand, which replaced the franchise's earlier red and blue.
