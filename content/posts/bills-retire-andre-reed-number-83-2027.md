**The Buffalo Bills are retiring Andre Reed's No. 83.** The team announced it on Thursday, November 19, with the line "83 is heading to the rafters at Highmark Stadium." Reed's number goes up during a home game in the **2027 season**, on a date the Bills have not set yet. He becomes the **fourth Bills player with a retired number**, joining his old teammates **Jim Kelly, Thurman Thomas and Bruce Smith**.

## Quick Answers

- **Are the Bills retiring Andre Reed's number?** Yes. Buffalo announced on November 19, 2026, that Reed's No. 83 will be retired.
- **When is the ceremony?** At a Bills home game during the 2027 regular season. The team has not named the game.
- **How many numbers have the Bills retired?** Reed's 83 makes four: Jim Kelly's 12, Thurman Thomas' 34, Bruce Smith's 78 and Reed's 83.
- **When was the last one?** Thurman Thomas' No. 34, in 2018.
- **Where will it hang?** In the rafters at the new Highmark Stadium. The Bills say 83 is the first number retired at the new building.
- **How did Reed find out?** Bills owner Terry Pegula told him, with Kelly and Thomas there to surprise him.
- **Is Reed speaking this weekend?** Yes. The Bills say he will hold a press conference at Highmark Stadium on Sunday, November 22, before the Week 11 game against the Miami Dolphins.

## What The Bills Announced

The team's site laid it out on Thursday evening. Reed's No. 83 "will go up into the rafters at Highmark Stadium during a home game to be determined during the 2027 regular season." The Bills surprised him with the news: Terry Pegula delivered it, and Jim Kelly and Thurman Thomas were in the room.

Thomas compared it to the day Reed made the Pro Football Hall of Fame. Kelly put it more simply, saying Reed is "one of the main reasons I'm up on that wall," and adding that he thought it should have happened a few years ago.

The announcement post on X carries a photo of Reed in the **royal blue home jersey with white numbers trimmed in red**, holding up a football, under the words "Forever 83."

All four played together on the Bills teams that went to four straight Super Bowls in the early 1990s. Every retired number in franchise history now belongs to that group.

## Why No. 83

Reed played **15 of his 16 NFL seasons in Buffalo, from 1985 to 1999**, and every one of them in 83. The Bills list him as the franchise's all-time leading receiver by a wide margin, with **13,095 receiving yards and 86 touchdown catches**. He is in the Pro Football Hall of Fame.

He was Kelly's No. 1 receiver for all 11 of Kelly's seasons, which is the point Kelly kept coming back to: when a quarterback goes into the Hall, he usually brings a receiver with him.

## Our Take

Our bar for retiring a number is simple: a Hall of Famer, or a player who changed the franchise. Reed is both. He is the best receiver the Bills have ever had, he played his whole prime in one number, and the other three players from those Super Bowl teams were already up there. The only question about 83 was why it took eight years after Thomas' 34 went up.
