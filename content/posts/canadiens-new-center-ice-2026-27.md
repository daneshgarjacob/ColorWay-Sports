The Canadiens have redone their center ice for 2026-27, and they have gone in the opposite direction from the version they used last season. The bold, solid red faceoff circle is out. In its place is open white ice with a ringed crest sitting on top of it, and the whole surface breathes in a way it has not in a year.

The club framed the reveal around the idea that this is the product of relentless drive, of the will to push past every limit and then past yourself. That is a lot of weight to hang on a paint job, but the design underneath it is genuinely good, and it is a smarter piece of ice than the one it replaces.

## What Changed

Last season's center ice, introduced in September 2025, filled the entire central faceoff circle with solid red. It carried a large 3D-styled crest, the words "Canadiens de Montréal," the founding year 1909, and twenty-four grooves running along the embossed edge, one for each of the franchise's record twenty-four Stanley Cups. It was a maximalist piece of work, and it replaced the single oversized logo Montreal had used since 2018.

The 2026-27 version keeps the ideas and throws out the fill. The circle is open white now, framed by a red outer ring and a thinner blue inner ring. The CH crest sits in the middle at a sane size, in its normal red and blue rather than a rendered 3D treatment. "CANADIENS" arches across the top of the ring and "DE MONTRÉAL" arches across the bottom, both in outlined blue. The founding year is still there, split into "19" on one side of the crest and "09" on the other.

Same story, told at a much lower volume.

## Why the Open Ice Is the Right Call

A solid red circle is the kind of thing that looks tremendous in a rendering and fights you on a broadcast. Ice is a reflective white surface lit from directly above, and dropping a large field of saturated red into the middle of it creates a hot spot that pulls the eye away from the puck. It also flattens the crest, because a red logo on a red field has to be outlined heavily to survive, and the more outline you add the less the mark looks like itself.

Going back to open white fixes all of it at once. The crest reads at its natural weight, the red and blue of the rings frame the circle without competing with it, and the ice looks like ice again. On a wide camera the center circle now functions the way a center circle should, as a reference point rather than a billboard.

The wordmark is the piece we would fight hardest to keep. "Canadiens de Montréal" arched around the crest in French is the most Montreal thing on the entire sheet, and it says something about the club that no logo alone can. Keeping it while dropping the red fill is exactly the right edit.

## Where It Falls Short

Losing the twenty-four grooves is a real cost. That detail was the single best idea in the 2025-26 design, a quiet count of the championships built into the geometry rather than announced, and the kind of thing a curious viewer discovers on a replay. Nothing in the new version replaces it.

The "19" and "09" split across the circle is also the weakest element here. It reads as decoration rather than as a date, and someone who does not already know 1909 is not going to assemble it from two halves separated by a crest. A single "1909" would have carried more.

## The Bottom Line

Montreal spent a season on a loud center ice and has quietly walked it back to something better. The open white field lets the crest breathe, the ringed treatment frames the circle instead of shouting over it, and keeping "Canadiens de Montréal" in French preserves the one detail that could not belong to any other team in the league.

It is not perfect. The twenty-four Cup grooves deserved to survive the edit, and splitting 1909 in half was a mistake. But this is a cleaner sheet than the Bell Centre had last year, and it is the version we would keep.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Canadiens' new center ice logo for 2026-27?**

An open white faceoff circle framed by a red outer ring and a thinner blue inner ring, with the CH crest centered in its standard red and blue. "CANADIENS" arches across the top of the ring and "DE MONTRÉAL" across the bottom in outlined blue, with the founding year split into "19" and "09" on either side of the crest.

**What did the Canadiens' center ice look like before?**

The 2025-26 design, introduced in September 2025, filled the whole circle with solid red and used a large 3D-styled crest, the "Canadiens de Montréal" wordmark, the year 1909, and twenty-four grooves along the embossed edge representing the club's twenty-four Stanley Cups. Before that, Montreal used a single oversized logo dating to 2018.

**Why did the Canadiens change the center ice again?**

The club has not given a design rationale beyond framing the reveal around drive and pushing past limits. Practically, the solid red fill was a difficult thing to light and broadcast, and returning to open white lets the crest read at its natural weight.

**Where do the Canadiens play?**

The Bell Centre in Montreal, Quebec, known in French as the Centre Bell, whose wordmarks also appear on the ice on either side of the center circle.

**Do the twenty-four grooves for the Stanley Cups return in 2026-27?**

They do not appear in the new design. The groove detail was specific to the 2025-26 center ice.
