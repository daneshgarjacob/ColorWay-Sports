A team name is the first uniform decision. Before anyone picks a color or draws a helmet logo, the name tells you what the team is supposed to look like. The Cardinals are literally named after the color of their jerseys. The Rams put horns on their helmets. The Steelers wear a logo that was built for the steel industry.

So we went back to the start for all 32 NFL teams. Some names came from fan contests with thousands of entries. Some came from one owner in one room. One came from a used box of college jerseys, one from a stove, and one from a 19th-century poem. Where the story is disputed, we say so.

This is the first part of our team-name history series. MLB, NBA, NHL and college football are next.

## Quick Facts: NFL Team Names

- **Oldest name:** the Cardinals, from 1901.
- **Newest name:** the Commanders, from 2022.
- **Named by a fan contest or public vote:** Bills, Dolphins, Patriots, Ravens, Steelers, Browns, Colts, Jaguars, Broncos, Raiders, Chargers, Falcons, Saints, Buccaneers and Seahawks. In a few of those, the owner overruled the fans or picked a lower finisher.
- **Fans voted, owner went another way:** the Chiefs. The most popular entry in the contest was "Mules."
- **Picked by the owner or front office:** Bengals, Texans, Titans, Jets, Cowboys, Giants, Eagles, Bears, Lions, Vikings, Panthers and Commanders.
- **Moved cities and kept the name:** Cardinals (Chicago, St. Louis, Arizona), Rams (Cleveland, Los Angeles, St. Louis, Los Angeles), Raiders (Oakland, Los Angeles, Oakland, Las Vegas), Chargers (Los Angeles, San Diego, Los Angeles) and Colts (Baltimore, Indianapolis).
- **Borrowed from a baseball team:** the Giants took the name outright. The Bears and Lions were picked to pair with Chicago's Cubs and Detroit's Tigers.
- **Named after a person:** the Browns (coach Paul Brown) and, in spirit, the Chiefs (Kansas City mayor H. Roe Bartle).

## AFC East

### Buffalo Bills

The name goes back to 1947, when Buffalo's All-America Football Conference team ran a name-the-team contest to replace "Bisons." The winner, James F. Dyson, pictured the club as "a band of Buffalo Bills," a nod to the frontiersman Buffalo Bill Cody. Owner James Breuil also ran Frontier Oil, so a Wild West theme fit. When Ralph Wilson started his AFL team in 1959, he brought the old name back. [Buffalo Bills uniform schedule 2026](/stories/bills-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Miami Dolphins

Owner Joe Robbie announced the name on Oct. 8, 1965, after a public contest that drew 19,843 entries and more than 1,000 different names. 622 people suggested "Dolphins." Robbie called the dolphin "one of the fastest and smartest creatures of the sea." The tiebreaker winner, Mrs. Robert Swanson of West Miami, got lifetime passes for correctly predicting a Notre Dame vs. Miami score. ### New England Patriots

Owner Billy Sullivan ran a fan contest in 1960, and "Patriots" was the pick for Boston's new AFL team. They were the Boston Patriots until 1971. The name went straight onto the helmet: Boston Globe cartoonist Phil Bissell drew a Minuteman hiking a football in 1960, and Sullivan made "Pat Patriot" the official logo in 1961. ### New York Jets

This team started as the New York Titans in 1960. A group led by Sonny Werblin bought the club in 1963 and renamed it the Jets on April 15, 1963. Their new home, Shea Stadium, sat between LaGuardia and Idlewild (now JFK) airports, right at the start of the jet age. The new owners also switched from navy and gold to green and white. A club spokesman said green stood for "hope, freshness and high spirits." [New York Jets uniform schedule 2026](/stories/jets-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Baltimore Ravens

After the Browns moved to Baltimore, the team's new name went to a vote. Focus groups cut 17 finalists down to a few, and a Baltimore Sun phone-in poll did the rest. Per the team, 22,463 of 33,748 callers picked "Ravens," a tribute to Edgar Allan Poe's poem "The Raven." Poe lived in Baltimore and is buried there. The name was announced March 29, 1996. ### Cincinnati Bengals

No contest here. Paul Brown picked the name himself when his expansion team was approved in 1967. He revived the Cincinnati Bengals who played in the 1930s and early 1940s, "to give it a link with past professional football in Cincinnati." That original team's founder, Hal Pennington, said he took the name from the Bengal tiger trademark on his mother's stove. ### Cleveland Browns

Owner Mickey McBride held a fan contest in 1945. "Panthers" was popular, but coach Paul Brown shot it down because an earlier Cleveland Panthers team had failed. Most of the entries pointed back to Brown himself, and McBride went with "Browns" over his coach's objection. For years Brown said the team was named after boxer Joe Louis, the "Brown Bomber." He later admitted he made that up, partly because he did not want a team named after himself. [Cleveland Browns uniform schedule 2026](/stories/browns-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Pittsburgh Steelers

Pittsburgh played as the Pirates from 1933 to 1939. After a 1-9-1 season, Art Rooney and the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette ran a 1940 fan contest, and "Steelers" won as a salute to the city's steel industry. Several people sent in the winning name, so no single fan gets the credit. The helmet logo came later: in 1962 Republic Steel offered the team the Steelmark, a symbol made for the American Iron and Steel Institute. Its three shapes originally stood for yellow coal, orange iron ore and blue steel scrap. [Pittsburgh Steelers uniform schedule 2026](/stories/steelers-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Houston Texans

Houston's expansion team got its name through months of research and focus groups, not a fan contest. In March 2000 the list was five names: Apollos, Bobcats, Stallions, Texans and Wildcatters. Owner Bob McNair and Commissioner Paul Tagliabue announced "Texans" at a downtown rally on Sept. 6, 2000. The colors are Battle Red, Deep Steel Blue and Liberty White. The team said its first uniform was built around the state's culture: the bull and the horns. [Houston Texans uniform schedule 2026](/stories/texans-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Indianapolis Colts

The Colts name started in Baltimore in 1947, when a fan contest named the city's new AAFC team after Baltimore's long history of horse breeding and racing. That team folded, but a new Baltimore NFL franchise kept the name in 1953 and took the blue and white of the folded Dallas Texans. The name moved to Indianapolis overnight on March 29, 1984. ### Jacksonville Jaguars

Jacksonville picked its name in 1991, almost two years before it even had a team. A local fan vote chose "Jaguars" over Sharks, Stingrays and Panthers. The team's own site says the Jacksonville Zoo's jaguar, at the time the oldest living jaguar in North America, "had a degree of influence." "Sharks" was a strong runner-up, partly because Jacksonville already had a Sharks team in the 1974 World Football League. ### Tennessee Titans

The Houston Oilers moved to Tennessee in 1997 and played two seasons as the Tennessee Oilers. Owner Bud Adams set up a committee, which narrowed the field to 39 options, and Adams announced "Titans" on Nov. 14, 1998. His reasoning: Titans come from Greek mythology, and Nashville is known as the "Athens of the South." The new name took effect in 1999. ### Denver Broncos

Owner Bob Howsam held a statewide contest in 1960, and eight people entered "Broncos." Ward M. Vining of Lakewood won with a 25-word essay. His daughter says he wrote that broncos were tough and typified the West. His prize was season tickets, reportedly one seat behind a post. The Broncos are the rare NFL team that has never changed its name. Their first colors, mustard and brown, did not last nearly as long. ### Kansas City Chiefs

When Lamar Hunt moved his Dallas Texans to Kansas City in 1963, the Kansas City Star ran a rename contest with more than 4,000 entries. The most popular pick was "Mules." Hunt went with "Chiefs" instead, a tribute to Mayor H. Roe Bartle, known as "the Chief," who had recruited the team. Hunt also designed the arrowhead logo himself, with the interlocking KC inside, inspired by the 49ers' helmet. [Kansas City Chiefs uniform schedule 2026](/stories/chiefs-uniform-schedule-2026)

### Las Vegas Raiders

This one almost went very differently. In 1960 an Oakland Tribune name-the-team contest picked the "Oakland Señors." The name was mocked, and critics questioned the contest because managing partner Chet Soda was known to call people "señor." Days later, the team switched to "Raiders," which had also been among the top finishers. The Raiders started in black, gold and white. Al Davis switched to silver and black in 1963, and the name went with the team to Los Angeles in 1982, back to Oakland in 1995 and to Las Vegas in 2020. ### Los Angeles Chargers

Owner Barron Hilton ran a name-the-team contest for his new Los Angeles AFL team in 1960. Hilton said he liked "Chargers" because fans yelled "charge" and sounded a bugle at Dodgers and USC games. There is a long-running theory that the name also plugged Hilton's new Carte Blanche charge card, but that story is not as well sourced. The name fit the look right away: lightning bolts on the helmets and pants from the first uniform. [Los Angeles Chargers uniform schedule 2026](/stories/chargers-uniform-schedule-2026)

### New York Giants

Tim Mara bought the franchise in 1925 and borrowed the name from baseball's New York Giants. The two teams shared the Polo Grounds. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which NFL team names came from a fan contest?**

The Bills, Dolphins, Patriots, Ravens, Steelers, Browns, Colts, Jaguars, Broncos, Raiders, Chargers, Falcons, Saints, Buccaneers and Seahawks all came out of a fan contest or a public vote. The Chiefs also held a contest, but Lamar Hunt passed on the top choice, "Mules."

**What is the oldest team name in the NFL?**

The Cardinals. The name dates to 1901, when Chris O'Brien bought faded maroon jerseys from the University of Chicago and called the color "Cardinal red."

**How did the Bengals get their name?**

Paul Brown picked it in 1967 to bring back the name of an earlier Cincinnati Bengals team from the 1930s and 1940s. That team's founder said he got the name from the Bengal tiger trademark on his mother's stove.

**Why are the Ravens called the Ravens?**

Fans picked it in a 1996 Baltimore Sun phone poll. It honors Edgar Allan Poe's poem "The Raven." Poe lived in Baltimore and is buried there.

**Are the Browns named after Paul Brown?**

Yes. Fans in a 1945 contest pushed for it, and the owner chose it over Brown's objection. Brown spent years claiming the team was named after boxer Joe Louis, the "Brown Bomber," then admitted he made that up.

**Why are the Packers called the Packers?**

The Indian Packing Company sponsored the team in 1919 and paid for its first uniforms. The local newspaper started calling the team the Packers, and it stuck.

**Why did the Jets change their name from the Titans?**

New owners renamed the team in 1963. Shea Stadium sat between LaGuardia and what is now JFK airport, and the jet age was just getting started.

**How did the Chiefs get their name?**

It honors Kansas City mayor H. Roe Bartle, nicknamed "the Chief," who helped bring Lamar Hunt's Dallas Texans to town in 1963.

**Which NFL teams kept their name after moving cities?**

The Cardinals, Rams, Raiders, Chargers and Colts all kept their names after moving. The Oilers became the Titans, and the Browns' move to Baltimore created the Ravens. Cleveland kept the Browns name.

**What is the newest NFL team name?**

The Washington Commanders, announced Feb. 2, 2022.

## The Bottom Line

About half of the 32 NFL names came out of fan contests, the oldest came from a jersey color, and the newest is barely four years old. And a lot of the league's best uniform details, from the Rams' horns to the Patriots' Minuteman to the Steelers' Steelmark, started with the name.
