The Blue Jays are the rare team that lives in its alternates. Between the royal blue and the powder blue, Toronto spends the large majority of its calendar in some shade of blue, treating color as the house style instead of a once-a-week treat, and it works because both blues are excellent. This is also the franchise's 50th-season year, so every jersey but one carries a commemorative 50th mark on the sleeve.

Then there is the City Connect, which we cannot stand. Here is every Toronto Blue Jays jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

We hate this one. The "Night Mode" City Connect, unveiled in 2024, is built on a deep "pitch blue" meant to evoke Lake Ontario after dark, with an illustration of the Toronto skyline running across the chest and a red "Toronto" wordmark and number stacked on the front. On paper the concept is fine. On the field it is a mess, a busy, murky jersey that puts a cityscape and a number and a wordmark all in the same crowded space and asks the deep blue to hold it together. It does not.

The Blue Jays own one of the cleanest visual identities in the sport, all crisp blues and the bird-and-leaf, and this throws every bit of that restraint out the window. It is the one Toronto look we would pull from the rotation tomorrow, and it earns the only F in the closet.

Once a year the closet turns red. For Canada Day the Blue Jays swap the blues for a vivid red jersey that nods to the national flag, worn for the July 1 home game and essentially that game alone. It is the rarest uniform Toronto owns, and the scarcity is the whole point. One game, one flag, one of the most distinctly Canadian sights in the sport.

We like it, and we like what it stands for, but as a piece of design it is more occasion than outfit. Red is not really the Blue Jays' color, and a single-day novelty is always going to grade below the looks the team actually builds its identity on. A solid, festive B.

It is a very good uniform that the team itself treats as a secondary option, and that is the whole story of this closet.

The road gray is genuinely one of the best in baseball, "Toronto" across the chest in the team's navy-white-navy split lettering, beautifully proportioned and unmistakably Blue Jays. If we were ranking gray road sets across the whole league, this one would be near the top. It sits at B+ here for the same reason the home white does: the royal blue out-snaps it on the road too, so a great gray ends up as a select-game look. When it does come out, it is a reminder of how good a simple, classic gray can be.

Here is the twist that defines the whole closet: the alternate is really the default. The royal-blue jersey, a clean blue top with "Blue Jays" across the chest and the navy bird-and-leaf cap, was Toronto's most-worn uniform in 2025 by a wide margin, used freely at home and on the road. It is bright, modern, and it leans into the half of the team's name everyone actually says. Most clubs treat a colored alternate as a treat; the Blue Jays made theirs the house style, and it more than earns the promotion. A clear A.

The powder blue is the one fans buy, and it is our favorite jersey the Blue Jays own. Toronto's modern version, introduced before the 2020 season, is a soft sky-blue top with navy-white-navy serifed split lettering, a nod to the powder-blue road sets the club has worn on and off since 1977, paired with a navy cap. It is nostalgic without being a literal throwback, and it got a national spotlight during Toronto's run to the 2025 World Series, which only cemented its status.

It edges the royal blue for the top spot because it does everything the royal does, then adds a layer of history and warmth on top. Between these two blues, Toronto covers the large majority of its calendar in the best colors it owns, and the powder is the crown jewel of the whole rotation. An easy A and the best look in the closet.

## The Bottom Line on the Blue Jays Jersey Rankings

Toronto built its identity on blue, and it was the right call. The powder blue and the royal blue are the two best jerseys the team owns, they grade out as A's, and together they cover the large majority of the calendar, which is exactly how it should be when your alternates are this good. The home white and the road gray are both genuinely excellent, a pair of B+ classics that most teams would run every day, and they only sit this low because the blues are simply better.

The Canada Day red is a fun once-a-year novelty, and then there is the City Connect, the one real miss in an otherwise sharp closet. The "Night Mode" kit takes a franchise built on clean blues and clutters it with a skyline, a wordmark, and a number all fighting for the same space, and it earns the only F on this list. Lose the City Connect and the Blue Jays might have the most consistent closet in the American League.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many jerseys do the Blue Jays have in 2026?**

Six: the home whites, the road grays, the royal-blue alternate, the powder-blue alternate, the red Canada Day uniform, and the "Night Mode" City Connect. Every jersey except the City Connect carries a 50th-season commemorative patch this year.

**What is the best Blue Jays jersey?**

We rank the powder-blue alternate first and grade it an A, just ahead of the royal blue. It is the modern version of Toronto's classic powder-blue look, a fan favorite that got a national spotlight during the 2025 World Series run, and the best jersey in the closet.

**Why is the Blue Jays City Connect ranked last?**

We grade the "Night Mode" City Connect an F, the only failing mark in the closet. It clutters a franchise built on clean blues with a chest full of skyline art, a "Toronto" wordmark, and a front number all competing for space, and against Toronto's otherwise sharp identity it reads as a busy mess.
