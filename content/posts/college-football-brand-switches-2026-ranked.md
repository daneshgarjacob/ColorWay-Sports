The summer of 2026 was the biggest apparel-shuffle in recent college football memory. Seven programs changed shoe-and-jersey brands at once, headlined by two blue-bloods walking away from Nike, a $300 million check, and one fan base waking up to find its team colors had quietly changed shade. Some of these switches were about money, some were about identity, and a couple were about both.

So we did what we do: graded every one of them. This is our ranking of the 2026 college football brand switches, worst to best, judged on the whole package, the money, the drip, and whether the new look actually improves on the old one. Two of them are barely revealed, so we will grade those when the uniforms land. The rest are fair game.

## 4. South Carolina: Under Armour to Nike

On paper this is an upgrade. South Carolina ended a 19-year run with Under Armour and signed a 10-year Nike deal worth about $70 million in product plus cash and a cut of net sales, and Nike is simply the bigger, more coveted football brand. The problem is what happened to the garnet. Nike slots its schools into standardized color categories, and the Gamecocks landed in "Team Crimson," which reads noticeably closer to red than the deep Pantone 202 garnet the fan base grew up on. People noticed immediately.

That is the whole story here, and it is why the switch grades a C+. The brand got better and the concept barely changed, but when your program's entire visual identity is one very specific shade and the switch quietly alters it, that is a real cost, not a cosmetic one. A great brand move should never leave fans squinting at their own team colors.

This is the biggest raise of the bunch. Georgia Tech left Adidas for a 10-year Under Armour deal reported at around $186 million, said to be nearly six times its old average annual take, and the Yellow Jackets used the move to refresh their identity, tweaking their shades of old gold and navy. We respect the ambition, and getting paid that kind of money is a win on its own.

We land at a B- because changing your colors and your brand in the same offseason is a real gamble. Under Armour has historically produced the least distinctive football uniforms of the three major brands, and a color refresh only works if the new palette actually looks better than the old one. Tech is betting big on itself here. The money is an A. The execution is a question mark until we see the full set on the field.

Tennessee walked away from Nike for a 10-year Adidas deal reported north of $200 million, with Adidas positioning the Vols as a face of its college portfolio. The rollout was patient and confident, home orange first, then the rest of the set over the following week, and crucially, the new uniforms barely changed. The Power T, the classic orange, the clean lines all survived the switch.

That restraint is exactly why it grades a B+. Tennessee's orange is one of the most recognizable looks in the sport, and the smart play when you change brands is to protect it, not reinvent it. Adidas did just that, cashed a huge check for the school, and kept the identity intact. The only thing keeping it from the top spot is that "we kept it the same" is good, but it is not quite the flex the school above it pulled off.

## 1. Penn State: Nike to Adidas

Penn State ran the perfect brand switch. After 33 years with Nike, the Nittany Lions signed a 10-year, $300 million deal with Adidas, the richest of any switch on this list, and then did the single most important thing a program with an iconic look can do: nothing. The uniforms stayed plain and simple, no names on the backs, no logos on the helmets, the same austere blue-and-white that has defined Penn State for generations.

That is why it takes the top spot at an A-. The whole point of the Penn State uniform is that it does not chase trends, and the risk in any big-money switch is that the new brand wants to leave its fingerprints all over the product. Adidas resisted, took the biggest bag on the board, and left a perfect look untouched. Maximum money, zero identity damage. That is how you do it.

## Still to Come: Rice

Rice is the one switch we cannot grade yet. The Owls left Adidas for a seven-year Nike deal and, as of now, have not fully revealed their new uniforms. Given Rice's clean, underrated navy-and-gray look, we are optimistic, and we will drop a graded verdict here the day the set is unveiled.

## The Other Switches

Two more programs changed brands without the blue-blood spotlight: New Mexico State moved from Under Armour to Adidas, and South Dakota left Adidas for Nike. Neither came with a dramatic redesign, but both are worth logging, because the 2026 brand carousel ran deeper than just the marquee names.

## The Bottom Line

The 2026 brand switches split into two clear camps: the schools that changed almost nothing and won (Penn State and Tennessee, who protected iconic looks and cashed enormous checks), and the schools that changed something and took on risk (Georgia Tech's color refresh, South Carolina's shade of garnet). Penn State's $300 million move is the model, prove your look is worth protecting, then get paid to protect it. We will update this ranking as Rice reveals its set and as Georgia Tech's new Under Armour uniforms hit the field.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which college football teams switched apparel brands for 2026?**

Seven programs switched: Penn State (Nike to Adidas), Tennessee (Nike to Adidas), South Carolina (Under Armour to Nike), Georgia Tech (Adidas to Under Armour), Rice (Adidas to Nike), New Mexico State (Under Armour to Adidas), and South Dakota (Adidas to Nike).

**How much is Penn State's Adidas deal worth?**

Penn State's deal with Adidas is a 10-year agreement worth $300 million, the largest of any 2026 college football brand switch, ending a 33-year partnership with Nike.

**Did South Carolina change its colors when it switched to Nike?**

Effectively, yes. Nike places schools in standardized color categories, and South Carolina landed in "Team Crimson," which reads closer to red than the deep garnet (Pantone 202) the program used under Under Armour. Many fans noticed the shift immediately.

**Which 2026 brand switch was the best?**

We rank Penn State's move to Adidas as the best. It brought the biggest payday ($300 million) while keeping the Nittany Lions' iconic plain, no-name uniforms completely intact, taking maximum money with zero risk to the identity.
