Wondering what kit your club is wearing in the Champions League this week? Here is the part most fans never hear: **UEFA settles it at a meeting the day before the match, and the visiting club is the one that changes.** Kits in Europe are not a matchday surprise either. They are agreed the night before, in a room with both clubs, the referee and the UEFA delegate.

This page is that schedule. Every league-phase fixture, every matchday, who wears what and why the away side sometimes reaches for a third kit. We confirm each match against matchday photography and highlights, never against preview graphics, and we update it as each night is played. For the Premier League version of this page, see our [Premier League kit schedule](/stories/premier-league-kit-schedule-2026-27).

## Matchday 1: Every Kit Matchup

Eighteen fixtures across three nights, Tuesday September 8 to Thursday September 10, and the season opened with the two biggest shirts in the competition on the same pitch. **Real Madrid wore the white home shirt with the dark green trim and the pink shoulder stripes, and Inter wore the black and blue home stripes** at the Bernabeu, a 2-1 Madrid win and the cleanest picture of the night: white against black and blue needs no discussion. **Borussia Dortmund wore the yellow home shirt and Villarreal, whose own home shirt is yellow, changed into the white away kit** at Signal Iduna Park, which is the whole reason change kits exist, yellow against yellow was never going to be allowed. **Porto wore the blue and white home stripes and Manchester City changed into the black away shirt with the gold bee pattern** at the Dragao, a 2-0 City win. City could not wear sky blue against Porto's blue stripes, and the black away shirt with the gold trim is one of the better travelling kits in Europe this season. **Club Brugge wore the blue and black home stripes and Aston Villa went all the way to the icy blue third shirt** in Bruges, because Villa's claret home shirt and black away shirt both fall too close to Brugge's stripes. Villa won it 3-2. The two early kickoffs, AEK Athens against LASK and Lille against Real Betis, are confirmed below as soon as we have matchday photography for each.

## How Champions League Kit Clashes Actually Get Decided

The Champions League runs on UEFA's equipment rules, not the domestic ones, and the process is different from the Premier League's in three ways that matter for this page.

**Clubs register their kits with UEFA before the season.** Every club in the competition submits its first, second and third choice kits, including the goalkeeper sets, and those are the only shirts it can wear in Europe. A club cannot pull out a one-off shirt for a European night the way it might for a domestic cup game.

**The home club wears its first-choice kit.** The visiting club has to wear a kit that contrasts clearly with it, and where the colors could be confused, the visiting club is the one that changes. If the second kit also falls too close, the third kit is the answer, which is why Aston Villa turned up in Bruges in the icy blue third rather than the black away shirt.

**It is settled at the organisational meeting the day before.** UEFA holds a meeting on the eve of every match with both clubs, the referee and the UEFA match delegate, and the kit colors for both teams, keepers included, are confirmed there. By the time the teams walk out, the decision is a day old.

**The sleeves are the tell.** Every club wears the competition's starball badge on one sleeve and the Respect badge on the other, and the league phase gives every club four home games and four away games, so every registered kit gets its airing across the eight matchdays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What kit is my club wearing in the Champions League this week?**

Check the matchday grid above. The home club wears its first-choice kit, and the visiting club changes only where UEFA decides the colors fall too close. Kits are agreed at the organisational meeting the day before the match, so the answer exists a day before kickoff.

**Who decides which kit a team wears in the Champions League?**

UEFA, through its match delegate, at a meeting with both clubs and the referee on the eve of the game. The home club has priority and wears its first-choice kit. The visiting club wears a contrasting kit, and if there is a clash it is the visitor that changes.

**Why did Aston Villa wear their third kit against Club Brugge?**

Because Brugge's blue and black stripes ruled out both Villa's claret home shirt and their black away shirt at once. The icy blue third was the only registered kit that contrasted clearly, which is exactly the situation a third kit exists for.

**Why did Manchester City wear black at Porto?**

Porto's home shirt is blue and white stripes, so City's sky blue home shirt was too close. The black away shirt with the gold bee pattern was the clear contrast, and it is the shirt City will wear at most blue-shirted opponents in Europe this season.

**Can a club wear its home kit away from home in the Champions League?**

Yes, whenever there is no clash. Real Madrid's white travels almost everywhere, and Arsenal's red home shirt is the expected kit at Napoli because red against sky blue needs no change.

**How many games does each club play in the league phase?**

Eight, four at home and four away, against eight different opponents, between September and January. The top eight in the single table go straight to the round of 16 and places nine to twenty-four go into a knockout play-off.
