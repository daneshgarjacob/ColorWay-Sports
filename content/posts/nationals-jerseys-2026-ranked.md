Washington's closet is a red, white and navy set that should be almost impossible to get wrong, and mostly it is not wrong. What it is, in places, is empty. The 2024 cleanup stripped the front chest number off the home white and the navy in the name of a tidier finish, and the ranking below is partly a story about what that number was holding together. Add a City Connect that replaced a genuinely beloved kit with a street map, and you get the widest spread we have seen outside Miami. Here is every Washington Nationals jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

Introduced for 2025 to replace the retired Cherry Blossom kit, the District Blueprint is an "American denim" blue jersey whose body traces the street grid of Washington, D.C. — quadrants, grand avenues and traffic circles — in a white blueprint motif. The chest carries a 3D interlocking "DC," the cap swaps in a block "W" with the Capitol Dome inside it flanked by cherry blossoms, and the white pants add mosaic trim. It anchors Friday and Saturday home games.

It grades a D-, the lowest mark we have handed out in this whole series, and the sin is not just the design, it is what it replaced. The Cherry Blossom kit was one of the two or three best things the City Connect program ever produced: soft pink and cream, instantly identifiable, tied to the one week a year the entire country thinks about Washington. Trading that for a denim shirt with a street map printed on it is a downgrade so steep it is hard to look at. A city grid is not a visual identity, it is a diagram, and printing one across a jersey gives you texture where a uniform needs shape. Two good pieces survive, the Capitol Dome cap and the interlocking "DC." Both deserve a better shirt.

The home white carries the cursive "Nationals" script angled gently upward across the chest in red trimmed with navy, under the red curly W cap. It became the primary home look in 2022, and in 2024 the front chest number was removed for what the club called a tidier finish. It takes most of every homestand.

It grades a C+, and the missing number is the entire problem. The script is fine, the palette is fine, the cap is one of the best in baseball. But a single line of cursive lettering on a white shirt leaves an enormous amount of blank fabric below it, and the front number was the thing giving that space something to do. We made this exact case in our breakdown of [which MLB teams wear numbers on the front of the jersey](/stories/mlb-teams-with-numbers-on-front-of-jersey), and it holds up: some clubs wear the no-number look beautifully, and Washington is not one of them. This is a uniform that got tidier and emptier in the same move.

On the road it is "WASHINGTON" arched across the chest in block navy trimmed white and red, with tricolour piping down the jersey and pants and the curly W cap. Unlike the home white and the navy, the road set kept its front chest number through the 2024 refresh.

It grades a C+ and edges the whites on that number alone. Same grade, opposite problems: the road gray has the piece the home jersey is missing, and the number does exactly what you would want, filling the chest and making the uniform feel finished. What drags it back down is the wordmark. That block "WASHINGTON" is a stiff, generic treatment with no character in the letterforms at all, the kind of type you would find on any road jersey in any league. The Nationals own a genuinely great mark in the curly W and a nice script in "Nationals," and the road set uses neither. Right instinct on the number, wrong font above it.

The red alternate sat out a season and returned for 2025: a red jersey with the curly W on the left chest, with the old piping stripped out for a cleaner finish. No fixed day, home or road, whenever the staff wants colour.

It grades a B+ and it is the jersey that proves the curly W can carry a chest on its own. Where the home white leaves blank space and the road gray fills it with a stiff wordmark, the red simply puts the best logo the franchise owns on the front and stops. Bright red against white pants is the boldest thing in the regular rotation, removing the piping was the right call, and keeping it off the schedule means it still feels like an occasion. The only reason it is not first is that the jersey above it does the same trick with more history behind it.

The navy alternate is the dark counterpart to the red: a navy base with the "Nationals" script across the chest and the curly W cap. Another no-fixed-day option, and it surfaces most often for night games where the dark top pops under the lights.

It grades an A, the best jersey Washington owns, and part of that is design and part of it is memory. On navy, the script that looks stranded on a white shirt suddenly has something to push against, and the red-and-white lettering glows under the lights in a way it simply cannot at home in daylight. The same wordmark, on the right base, becomes a completely different jersey. The rest is 2019. This is the look the Nationals lived in through the run to the only World Series title in franchise history, and for anyone who watched it, the navy is not an alternate at all. It is what that team looked like. Uniforms accumulate meaning, and this one has more of it than anything else in the closet.

## The Bottom Line on the Nationals Jersey Rankings

Two things decide this ranking, and neither is the colour scheme. Red, white and navy is a fine palette and Washington has never had a problem with it.

The first is the chest. The two jerseys at the top, the navy and the red, both put one strong element on the front and leave it alone: the script on a base that suits it, and the curly W standing by itself. The two in the middle both have chest problems. The home white lost its front number in 2024 and has been carrying dead space ever since. The road gray kept the number, which is why it edges the whites, but hangs a stiff block wordmark above it that could belong to anybody.

The second is history. The navy wins outright because it is the jersey of 2019, and no amount of design analysis competes with the uniform a franchise won its only title in.

Then there is the District Blueprint at a D-, the lowest grade in this entire series, and it earned that mostly by following the Cherry Blossom kit. Washington had one of the best City Connects in baseball and swapped it for a street map on denim. Some of these rankings are close calls. That one is not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Nationals jersey for 2026?**

We rank the navy alternate No. 1 with an A. The "Nationals" script works far better on navy than it does on the home white, it pops under the lights, and it is the jersey Washington wore through the 2019 World Series run. The red curly W alternate is second at a B+.

**How many uniforms do the Nationals have in 2026?**

Five: the script "Nationals" home white, the "WASHINGTON" road gray, the red curly W alternate, the navy alternate, and the "District Blueprint" City Connect.

**What happened to the Nationals Cherry Blossom City Connect?**

It was retired after the 2024 season, as part of MLB and Nike's plan to refresh City Connect designs roughly every three years. It was replaced for 2025 by the "District Blueprint," a denim-blue kit tracing the D.C. street grid. We grade the replacement a D-, our lowest mark in this series, largely because of what it followed.

**Why don't the Nationals have a number on the front of their home jersey?**

It was removed in the 2024 refresh for a cleaner look, along with the front number on the navy alternate. The road gray kept its front number, which is why the road set feels more finished than the home white despite sharing the same grade.

**When do the Nationals wear their red jerseys?**

There is no fixed day. The red curly W alternate returned for 2025 after a season away and is a manager's-choice look that appears both at home and on the road whenever the staff wants a jolt of colour.
