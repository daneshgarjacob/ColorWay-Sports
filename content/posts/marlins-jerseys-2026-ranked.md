This is the widest gap in any closet we have ranked. One Marlins jersey is an A. Nothing else clears a C+. That is not a team with a bad designer, it is a team whose best idea is thirty-three years old and only comes out on Sundays, while the identity it actually built in the meantime cannot get out of the C range. Here is every Miami Marlins jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

Debuted in 2021 and officially branded Retro Wave, this is neon pink, teal, and black lifted from the Miami Vice era and the art deco storefronts of South Beach. The cap is matte black with a hot pink brim, and the "Miami" wordmark runs in a script that looks like it came off the side of a 1985 convertible. It owns every Friday home game at loanDepot park.

It grades a D, and the concept is not the problem. Tying a uniform to Miami Vice and art deco is a genuinely real connection to the city, which is more than most City Connects can claim. The execution is where it comes apart. The base is black, so the neon pink and teal that are supposed to be the whole idea end up as trim on a dark shirt, and a black jersey in Miami in July is its own kind of wrong. Worse, it is competing directly with a teal jersey this same team wears two days later that does the retro-Miami thing without a gimmick attached. When your novelty jersey and your throwback are chasing the same feeling, the throwback wins every time.

The black alternate is the plain dark top in the everyday rotation, carrying the cursive "Marlins" script with the Caliente red and white trim. It has no fixed day and turns up midweek at home and on the road when the staff wants something other than the white or the gray.

It grades a C. There is nothing offensive here and the script is the same decent piece of lettering that appears on the whites. The trouble is that it is the second black jersey in a five-uniform closet, and Miami is not a black-jersey city. This is a franchise with teal, hot pink, and Caliente red available to it, playing in the most colour-saturated market in American sport, and two of its five uniforms are dark. The black alternate is not bad. It is just the most replaceable thing they own, and you can feel the closet getting duller every time it appears.

The road gray is the standard away set, carrying the "Miami" wordmark with black and Caliente red trim and the stylised "M" cap. It handles most of the trip.

But it is also the clearest illustration of the problem: on gray, with black lettering and a thin red outline, there is essentially no colour on this uniform at all. A team whose whole visual pitch is a tropical city ends up with a road look you could hand to half the National League without anyone noticing. Competent, forgettable, and a waste of the best palette in baseball.

The everyday home jersey: clean white with black piping, the "Miami" wordmark across the chest in black and Caliente red, and the stylised "M" cap. Under the current weekend system it owns Saturday home games, sitting between Retro Wave Friday and Teal Sunday.

It grades a C+, and it edges the road gray on the strength of the same red that gets lost on a neutral base. On white, the Caliente red trim actually registers, and the wordmark has enough contrast to read from the seats. It is a perfectly decent modern home white. The frustration is what it is standing next to. Slotted between the neon Friday and the teal Sunday, the Saturday whites are the day the Marlins are least themselves, and a closet this colourful should not have its most-worn home look be the quietest thing in it.

Teal Sundays. Miami wears the original 1993 Florida Marlins teal for every Sunday home game, in the exact shade from the inaugural season, with the block "MARLINS" wordmark and its swoosh underline across the chest, a legacy patch carrying the original circular Florida Marlins logo on the sleeve, and white pants underneath so the teal does all the talking.

It grades an A, and it is not close to anything else in this closet. That teal is one of the great colours in baseball history, specific enough that you can date it to the year, and nobody else in the sport is anywhere near it. The design is disciplined too: one loud colour, one strong wordmark, white pants to keep it grounded, and no concept to explain. Everything the Retro Wave is straining to say about Miami being bright and distinctive and a bit 1990s, this jersey says without trying, because it actually is those things. Handing it a standing Sunday slot is the single best decision in this wardrobe, and it is the only Marlins uniform that makes you want to watch the game it is in.

## What About the Caribbean Blue Alternate?

Not in play. The blue alternate with the cursive "Marlins" script is still part of the official wardrobe, but Miami has not worn it in a single game in 2026. Our [daily uniform tracker](/stories/mlb-uniform-tracker-2026) has logged every Marlins game this season and the blue has not appeared once, so we have left it out of this ranking rather than grade a jersey that is not on the field. If it comes back, we will add it.

## The Bottom Line on the Marlins Jersey Rankings

An A and then a cliff. No other closet we have ranked has a gap like this one, and the reason is that Miami already solved its own uniform problem in 1993 and has spent the thirty-odd years since trying to solve it again.

The teal is the answer. It is bright, it is specific, it is impossible to confuse with anyone else, and it fits a city that is itself bright and specific and impossible to confuse with anyone else. Give it Sundays and it becomes the best jersey program in the sport.

The rest of the wardrobe keeps reaching for the same feeling and missing. The Retro Wave has the right reference points and buries them under black. The black alternate is a second dark jersey in a tropical market. The whites and grays are perfectly competent and completely anonymous, which is the last thing this franchise should be.

Miami does not need a new idea. It needs to wear its old one more than once a week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Marlins jersey for 2026?**

We rank the teal throwback No. 1 with an A. It is the original 1993 Florida Marlins teal in the exact inaugural-season shade, with the block "MARLINS" wordmark and a legacy sleeve patch, worn for every Sunday home game at loanDepot park. Nothing else in the closet grades above a C+.

**How many uniforms do the Marlins wear in 2026?**

Five have appeared this season: the home white, the road gray, the black alternate, the Retro Wave City Connect, and the teal throwback. The Caribbean blue alternate remains in the official wardrobe but has not been worn in a game all year.

**What is the Marlins City Connect uniform?**

The Retro Wave, debuted in 2021. Neon pink, teal, and black drawn from Miami Vice and South Beach art deco, with a matte black cap, hot pink brim, and a "Miami" script pulled from mid-eighties car styling. It is worn every Friday home game. We grade it a D, because the black base smothers the neon that is supposed to be the whole point.

**When do the Marlins wear their teal jerseys?**

Every Sunday home game, as part of Teal Sundays. Miami committed the entire Sunday home slot to the 1993 throwback, which is why it is the one guaranteed sighting on the schedule.

**Do the Marlins still have their blue alternate?**

It is still in the official wardrobe, but they have not worn it in a game in 2026. Our daily tracker has covered every Marlins game this season and the Caribbean blue has not appeared, so treat it as shelved for now rather than retired outright.
