**The shortest player in NBA history is going to the rafters.** The Charlotte Hornets announced on **Tuesday, October 27, 2026**, that they will retire **Muggsy Bogues' No. 1** at halftime of their home game against the **Milwaukee Bucks** on **Thursday, February 11, 2027**, at Spectrum Center. Bogues becomes only the **third player in franchise history** with a retired number, joining **Bobby Phills (No. 13)** and **Dell Curry (No. 30)**.

Here is everything about the Muggsy Bogues jersey retirement: the date, the opponent, the jersey Charlotte is scheduled to wear that night, the full list of Hornets retired numbers (including the strange Bobcats-era gap), and the one name that should have been on this list before anyone else's. Tell us we're wrong.

## Quick Answers

- **Are the Hornets retiring Muggsy Bogues' number?** Yes. The team announced it on October 27, 2026.
- **When is the ceremony?** Halftime of Hornets vs. Bucks, **Thursday, February 11, 2027**, at Spectrum Center in Charlotte.
- **What number?** **No. 1**, the number Bogues wore for 10 seasons in Charlotte (1988-89 to 1997-98).
- **What jersey do the Hornets wear that night?** The **Statement Edition**, per the NBA's official 2026-27 uniform schedule. Milwaukee is scheduled to wear its **Association** (white) jersey.
- **How many numbers have the Hornets retired?** Three once Bogues' goes up: **1, 13 and 30**.
- **Who wore No. 1 before this?** **LaMelo Ball**, from the 2020 draft until Charlotte traded him to Minnesota in summer 2026.

That is the entire list. Three numbers in franchise history, and two of the three ceremonies fall inside 11 months.

### No. 13 Bobby Phills

Phills died in a car crash in Charlotte on January 12, 2000, at 30, in the middle of his third season with the team. The Hornets retired No. 13 less than a month later, on February 9, 2000, the first retired number in franchise history. Then it got complicated: when the original Hornets moved to New Orleans in 2002, [the banner went with them](https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/hornets-to-retire-late-bobby-phills-jersey-again). Charlotte held a second ceremony and hung it again on November 1, 2014, at halftime against Memphis, after the Bobcats took back the Hornets name and history. New Orleans, now the Pelicans, later put No. 13 back into circulation.

### No. 30 Dell Curry

Curry was an original 1988 Hornet and retired as the franchise leader in games (701), points (9,839) and made threes (929). Charlotte raised No. 30 at halftime against Orlando on March 19, 2026, with Stephen and Seth Curry in the building.

### No. 1 Muggsy Bogues

Bogues was the No. 12 pick in 1987 by the Washington Bullets, left unprotected, and taken by Charlotte in the 1988 expansion draft. In 10 seasons and 632 games as a Hornet he averaged 8.8 points, 8.8 assists and 1.7 steals, and he left as the franchise leader in **assists (5,557)**, **steals (1,067)** and **minutes (19,768)**. He is still the only Hornet to average 10 or more assists in a season, and he did it twice (10.7 in 1989-90 and 10.1 in 1993-94). Those teams won three 50-win seasons, the franchise's first playoff series in 1993 and a franchise-best 54-28 in 1996-97.

At 5-foot-3, he will be the shortest player ever to have his number retired by an NBA team. [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/hornets-retire-jersey-muggsy-bogues-shortest-player-nba-history/) notes the previous shortest was 5-foot-9 Calvin Murphy. Wake Forest retired his college No. 14 back in 1987.

## The Charlotte History Quirk: Whose Numbers Are These?

Charlotte's retired-number list only makes sense once you know the franchise's family tree:

- **1988-2002:** The original Charlotte Hornets. Bogues, Curry and Phills all played here.
- **2002:** The team moved to New Orleans and took its records, and the Phills banner, with it.
- **2004-2014:** Charlotte got an expansion team, the **Bobcats**.
- **May 20, 2014:** The Bobcats officially became the Hornets again, and Charlotte took back the 1988-2002 Hornets history and records. New Orleans kept its own 2002-2013 records.

So every Hornets retired number belongs to a player from the original 1988-2002 team. **Not one Bobcats-era player has his number in the rafters.** Which brings us to the problem.

## Our Take: The NBA Has Gone Soft on Retired Numbers

We will say it plainly: retiring Muggsy Bogues' No. 1 is the wrong call, and it is part of a bigger problem. The NBA has gotten way too soft about retiring numbers.

A retired number used to mean something close to untouchable. Our standard is simple. **If you made the Hall of Fame, your number goes up.** If you did not, you have to be something extremely rare, a player who changed the franchise or the game itself. Being beloved, being a great teammate, being a fan favorite and staying on as an ambassador are all real things. They are what a ring of honor, a bobblehead night or a statue in the concourse are for. They are not what the rafters are for.

Bogues is a great story. At 5-foot-3 he is the shortest player in NBA history, he is Charlotte's leader in assists, steals and minutes, and he was the face of the teal-and-purple era. He is not a Hall of Famer, he never made an All-Star team, and his career averages with Charlotte were 8.8 points and 8.8 assists. That is a fun, important player. It is not a retired number.

He is not the only one. The Warriors [retired Andre Iguodala's No. 9](https://www.ktvu.com/news/warriors-retire-andre-iguodalas-jersey-emotional-ceremony) on February 23, 2025, after a win over the Mavericks, the seventh number in franchise history. Iguodala was a Finals MVP and a key piece of four championship teams, and he is not in the Hall of Fame. We thought that was the wrong call too. A banner for a great role player on a dynasty is a team-ring banner, not a number.

And if Charlotte follows this with **Kemba Walker's No. 15**, as plenty of fans and even Stephen Curry have pushed for, that would be the wrong call as well. Walker is the franchise's all-time leading scorer with 12,009 points and a four-time All-Star, per [NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/kemba-walker-announces-retirement), and he was the best player of the post-2004 years. He is also not a Hall of Famer, and Charlotte never won a playoff series in his eight seasons there. Leading a franchise in points is not the same as being one of the greats.

The more numbers a team hangs, the less each one means. Save the rafters for the players who would be there no matter which jersey they wore. Tell us we're wrong.
