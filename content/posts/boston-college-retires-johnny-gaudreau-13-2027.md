**Boston College will retire Johnny Gaudreau's No. 13.** The school announced on Wednesday, November 18, that the number goes to the rafters in a pregame ceremony on **Friday, February 12, 2027, when BC hosts UConn at Kelley Rink** inside Conte Forum.

Gaudreau becomes only the third player in BC men's hockey history with a retired number. The other two are David Emma's No. 16 and Mike Mottau's No. 3.

## Quick Answers

- **Is Boston College retiring Johnny Gaudreau's number?** Yes. BC announced on November 18, 2026, that his No. 13 will be retired.
- **When is the ceremony?** Before the game against UConn on February 12, 2027, at Kelley Rink in Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts.
- **How many numbers has BC men's hockey retired?** Three, once Gaudreau's goes up: David Emma's 16, Mike Mottau's 3 and Gaudreau's 13.
- **What did Gaudreau win at BC?** The 2012 national championship as a freshman, the 2014 Hobey Baker Award, two Hockey East Player of the Year awards and three Beanpots.
- **How many points did he score at BC?** 175 in 119 games: 78 goals and 97 assists over three seasons, 2011 to 2014.
- **Have other teams retired his number?** The USHL's Dubuque Fighting Saints retired his No. 8 in January 2025, the first number retired in that franchise's history. The Columbus Blue Jackets raised a No. 13 banner with his name at their 2024 home opener.

## What Boston College Announced

The release came from BC Athletics on Wednesday morning. The ceremony is a pregame event, so it happens before puck drop against UConn, not between periods.

"We are proud to honor Johnny Gaudreau by retiring his No. 13 at Boston College," said Blake James, BC's director of athletics, who called Gaudreau "one of the most accomplished players in our program's history."

The men's hockey account posted the news with a short "To the rafters. 2.12.27" teaser a few minutes earlier, then the full announcement, which it pinned to the top of its profile.

That is a short list for a program with five national championships. It is also a list of BC's Hobey Baker winners. Emma, who wore 16, won the school's first Hobey in 1991. Mottau, who wore 3, won the second in 2000. BC's release counts Gaudreau as the third.

One detail on how BC handles the honor: when Mottau's jersey was retired on November 27, 2016, before a game against Minnesota, BC's student paper The Heights reported that No. 3 would stay in rotation, and a current Eagle was wearing it at the time. BC's 2026 release now lists Mottau's 3 among the program's retired numbers. The announcement does not say whether anyone will be allowed to wear 13 after February.

One more number detail: No. 13 is not only Gaudreau's number at BC. When the Eagles honored Gaudreau, his brother Matthew and 2001 national champion Tony Voce in November 2024, they warmed up against Maine in jerseys that read Voce 13, Gaudreau 13 or Gaudreau 21. Voce, who died in the summer of 2024, also wore 13 at BC. Matthew wore 21.

## Johnny Gaudreau At Boston College

Gaudreau, from New Jersey, played three seasons at the Heights, from 2011 to 2014.

- **Freshman year, 2011-12:** 21 goals and 23 assists, one point behind Chris Kreider for the team lead. BC won the national championship, its third in five years.
- **Sophomore year, 2012-13:** 51 points, the most in college hockey, at 1.46 per game. His first Hockey East Player of the Year award and a spot as a Hobey Baker Hat Trick finalist.
- **Junior year, 2013-14:** 80 points, the third-best season in BC history and three points short of Craig Janney's school record of 83 from 1986-87. BC says no college player had scored more in a season since Peter Sejna's 82 for Colorado College in 2002-03. He won the Hobey Baker, his second Hockey East Player of the Year award, the Walter Brown Award and a second First Team All-America nod.

He left BC with one national title, three Beanpot trophies and 175 points in 119 games. The Calgary Flames flew him from the Hobey Baker ceremony to Vancouver, where he played his first NHL game on April 13, 2014, and scored on his first shot, the Flames' only goal of the night.

Nicknamed "Johnny Hockey," he played 11 NHL seasons with the Flames and the Columbus Blue Jackets: 763 games, 243 goals, 500 assists and 743 points, with seven All-Star selections. He also holds the United States records for career points (43) and assists (30) at the IIHF Men's World Championship. He came back to Boston College to finish his degree and graduated with the Class of 2021, keeping a promise to his parents.

Johnny and Matthew Gaudreau were killed on August 29, 2024, when they were struck by a driver while riding bicycles in Oldmans Township, New Jersey, where they had come home for their sister's wedding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When will Boston College retire Johnny Gaudreau's jersey?**

Friday, February 12, 2027, in a pregame ceremony before BC plays UConn at Kelley Rink in Conte Forum. The school announced the date on November 18, 2026.

**Who else has a retired number at Boston College hockey?**

David Emma (No. 16) and Mike Mottau (No. 3), per BC's announcement. Gaudreau's No. 13 makes three. All three won the Hobey Baker at BC: Emma in 1991, Mottau in 2000 and Gaudreau in 2014.

**What number did Johnny Gaudreau wear at Boston College?**

No. 13, the same number he wore for the Calgary Flames and the Columbus Blue Jackets.

**Did Johnny Gaudreau win the Hobey Baker?**

Yes, in 2014, as a junior. He scored 80 points that season and was BC's third Hobey Baker winner.

**Did Johnny Gaudreau win a national championship at BC?**

Yes. He was a freshman on BC's 2012 national championship team, scoring 44 points.

**How many points did Johnny Gaudreau score at Boston College?**

175 points in 119 games: 78 goals and 97 assists in three seasons.

**Did the Blue Jackets retire Gaudreau's number?**

At their home opener on October 15, 2024, the Blue Jackets raised a banner with his name and No. 13 into the rafters at Nationwide Arena, and both teams warmed up in Gaudreau 13 jerseys. Coverage of the ceremony described the banner but not formal retirement terms.

**What number did Dubuque retire for Gaudreau?**

No. 8, his number with the USHL's Dubuque Fighting Saints. It was the first number the franchise ever retired, in January 2025.

**Why did Cole Caufield change to No. 13?**

To honor Gaudreau. Caufield said in September 2024 that he had worn 13 earlier in his career because of Gaudreau and would wear it again for him.

**What was Johnny Gaudreau's nickname?**

"Johnny Hockey."

**What is the patch Boston College wore for Gaudreau?**

During the 2024-25 season BC wore a patch on every jersey with the initials of Tony Voce and Johnny and Matthew Gaudreau, all former Eagles who died in the summer of 2024.
