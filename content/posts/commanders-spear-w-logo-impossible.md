**The spear in the Commanders' new logo can't exist.** Look closely at the **Spear W**, the logo on the black **Hail Raiser** helmet Washington wears for the first time tonight against the Bengals on Monday Night Football. The spear runs straight across the W, and it passes **over** the first stroke, **under** the second, **over** the third and **under** the fourth. The team calls it an interweave. Through a flat letter, a straight spear can't weave. It would have to pass through the letter three times, and a straight line can only cross a flat surface once.

## Quick Answers

- **Is the Commanders' Spear W logo impossible?** Yes, as drawn. The spear goes over, under, over and under the four strokes of a flat W. A straight, rigid spear can't do that.
- **What does the team say?** The Commanders describe it as "the spear and the W interwoven at their center," a joining of past and present.
- **Where does the spear come out?** The back end sticks out on the left as a plain gold shaft with no feathers. On the right, the shaft stops short, and the spearhead floats just past it without touching.
- **Where is the logo?** On both sides of the black Hail Raiser helmet. It isn't on the burgundy helmet that goes with the white and burgundy jerseys.
- **When can I see it?** Tonight, Monday, November 23, 8:15 p.m. ET on ESPN, Commanders vs. Bengals in Landover, and again on December 20 against the Falcons.
- **Where does the spear come from?** Washington wore a spear on its helmets from 1965 to 1969, and brought that helmet back for its 70th season in 2002.
- **Does it matter?** No. It's still the best logo in Washington's new set.

## Follow The Spear, Step By Step

We traced the spear on the team's own logo graphic. Here it is with our notes added on top. The image underneath is the Commanders' reveal graphic, unchanged. The labels, lines and dots are ours.

The W is made of four slanted strokes, each one burgundy with a gold outline. The spear is a straight gold bar, the same gold as the outlines, running left to right across the middle. You can tell which piece is in front by looking at the burgundy. Where the gold bar cuts a burgundy stroke in two, the spear is in front. Where the burgundy runs straight through without a break, the stroke is in front and the spear is behind it.

1. **The back end.** The spear starts left of the W as a plain gold shaft. Its end is cut at a slant, and there are no feathers.
2. **Stroke 1, over.** The first stroke, the thin one that leans down to the right, is cut in two by the gold bar. The spear is in front.
3. **Stroke 2, under.** The second stroke, the thick one leaning the other way, is solid burgundy from top to bottom. The spear goes behind it.
4. **Stroke 3, over.** The third stroke, the other thin one, is cut in two again. The spear is back in front.
5. **Stroke 4, under.** The fourth stroke, the big one on the right, is unbroken. The spear is behind it again.
6. **Out the other side.** The shaft comes out of the W and ends square. Then there's a small black gap, and the spearhead sits by itself: two gold blades with a black notch between them, lined up with the shaft but not attached to it.

**Over, under, over, under.** It's a basket weave, and that's what the Commanders call it. "Interwoven" is the team's own word.

## Why A Real Spear Couldn't Do That

Picture the W as a flat sign and the spear as a straight pole. To pass in front of stroke 1 and then behind stroke 2, the pole has to cross through the sign once. To get back in front of stroke 3, it has to cross back. To get behind stroke 4, it crosses a third time. **A straight line can cross a flat surface only once.** One crossing gets you over-then-under. Over-under-over-under needs three.

Even one crossing would be tough here. At the spear's height, the four strokes touch each other with only gold outline between them, so the letter has no gap for the spear to pass through. It would have to break through the letter.

There are only two ways to make the drawing real, and both change the objects:

- **Bend the spear**, the way a thread weaves through cloth. Then it isn't a rigid spear.
- **Bend the W**, folding it like an accordion so the thin strokes sit further back than the thick ones. Then it isn't a flat letter.

The logo is a flat W on a helmet, so as drawn, it's impossible. It also looks deliberate. Our read: with the shaft in front of every stroke, it would look like a spear lying across a letter, and with the shaft behind every stroke, the letter would hide it. Weaving it makes the two look joined, which is what the team says it wanted.

The detached spearhead is the same kind of trick. On a real spear, the head is fixed to the shaft. Here the shaft ends, there's a gap, and the head continues on the same line. Your eye fills it in.

## Where The Spear Comes From

The Commanders call the Spear W "a joining of past and present." The past is the spear. Washington put a spear on the sides of its helmets in the mid-1960s, and most histories date it 1965 to 1969. ESPN described it as a "spear-and-feathers insignia," so the old spear came with feathers. The new one has none. Vince Lombardi's arrival ended it: in 1970 the helmet switched to a feathered "R" in a circle, which Lombardi pushed for because it resembled the Packers' G.

The spear came back once before. For the franchise's 70th season in 2002, Washington wore a reproduction of the 1965 to 1969 helmet with a special burgundy-over-gold home uniform. The 2026 version keeps the idea and drops the feathers, and the W replaces the rest of the old design. We couldn't find a freely licensed image of the original 1960s helmet, so it's described in words here.

## Other Sports Logos With A Trick In Them

Impossible drawings are rare in sports logos. Hidden letters are more common:

- **The Milwaukee Brewers' ball-in-glove** logo hides a lowercase "m" and "b" in the glove.
- **The Montreal Expos' logo** is built from a lowercase e, a big M and a b.
- **The Olympic rings** are a weave too, but it works. Each ring goes over one neighbor and under the next, and real rings can do that because each ring can tilt. A straight spear through a flat W can't.

## Our Take: Impossible, And Still The Best Logo They Have

It doesn't matter that it's impossible. Logos aren't engineering drawings, and the weave is what makes the spear and the W read as one piece. It ties the 1960s spear to the current W in one mark, and from the front, the two spearheads give the black helmet a look nobody else in the league has. That's why we graded the Hail Raiser an **A** and ranked it first in [every Commanders jersey ranked](/stories/commanders-jerseys-2026-ranked).

If we could change one thing, it would be the gap at the head. The weave is a choice. The head floating off the end of the shaft looks like a part that hasn't been attached yet. Tonight, under the lights, nobody will notice either one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Commanders' Spear W logo?**

Washington's alternate logo, revealed April 15, 2026: the burgundy and gold W with a gold spear running straight through it and a spearhead pointing forward. It's on both sides of the black Hail Raiser helmet.

**Why is there a spear in the Commanders logo?**

It refers to the spear Washington wore on its helmets from 1965 to 1969. The team calls the logo "a joining of past and present," and says the spearhead "symbolizes those leading the fight."

**Is the Commanders' Spear W logo physically possible?**

Not as drawn. The spear passes over the first stroke of the W, under the second, over the third and under the fourth. A straight spear would have to cross a flat letter three times to do that, and it can only cross once.

**How does the spear go through the W?**

Over, under, over, under. The thin strokes of the W are cut in two by the spear, so the spear is in front of them. The two thick strokes run unbroken, so the spear goes behind them.

**Does the spear touch the spearhead in the Commanders logo?**

No. The shaft comes out of the right side of the W and ends, and the spearhead starts just past it with a small gap between them.

**Does the Commanders spear have feathers?**

No. The back end of the new spear is a plain gold shaft. The 1960s helmet spear had feathers.

**What helmet are the Commanders wearing tonight?**

**Is the Spear W on the Commanders' regular helmet?**

No. The burgundy helmet worn with the white and burgundy jerseys has the primary W. The Spear W is only on the black Hail Raiser helmet.

**When did Washington have a spear on its helmet?**

From 1965 to 1969, before the feathered "R" arrived in 1970. Washington wore a reproduction of that helmet again in 2002 for its 70th season.
