The best hats New Era made this year are not the ones the whole league is wearing. They are the throwbacks.

Alongside the standard 2026 Sideline caps, 26 teams got a Historic version: same 9SEVENTY Stretch-Snap silhouette with the notched brim, same raised rubber logo, same sliver of color under the brim edge, but with an old logo on the front and, in several cases, an old color underneath it. As a group they grade a full half-letter higher than the primaries, a B+ against a B-, and the reason is simple. Old logos were drawn to be seen from a distance, and this treatment rewards exactly that.

Six teams do not get one at all: the Ravens, Panthers, Texans, Chiefs, Titans, and Commanders. Five of the six are either expansion-era franchises or teams that have rebranded away from what came before, which tells you something about who actually has a vault worth opening.

We graded all 26, worst to first.

## The Bottom Line

Put the two collections side by side and the gap is not subtle. The primary caps average a B-, the historics average a B+, and the top of this list is stronger than anything in the standard set. The Eagles and Vikings both earned A+ grades here. Not one primary cap did.

The reason is the same thing that sank half the primary ranking. New Era's 2026 template renders a logo as a flat raised shape, and old logos were built for exactly that world, drawn with heavy outlines and solid shapes because they had to read on a helmet from the upper deck of a stadium in 1968. Modern marks are full of gradients, thin strokes, and internal detail that a rubber patch simply cannot hold.

The teams that jumped furthest prove it. Tampa Bay went from our worst-graded primary to a top-three throwback by swapping a red-on-red flag for Bucco Bruce on creamsicle. New Orleans, Cincinnati, and the Giants all made similar leaps. Meanwhile the four teams whose historic cap graded lower than their primary, the Rams, Jets, Bears, and Jaguars, are all teams whose modern logo is already excellent or whose history is too short to raid.
