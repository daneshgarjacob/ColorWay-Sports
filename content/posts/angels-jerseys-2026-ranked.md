The Angels closet is smaller than most and better than people give it credit for, and the reason is that the best thing in it is a jersey the team only pulls out occasionally. Los Angeles owns three different white tops, which is unusual on its own, and they are not remotely equal: one of them is the sharpest uniform the franchise has worn in forty years, one is perfectly fine, and one is cream and finishes last. Here is every Los Angeles Angels jersey and uniform for 2026, ranked worst to first and graded.

Unveiled in 2022 and debuted that June against the Mets, the Surf City kit is the most place-specific jersey the Angels own. The base is a cream "beach sand" tone, and "Angels" runs across the chest in a script lifted straight from Southern California surf brands, with the tail of the "S" shaped like a surfboard fish tail. Two asymmetrical stripes borrowed from retro boards run down the left sleeve, the right sleeve carries a railroad roundel honoring the region's early rail lines, and the two-tone cap nods to SoCal skate culture. It anchors Friday home games at Angel Stadium.

It grades a B-, and finishing last here is not the insult it looks like, because this closet has no bad jerseys in it. The concept is genuinely local in a program full of clubs reaching for a theme they do not really have, and the surf script is a nice piece of lettering. The trouble is the cream. It drains the contrast out of a team whose entire visual identity is built on a bright, unmissable red, and next to the four uniforms below it this is the one that looks washed out on a broadcast. Good ideas, quiet execution, last place in a strong field.

The standard home white is the version of this uniform most people picture: a white base with "Angels" arched across the chest in red script trimmed in navy and silver, red numbers, and the red cap carrying the silver-haloed "A" the franchise has built its identity around since 2002. It is the tidy, modern, corporate-correct version of the look.

It grades a B. There is nothing wrong with it, and that is more or less the whole review. The halo cap is a great piece of design, the palette is clean, and it does everything a home white is supposed to do. It just does not have a single detail on it that makes you look twice, which is a real problem when the team owns another white jersey that is nothing but interesting details. Rank it against the rest of the league and it is a comfortable middle-of-the-pack home set. Rank it against the throwback sitting at the top of this list and it looks like the safe choice, because it is.

Here is the jersey that defines the modern Angels: a bright red top with "Angels" across the chest in white script outlined in navy, worn with the red halo cap and white pants. It is technically an alternate, but in practice it is the most-worn shirt the club owns, turning up at home and on the road, on weeknights and weekends, far more often than a typical team leans on its colored top.

It grades a B, same as the home white, and it takes the higher spot because it is the jersey that actually looks like this franchise. That red is the whole brand, the same shade as the cap and the halo, and putting it on the full uniform gives the Angels a look nobody in the American League West can be confused with. What keeps it from climbing higher is how hard the team leans on it. A colored alternate is supposed to be a change of pace, and when it becomes the default it stops feeling like an occasion. Great jersey, worn so often that it has quietly turned into the uniform.

The road gray carries "Los Angeles" across the chest and is the most traditional thing the Angels own. It spent part of 2026 looking like a jersey on its way out, which is exactly why it is worth talking about: the club has kept putting it on the field, Mike Trout wore it at the All-Star Game, and the photo above is from Hall of Fame Weekend in July, when Los Angeles paired it with a vintage "Los Angeles" cap for one of the best throwback lids anyone wore that weekend.

It grades a B+, the second-best jersey in the closet, and we will admit to some sentiment in that. A road gray with a city name arched across it in script is the oldest idea in baseball uniforms and it still works, especially for a team whose alternative is wearing red almost every night. The gray also does something none of the other four do, which is give the Angels a look that reads as a road uniform. Seeing it turn up less often has made it better, not worse. We would happily take more of it.

This is the best jersey the Angels own and it is not close. A white V-neck pullover with red and navy striping around the collar and the sleeve cuffs, "Angels" across the chest in the block-serif lettering of the California Angels era with a halo ringing the "A", red numbers below it, and the state of California on the sleeve inside a gold halo. Everything about it is a callback, and every piece of it is doing something.

It grades an A+. The V-neck pullover cut is the foundation, a silhouette almost nobody wears anymore, and it instantly separates this from the button-front sameness of the modern league. The collar and cuff striping gives the uniform trim in a place the current set leaves empty, the serif lettering has actual character where the modern script is merely neat, and the California sleeve patch ties the whole thing to a specific era instead of a generic vintage mood. It is the rare throwback that is not just nostalgia, it is genuinely a better-designed uniform than the one it is standing in for. The only bad thing about it is how rarely it comes out.

## The Bottom Line on the Angels Jersey Rankings

This is the tightest closet we have ranked. There is no D in here, nothing embarrassing, and the whole set fits inside a single grade and a half from top to bottom. The Angels do not have a bad uniform.

What they have instead is a ranking that runs almost exactly opposite to how often each jersey gets worn. The best thing in the closet is the throwback white, which shows up occasionally. Second is the road gray, which has been squeezed. The jersey the team reaches for most, the red alternate, sits third, and the plain home white is fourth precisely because it is plain.

The lesson is one the Angels could act on tomorrow. They already own the answer to their own uniform problem. The 1980s throwback is better than the modern home white in every way that matters, the road gray gives them a genuine second identity, and both of them are sitting in the closet while the red does the heavy lifting. Wear the good stuff more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Angels jersey for 2026?**

We rank the throwback white No. 1 with an A+. It is a V-neck pullover with red and navy collar and cuff striping, the California Angels era block-serif "Angels" lettering with a halo over the "A", and the state of California on the sleeve. It is the best-designed uniform the franchise owns, modern sets included.

**How many uniforms do the Angels have in 2026?**

Five: the home white, the road gray, the red "Angels" alternate, the Surf City City Connect, and the throwback white. Three of those five are white or cream tops, which is unusual for any club.

**What is the Angels City Connect called?**

The Surf City kit. It debuted in June 2022, uses a cream "beach sand" base with an "Angels" script borrowed from Southern California surf brands, retro surfboard striping on the left sleeve, and a railroad roundel on the right. It anchors Friday home games.

**Do the Angels still wear their road grays?**

Yes. The "Los Angeles" road gray has stayed on the field in 2026, Mike Trout wore it at the All-Star Game, and the club wore it through Hall of Fame Weekend in July paired with a vintage "Los Angeles" throwback cap.

**Why do the Angels wear red so often?**

Because red is the center of the brand, matching the cap and the halo, and the front office knows it photographs and sells better than the whites. The red alternate is the most-worn jersey in the closet by a wide margin, to the point that it functions as the team's everyday uniform rather than an alternate.
