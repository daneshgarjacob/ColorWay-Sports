**Turn a Penn State jersey around and there is nothing there but a number.** No name, no nickname, no logo on the helmet either. The Nittany Lions play Rutgers at Beaver Stadium on Saturday in the same plain navy home set they have worn all season, and the back of every jersey will be blank.

So why does Penn State have no names on its jerseys? The short answer is that it never did, for 125 years, and Joe Paterno turned that into a statement: the team matters more than any one player. Names did go on once, in 2012, for a specific reason. Three seasons later they came off again. Here is the full story, with the sources.

## Quick Answers

- **Why doesn't Penn State put names on its jerseys?** Tradition. The program went 125 years without them, and the plain look became a symbol of Joe Paterno's "team first" approach. When names came off again in 2015, the program said there was "only one name that truly matters, Penn State."
- **Has Penn State ever worn names?** Yes, once: from 2012 through 2014. Bill O'Brien added them in August 2012, after the NCAA sanctions, in recognition of the players who stayed.
- **When did the names come off?** July 2015. James Franklin announced it with the slogan "Black Shoes. Basic Blues. No Names. All Game."
- **Does Penn State have names on its jerseys in 2026?** No. The 2026 home and road sets have no nameplates, including the first season in Adidas uniforms.
- **Why is there no logo on the Penn State helmet?** Same idea. The helmet is plain white with one navy stripe and no logo.
- **Our take (opinion):** the blank back is the best thing about the uniform. Almost every team in the sport adds things. Penn State is famous for what it leaves off.

## The Look: Plain On Purpose

Penn State's uniform is close to the simplest in major college football:

- **Home:** navy jersey, white numbers, white pants.
- **Road:** white jersey, navy numbers, white pants.
- **Helmet:** white, one navy stripe down the middle, no logo and no number.
- **Back of the jersey:** a number and nothing else.

The colors themselves came almost by accident. Penn State's first colors in 1887 were pink and black, and they faded in the sun to white and dark blue, which became the school's colors in 1890. The look of the uniform has barely moved since the 1950s and 1960s. The Philadelphia Inquirer wrote in 2012 that the only major change in the Paterno years was the Nike swoosh, which arrived with Nike's deal in the 1990s.

## Reason One: 125 Years Of Tradition

Penn State did not invent the blank back to make a point. For most of college football history, nobody wore names. Numbers came first; names spread later, mostly in the television era. In baseball, the Chicago White Sox were the first team to put names on jerseys in 1960, and the AFL did it the same year. Penn State simply never followed.

When the program brought the blank backs back in 2015, its own announcement said Penn State had "proudly followed" the no-names approach for 125 years before 2012. That number, 125 years, is the program's own count.

## Reason Two: Joe Paterno And "Team People"

The tradition is older than Paterno, but he is the reason it means something. Paterno was Penn State's head coach for 46 seasons, from 1966 to 2011, and the plain uniform became the symbol of his program.

"We're conservative, and we like team people," Paterno said of the look, as quoted by The Philadelphia Inquirer. "I think people every once in a while need symbols to reinforce those sentiments."

According to the Inquirer, his model was the New York Yankees of the 1940s, the team he admired as a teenager in Brooklyn: pinstripes, polished black shoes, no names. The Yankees still have no names on their jerseys today, and we explain their side in [why the Yankees have no names on their jerseys](/stories/why-yankees-have-no-names-on-jerseys). Paterno's idea was that a player earns the uniform, and the uniform does not need to introduce him.

In August 2012, Penn State announced that players' last names would go on the backs of the jerseys for the first time, along with a blue ribbon honoring victims of child abuse. It came weeks after the NCAA sanctions that followed the Jerry Sandusky scandal, which included a bowl ban and allowed players to transfer without sitting out.

New coach Bill O'Brien had said in January that he would keep the uniforms the same. He changed course. The names were added, in the program's later words, "in recognition of the players' resolve and dedication" to the team and the university: the players who stayed when they could have left. O'Brien described them as "special, competitive kids."

Not everyone liked it. Former tight end Troy Drayton called it "a slap in the face" in an interview with the York Daily Record. Players on the 2012 team were more practical. The first announcement covered one season, but the names stayed on through 2013 and 2014. Penn State went 22-15 in those three years with names on the jerseys.

## Why The Names Came Off In 2015

On July 16, 2015, Penn State announced that names would come off the jerseys starting that season. James Franklin, then in his second year, brought former lettermen in to talk to the team about the tradition, and the program gave the change a four-part slogan: **"Black Shoes. Basic Blues. No Names. All Game."**

"There is only one name that truly matters, Penn State," Franklin said in the announcement. He also said the 2012 team "will hold an enduring place in our program's history" and that its commitment "will never be forgotten."

Senior lineman Angelo Mangiro, one of six players left from the 2012 team, said publicly that he understood the names at the time, that the moment had passed, and that he played for the name on the front of the jersey, not the one on the back.

## What Has Changed (And What Never Did)

The plain look is not frozen. A few things have come and gone:

- **Helmet numbers.** Penn State wore numbers on the sides of its helmets in parts of the 1950s, 1960s and 1970s. Sources disagree on the exact years, but they agree the numbers came off after 1974.
- **The Nike swoosh.** Added in the 1990s, the only big change of the Paterno years according to the Inquirer.
- **Generations Of Greatness.** Penn State's first throwback, unveiled in June 2017 and first worn on September 30, 2017, against Indiana. It brought the helmet numbers back for the day and has returned for later games, including against Illinois in 2021.
- **Adidas.** On July 1, 2026, Penn State moved from Nike, its outfitter since 1993, to Adidas. The new jerseys look almost exactly like the old ones. The chest logo changed. The back did not.

What never changed outside 2012 to 2014: no names, and no logo on the helmet.

## Our Take

The blank back is the best detail on the uniform, and Franklin was right to bring it back. The 2012 names were a fair gesture for a team that had been through something no team should, but three years was enough. What makes the Penn State uniform work is restraint. Almost every program in the sport adds a new alternate every fall. Penn State is known for what it leaves off, and in 2026, with a new coach and a new outfitter, it still leaves the names off. That is a brand.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does Penn State have no names on its jerseys?**

Tradition and a team-first message. Penn State went 125 years without names on its jerseys, and Joe Paterno made the plain look a symbol of putting the team ahead of any one player. When the names came off again in 2015, the program said "there is only one name that truly matters, Penn State."

**Has Penn State ever had names on its jerseys?**

Yes, from 2012 to 2014. Bill O'Brien added last names in August 2012, after the NCAA sanctions, to recognize the players who stayed with the program. They were the only seasons with names in Penn State history.

**When did Penn State take the names off the jerseys?**

Penn State announced it on July 16, 2015, before James Franklin's second season. The slogan was "Black Shoes. Basic Blues. No Names. All Game."

**Does Penn State have names on its jerseys in 2026?**

No. Penn State's 2026 navy home and white road jerseys have no nameplates, and that did not change with the move to Adidas.

**Why did Penn State add names in 2012?**

The program said the names were added "in recognition of the players' resolve and dedication" to the team and the university. After the NCAA sanctions in July 2012, Penn State players could transfer without sitting out, and the names honored the ones who stayed.

**Did Joe Paterno ever put names on the jerseys?**

No. Penn State never wore names in Paterno's 46 seasons as head coach. He described the program as "conservative" and said "we like team people."

**Why is there no logo on Penn State's helmet?**

It is the same philosophy as the blank jersey back. The helmet is plain white with one navy stripe. Penn State has put numbers on the helmet at times, including for its Generations Of Greatness throwback, but never a logo.

**What does "Black Shoes. Basic Blues. No Names. All Game." mean?**

It was the slogan Penn State used in 2015 when it took names off the jerseys. Each part describes the traditional plain uniform: black cleats, the simple navy and white jerseys, no names on the back, and a focus on the game rather than the individual.

**Did Penn State's record change with names on the jerseys?**

Penn State went 22-15 from 2012 to 2014, the three seasons with names. Those teams played under NCAA sanctions, including a bowl ban, so the record says more about the sanctions than the jerseys.

**What other teams have no names on their jerseys?**

The best-known example in pro sports is the New York Yankees, who have never worn names at home or on the road, and they were Paterno's model. We explain the Yankees' tradition in [why the Yankees have no names on their jerseys](/stories/why-yankees-have-no-names-on-jerseys).

**Is Penn State's uniform the plainest in college football?**

It is one of the plainest. White helmet, one stripe, no logo, no names, navy or white jersey and white pants. Very few major programs go that far.

**When did Penn State switch to Adidas?**

July 1, 2026. Penn State had been with Nike since 1993. The new Adidas football jerseys kept the classic look, and the visible change is the logo on the chest.
