**Navy football has the simplest uniform in college football for eleven games a year and the most elaborate one for the twelfth.** Every regular-season Saturday the Midshipmen wear navy blue and gold with a plain gold helmet. Then, for the Army-Navy Game, they wear something built from scratch around one piece of naval history: a frigate, a squadron, a submarine, a goat.

This is the full history: where the colors and the name came from, how the logos changed, what the helmet has looked like, and a year-by-year table of every special Army-Navy uniform Navy has worn since the tradition started in 2008.

## Quick Answers

- **What are Navy's colors?** Navy blue and gold. The Naval Academy adopted them officially in **1892**, and the **Class of 1890** was the first to use them, according to Navy Athletics. Before 1892, each class picked its own colors.
- **Why are they called the Midshipmen?** Every student at the Naval Academy holds the rank of midshipman, so the teams are the Midshipmen, or the Mids. Navy Athletics says "Middie" is not correct.
- **Why is the mascot a goat?** Navy Athletics dates the first recorded goat mascot to **1893**, an animal named El Cid, and says goats have served without interruption since **1904**. The name Bill is the one that stuck.
- **What does Navy wear in a normal game?** Navy blue at home with gold numerals and gold pants, white on the road, and a gold helmet with no logo on the side.
- **What is Navy wearing against Army in 2026?** It has not been revealed as of November 11. The 127th Army-Navy Game is **Saturday, December 12** at MetLife Stadium. Navy's reveal came on November 18 last year.

## The Colors: Navy Blue And Gold Since 1892

Navy Athletics puts it plainly: the Academy adopted navy blue and gold as its colors in 1892, and the Class of 1890 was the first to use them. Until then the colors belonged to individual classes, which changed every year. The 1892 decision made them permanent.

No official source we could find explains why those two colors were chosen. The obvious reading, navy blue for the service and gold for the braid on an officer's uniform, shows up on fan and logo sites, but none of them cite a record for it, so we are not presenting it as fact.

The 1926 team, Navy's only national champion, wore those colors in a design that is now the most famous sweater in program history: **chevron stripes** across the chest and arms.

## The Name And The Goat

The Midshipmen name is not a nickname anyone invented. It is the rank every Naval Academy student holds, so the football team, like every Navy team, is simply named after the people on it.

The goat is older than the colors' official adoption. A plaque in Annapolis says midshipmen brought a goat to the first Army-Navy Game in 1890. Navy Athletics' own record starts in **1893**, with a goat named **El Cid**, and says goats have served without interruption since **1904**. The Naval Academy says the name Bill came from a pet goat kept by Commander Colby M. Chester, who was Commandant of Midshipmen in the early 1890s. The sources disagree on which goat came first, so we are giving you all three.

Bill has worn the uniform, too. Navy's 2018 Army-Navy Game uniform put the goat on the right side of the helmet, with the player's number on the left, and on the gloves and cleats.

## The Logos: The N-Star, The Anchor And Bill

Navy uses three marks again and again, and each one means something specific.

**The N-Star.** This is the one most people miss. Navy varsity athletes wear a gold "N." When their team beats Army in a designated "Star" competition, it becomes an **N-Star**, a gold N with a star beside it. Midshipmen say it out loud as "N star." When Navy puts an N with a star on a jersey, as it did on the 2018 shoulder above, that is what it is pointing to: beating Army.

**The anchor.** The anchor has been on Navy helmets on and off for decades. A Navy helmet with an anchor logo appears in 1960 photos, and the anchor came back on special uniforms in 2011, 2012 and 2016.

**Bill the Goat.** The goat logo has been drawn many ways, most famously as a half-body goat wearing a sweater. It is the mark fans know best, and it is the one Navy chose when it built a whole uniform around the mascot in 2018.

The everyday helmet, the one Navy wears every week, has none of them. It is plain gold with a navy facemask and NAVY across the front bumper.

## The Helmet

The gold helmet is the constant. It is on the 2004 team below, it is on the 2016 throwback, and it is on every regular-season game today. Navy dressed it up for special games: an orange helmet appeared for the 1961 Army game, and a "100" decal marked college football's 100th anniversary in 1969, according to helmet historians.

## Every Navy Army-Navy Game Uniform Since 2008

Special Army-Navy uniforms started in **2008**, when both academies wore custom designs. **Under Armour** became Navy's outfitter in **2014**, and every Navy rivalry uniform since has been a Naval Academy and Under Armour design. Army has stayed with Nike.

## Our Take

Navy's everyday uniform is right where it should be: gold helmet, navy and gold, no clutter. It is a **B+** look that gets out of the way.

The Army-Navy uniforms are where Navy has been better than almost anyone in college football at building a uniform around a real story. Two stand out for us: the **2017 Blue Angels** set, where the color itself is the story, and the **2021 naval aviation** helmet, a painted Super Hornet across a silver shell that is the best single helmet either academy has worn. Every year since 2013 has had one clear idea behind it.

## FAQs

**What year did Navy adopt navy blue and gold?**

1892. Navy Athletics says the Academy adopted navy blue and gold that year, and the Class of 1890 was the first to use the colors. Before that each class chose its own.

**Why is Navy called the Midshipmen?**

Because every Naval Academy student is a midshipman, a rank. The teams are the Midshipmen or the Mids.

**Why is Navy's mascot a goat?**

Navy Athletics dates the first recorded goat mascot to 1893 and says goats have served continuously since 1904. A plaque in Annapolis says a goat was at the first Army-Navy Game in 1890.

**Where did the name Bill the Goat come from?**

The Naval Academy says it came from a pet goat kept by Commander Colby M. Chester, Commandant of Midshipmen in the early 1890s.

**What is the N-Star?**

A Navy varsity letter earned by beating Army. Navy athletes wear a gold N, and a win over Army in a designated "Star" competition adds a gold star beside it.

**Does Navy have a logo on its helmet?**

Not on the everyday helmet. It is plain gold with NAVY on the front bumper. Anchors, the goat, ships, aircraft and squadron insignia have all appeared on special Army-Navy helmets.

**When did Navy start wearing special uniforms for the Army-Navy Game?**

In 2008. Both academies wore custom uniforms that year, and the tradition has grown every year since.

**Who makes Navy's uniforms?**

Under Armour, since 2014. Army's are made by Nike.

**What did Navy wear in the 2025 Army-Navy Game?**

A uniform for the Navy's 250th anniversary built around USS Constitution: a copper helmet with a USN monogram, six ropes on the sleeves and collar for the original six frigates tied with 250 knots, and 126 knots on the pants for the 126th game.

**What is Navy wearing in the 2026 Army-Navy Game?**

Navy has not revealed it as of November 11. Last year's reveal came on November 18. The game is Saturday, December 12 at MetLife Stadium.

**What did Navy wear in 1926?**

Navy blue and gold sweaters with chevron stripes. Navy brought the chevrons back for its 2026 centennial uniform, with nine stripes for the nine wins of 1926.

**Which Navy Army-Navy uniforms stand out?**

For us, the 2017 Blue Angels uniform and the 2021 naval aviation helmet with its hand-painted F/A-18 Super Hornet.
