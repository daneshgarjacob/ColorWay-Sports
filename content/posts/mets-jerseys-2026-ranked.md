The New York Mets carry five uniforms in 2026, and unlike the buttoned-up two-piece closet up the road in the Bronx, the Mets actually use theirs. Pinstripes, road grays, a royal blue alternate, the black set that fans spent a decade begging to get back, and a City Connect that tries to bottle the entire subway map. It is one of the deeper wardrobes in the National League, and it gives us real jerseys to argue about.

So we ranked all five, worst to best, and graded each one. These grades are our take on the full package, cap to socks, not just the shirt. Here is every Mets jersey in 2026, counted down to the best.

## 5. City Connect, the "NYC" Subway Kit

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Mets/status/2065853193603485730"></a></blockquote>

The City Connect is the most conceptually loaded jersey the Mets own, and it is our least favorite. The base is concrete gray for the city's concrete jungle, the pinstripes are built from circles and diamonds that echo the MTA's local and express subway symbols, and "NYC" arcs across the chest in black capitals so the Mets can claim the whole city rather than just Queens. Purple threads through the details for the 7 line that runs to Citi Field, and the cap even hides a subway map inside.

The concept is genuinely clever, and we respect how much of the city is packed into it. But that is also the problem: it is busy, and busy is the opposite of what makes the best Mets looks work. There is a lot to explain and not a lot to just enjoy, and when the same closet holds a black alternate this good, the gray subway kit is the one we would leave on the hook. A C, and the concept is doing most of the lifting to get it there.

## 4. Blue Alternate

  <span style="font-size: 0.85em; text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: 0.1em; font-weight: 600; color: #ffffff;">ColorWay Sports Grade</span>
  <div style="font-size: 3em; font-weight: 800; line-height: 1.2; color: #ffffff;">B-</div>
</div>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Mets/status/2068787760761041364"></a></blockquote>

The blue alternate is the closet's wild card, and it is a case of loving the color more than the design. It is a royal blue top with orange striping at the collar and cuffs and a cursive "New York" script pulled from the 1987 set, the number tucked underneath. That royal blue is a great color on the Mets, easily the brightest, boldest thing they own, and there is no fixed day attached to it, so it feels like an event whenever it shows up, usually on the road.

Where it loses us is the execution. The revived cursive script is fine, but it does not have the punch of the "Mets" wordmark on the black or the clean pinstripe script at home, and the whole thing can tip from bold into a little loud depending on the pants and the light. We like reaching for it. We just do not love the way it is put together. A B-.

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Mets/status/2068425792166437282"></a></blockquote>

The road gray is where the Mets' colors get to do quiet work. Gray shirt, the blue "New York" script arced across the chest, orange trim tracing the collar and cuffs. We like the colors here a lot, the blue-and-orange on gray is a genuinely nice combination, and it never gets in its own way.

It grades out at a B because it is good without being special. This is a road gray doing exactly what a road gray is built to do: stay clean, let the home team lead, and get out of the way. There is nothing wrong with it, and the blue-orange trim keeps it from ever looking anonymous, but it is not a jersey anyone is buying a ticket to see. Solid, likable, and firmly in the middle of the closet.

## 2. Home White Pinstripes

<div style="margin: 2em 0; padding: 1.5em; border-radius: 12px; background: linear-gradient(135deg, #eef0f3, #c9d2df); color: white; text-align: center;">

  <div style="font-size: 3em; font-weight: 800; line-height: 1.2; color: #002D72;">A</div>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Mets/status/2069499941429354640"></a></blockquote>

The white pinstripe is the Mets' signature, and it is very hard to argue with. A clean white shirt laced with blue pinstripes, the blue "Mets" script angled gently up across the chest, orange trim, and the blue cap with the interlocking orange NY. It is one of the most recognizable home uniforms in the National League, and the Mets are smart to leave it almost entirely alone. Timeless does not need tinkering.

So why an A and not the top spot? Because we like it a lot without quite loving it. It is the correct, classic, can't-mess-it-up look, and it will always be a strong uniform, but it does not give us the jolt the black alternate does. Think of it as the dependable A student of the closet: excellent every single day, just not the one we circle on the calendar. A clean, clear A.

## 1. Black Alternate

  <span style="font-size: 0.85em; text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: 0.1em; font-weight: 600; color: #FF5910;">ColorWay Sports Grade</span>

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet" data-dnt="true"><a href="https://twitter.com/Mets/status/2065839072023609557"></a></blockquote>

This is the one. The black alternate is our favorite Mets jersey, full stop, and it is not particularly close. There is something timeless and effortlessly cool about the Mets in black, the blue-and-orange "Mets" wordmark popping harder against the dark base than it does anywhere else in the closet, and the black cap finishing the whole thing off perfectly. When they wear black top to cap, the Mets look as good as any team in baseball.

It earns the A+ because it does what only the best uniforms do: it makes you want to see it. The black-trim era ran from 1998 to 2011 before the Mets shelved it, and fans spent years asking for it back. Now it owns Friday nights at Citi Field, and every time it comes out it feels like an event. Timeless, cool, and pulled off perfectly with the black hats, this is the crown jewel of the Mets' closet and the clear number one.

## The Bottom Line on the Mets Jersey Rankings

The Mets have one of the most fun closets in baseball, and this one was not especially hard to top. The black alternate is the crown jewel, an A+ that is timeless, cool, and finished off perfectly by the black caps, and nothing else in the closet gives us the same jolt. The home pinstripes are the reliable A right behind it, classic and correct every single day.

From there it slopes off honestly: the road gray is a likable B, the blue alternate a B- we reach for more for the color than the design, and the "NYC" City Connect a C whose clever subway concept can't outrun how busy it is. Five uniforms, one true star, and a very clear answer to which Mets jersey we would wear first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Mets jersey?**

The black alternate, which we grade an A+. It is timeless, the blue-and-orange "Mets" wordmark pops against the black, and it is finished off perfectly by the black caps. It is worn on Friday home games at Citi Field and is the clear number one in the closet.

**How many jerseys do the Mets have in 2026?**

Five: the home white pinstripes, the road grays, the royal blue alternate, the black alternate that owns Friday home games, and the gray "NYC" City Connect worn on Saturday home games.

**Did the Mets bring back the black jerseys?**

Yes. The black alternate, worn during the 1998 to 2011 black-trim era and then shelved, returned as a weekly alternate and now anchors Friday home games at Citi Field. It carries a cleaner two-color "Mets" wordmark than the original, and it is the highest-graded jersey in our ranking.

**What is the worst-ranked Mets jersey?**

We ranked the "NYC" City Connect last with a C. The concrete-gray subway-map concept is genuinely clever, but it is the busiest jersey the Mets own, which keeps it out of the top four.
