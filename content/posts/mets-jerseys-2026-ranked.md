The New York Mets carry five uniforms in 2026, and unlike the buttoned-up two-piece closet up the road in the Bronx, the Mets actually use theirs. Pinstripes, road grays, a royal blue alternate, the black Friday-night set that fans spent a decade begging to get back, and a City Connect that tries to bottle the entire subway map. It is one of the deeper wardrobes in the National League, and it gives us real jerseys to argue about.

So we ranked all five, worst to best, and graded each one. A quick note before we start: these grades are our take on the full package, cap to socks, not just the shirt. Here is every Mets jersey in 2026, counted down to the best.

Somebody has to finish last, and on a roster of jerseys this deep it lands on the road gray, which is not an insult so much as a job description. Gray shirt, blue "New York" script arced across the chest, the orange and blue trim doing quiet work at the collar and cuffs. It is clean, it is correct, and it never once gets in the way of the game.

The reason it sits at a B rather than higher is that it is the most anonymous thing the Mets own. Strip the script off and you could hang it in half the road closets in baseball. That is by design, and there is nothing wrong with a road gray that behaves, but when the same team also owns a black nostalgia bomb and a screaming blue alternate, "behaves" is what gets you fifth. It does its job. Its job just is not to be memorable.

The blue alternate is the wild card of the set, and we mean that as a compliment. It is a royal blue top with orange striping at the collar and cuffs and a cursive "New York" script pulled straight from the 1987 design, the number tucked underneath. It is the brightest, boldest thing in the closet, and the Mets are smart enough not to schedule it. It shows up when the staff wants a jolt of color, usually on the road, and the lack of a fixed slot is exactly what keeps it feeling like an event.

What holds it at a B+ is that the blue can tip from bold into loud depending on the pants and the light, and the script, as fun as it is, is a revival rather than a signature. But this is the jersey that reminds you the Mets are a blue and orange team first, and on a night when the pinstripes would feel sleepy, it delivers. A great changeup. Not the pitch you build the staff around.

The City Connect is the most conceptually loaded jersey the Mets own, and it is the hardest to grade because it is trying to do so much. The base is concrete gray for the city's concrete jungle, the pinstripes are built from circles and diamonds that echo the MTA's local and express subway symbols, and "NYC" arcs across the chest in black capitals so the Mets can claim the whole city rather than just Queens. Purple threads through the details for the 7 line that runs to Citi Field, and the cap hides a subway map inside.

We land it at a B+, right in the middle, and that is a real compliment for a City Connect. Most of them are gimmicks. This one has a point of view, and every strange choice on it actually means something if you know the city. What keeps it out of the top two is simply that it is busy, and busy is the opposite of what makes the best Mets looks work. It is one of the more thoughtful entries in the entire City Connect program. It is also the jersey you have to explain, and the two above it explain themselves.

Black is back, and it might be the best story in the Mets' closet. The black alternate carries the blue-and-orange "Mets" wordmark, cleaned up to a sharper two-color graphic when it returned, and it has taken over Friday nights at Citi Field. The black-trim era ran from 1998 to 2011 before the Mets shelved it, and bringing it back as a weekly alternate tapped straight into a wave of nostalgia that never really went away. Fans spent years asking for this. The team finally listened.

It gets an A- because it is genuinely great and because the emotion is earned, not manufactured. The black makes the orange and blue pop harder than they do anywhere else in the set, and giving it a standing Friday slot is the rare bit of scheduling discipline on an otherwise loose rotation. The only thing keeping it out of the top spot is that it is, at its heart, a nostalgia play, and the jersey above it is not chasing a feeling from twenty years ago. It is the feeling. Under the lights on a Friday, though, this is the best the Mets look all week.

The white pinstripe is the Mets' signature and the jersey every other look in the closet is measured against. A clean white shirt laced with blue pinstripes, the blue "Mets" script angled gently up across the chest, orange trim, and the blue cap with the interlocking orange NY. It is one of the most recognizable home uniforms in the National League, and the smartest thing the Mets do with it is almost nothing. Timeless does not need tinkering.

We hold the top grade at A- rather than a perfect score because the pinstripes win by being safe, and we save the ceiling for a jersey doing something a little more daring. That is not a knock. It is the entire point. When the black alternate wants to make you feel something and the City Connect wants to make you think, the pinstripes just show up and look exactly like the Mets are supposed to look, ten years ago and ten years from now. Every other jersey here is judged against this one, and that is why it wins.

## The Bottom Line on the Mets Jersey Rankings

The Mets have one of the most fun closets in baseball, and the top of it comes down to a fight between history and nostalgia. The home pinstripes win because they are the standard, an A- that never has to try. The black alternate pushes them hard at the same grade because it carries a decade of fan longing and pays it off every Friday night. Below them, the City Connect earns real credit for having a point of view, the blue alternate is the changeup that keeps the whole thing loose, and the road gray does the quiet work every closet needs.

Five uniforms, five looks worth talking about, and not a dud in the bunch. That is more than most teams can say, and it is why the Mets are one of the best watches in the league on any given night.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many jerseys do the Mets have in 2026?**

Five: the home white pinstripes, the road grays, the royal blue alternate, the black alternate that owns Friday home games, and the gray "NYC" City Connect worn on Saturday home games.

**What is the best Mets jersey?**

We grade the home white pinstripes the best at A-, just ahead of the black alternate at the same grade. The pinstripes are the Mets' timeless signature, while the black alternate is the beloved nostalgia look reserved for Friday nights.

**Did the Mets bring back the black jerseys?**

Yes. The black alternate, worn during the 1998 to 2011 black-trim era and then shelved, returned as a weekly alternate and now anchors Friday home games at Citi Field. It carries a cleaner two-color "Mets" wordmark than the original.

**Do the Mets have a City Connect?**

Yes. The "NYC" City Connect, unveiled in April 2024, is a concrete-gray kit with subway-symbol pinstripes, purple 7-line accents, and "NYC" across the chest. The Mets wear it on Saturday home games.
