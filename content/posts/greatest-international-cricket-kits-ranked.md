Cricket is one of the most-watched sports on earth, and yet its visual identity is split in two. Test cricket is played in pristine whites, timeless but deliberately uniform. The personality lives in the colored kits: the one-day international (ODI) and T20 jerseys where each nation's flag, history, and fanbase get poured into a single color. It is the same reason [national soccer teams wear the colors they do](/stories/why-national-teams-wear-their-colors), and cricket takes it just as seriously.

This is ColorWay's lens on the global game. We are not ranking batting averages. We are ranking the shirts: the color stories, the crests, and which national uniforms hit hardest in a stadium of 90,000 fans. Here are the ten greatest international cricket kits of all time, counted down to No. 1. One of the youngest kits on this list and one of the cleanest. Afghanistan's bright royal blue with red-and-green flag accents is a modern, optimistic look that has become a genuine fan favorite as the team's rise continues. Simple, saturated, and instantly readable.

## 9. England (ODI Blue)

England's limited-overs blue is a study in restraint: pale and navy blues with a red detail nodding to the cross of St George. It can read corporate in its weaker years, but the best versions are crisp and classic, and the 2019 World Cup-winning shirt earned its place in history.

Sri Lanka's royal blue with a flash of golden yellow is one of the warmest looks in the sport, pulled straight from the lion flag. At its best, think the late-90s and 2000s, it is vivid and unmistakable, a tropical blue that pops against any ground.

## 7. Bangladesh

The Tigers wear one of cricket's boldest combinations: deep bottle green with red, mirroring the national flag. It is a high-contrast, high-energy kit that has only gotten sharper, and the tiger-stripe detailing on recent jerseys is exactly the kind of identity flourish we love.

Pakistan's green is heritage itself, the color of the flag and of the legendary 1992 World Cup-winning side. The shamrock-tinged kelly greens of the modern era are gorgeous, and when Pakistan leans all the way into a single saturated green with a clean crescent, few uniforms feel more national.

New Zealand's blackout look with a silver fern is one of the most intimidating and most coherent identities in world sport, with every national team across codes singing the same note. Decades of dominance baked it into the sport's visual memory, and it has barely needed to change because it never needed to.

It is understated and unforgettable at the same time, the rare jersey that feels like a flag for a whole region.

## 1. India (Bleed Blue)

Nothing in cricket carries the visual weight of India in blue. "Bleed Blue" is not a slogan, it is a national mood: a sky-to-royal blue worn by the most-watched cricket team on the planet in front of the most passionate crowds in the sport. The tricolor accents, the saturated sea of blue in the stands matching the eleven on the field. It is the most powerful color story cricket has, and it is not particularly close.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why are Test cricket kits all white?**

Test cricket is the sport's oldest format and is played in traditional whites, sometimes called cream flannels. The colored kits ranked here are from one-day internationals (ODIs) and T20s, the limited-overs formats where each nation expresses its identity through color.

**What is "bleed blue" in cricket?**

"Bleed Blue" is the unofficial rallying cry of India's cricket fans, referring to the team's iconic blue limited-overs jersey. It captures the idea that supporting India runs deeper than fandom, because the blue is part of the national identity.

**Which cricket nation has the most recognizable kit color?**

It is a close call between Australia's canary yellow, the West Indies' maroon, and India's blue. Australia's yellow is the most eye-catching on screen, but India's blue reaches the largest audience in the sport, which is why it tops our ranking.

**What is the best cricket kit of all time?**

Our pick is India's blue limited-overs kit, ahead of West Indies maroon at No. 2 and Australia's canary yellow at No. 3. All three are instantly recognizable national uniforms with decades of history behind them.
