Miami has set the date for its Dark Water Rivalries uniform: a Week 17 home game against the Buffalo Bills on **Sunday, January 3, at 1:00 PM ET on CBS** at Hard Rock Stadium. It is the team's one Rivalries appearance of the year, and the graphic looks great.

We still do not like the jersey. Not a little. Let us explain why, because this is not a lazy "black alternates are played out" take. It is that this specific blackout is wrong for this specific team.

## Miami Has No Black in Its Palette

Start with the most basic problem. The Dolphins are aqua, orange, and white. That is the entire identity, and it is one of the best color combinations in the league precisely because nobody else owns it. There is no black anywhere in the real uniform. So when the team blacks the whole thing out, even under the official "pitch blue" label, it is not turning up its own identity. It is borrowing a color it does not have and burying the two colors it does.

Look at what happens to the aqua. On the home whites, that aqua is the brightest, happiest thing on an NFL field. On a near-black base it becomes an accent, a trim color, a glow around the edges of the numbers. The best thing Miami owns gets demoted to piping. A uniform should make a team's signature color louder, not quieter, and this one does the opposite.

## Monochrome Black Is a Costume, Not an Identity

We are not fans of full-monochrome blackouts in general, and this is the cleanest example of why. When a team with no black in its colors puts on an all-black set, it stops looking like itself and starts looking like every other team that reached for the same easy move. Blackout uniforms photograph as "cool" for one night and read as anonymous by the second quarter. Half the league has done one. There is nothing in a pitch-blue Dolphins set that says Miami except the logos you have to squint to find.

The Rivalries program was supposed to be the opposite of anonymous. It was pitched as uniforms rooted in a specific place and a specific rivalry. A blackout is the least place-specific thing a uniform can do. The "305" on the bumper and the sunset-orange wordmark are trying to drag some Miami back into it, and we will give the design team real credit for the execution, but they are decorating a concept that was pointed the wrong way from the start.

## And Miami Plays in the Sun

Here is the part that pushes it from "not our taste" to "actually wrong for this franchise." The Dolphins are a warm-weather team that wears white at home for a reason. For decades Miami used its home whites as a genuine competitive edge, forcing visiting teams to bake in dark jerseys under the South Florida sun while the Dolphins stayed cool in white. White at home in Miami is not just tradition, it is climate-smart, and it is part of the team's whole sunny, tropical, aqua-and-orange character.

An all-dark uniform throws that away. It fights the light instead of using it, it works against the exact identity that makes the Dolphins one of the best-looking teams in football, and it does it in the one market where a blackout makes the least sense. Sunshine, aqua, and orange is who they are. Pitch black is who they are not.

## The Bottom Line

Credit where it is due: the Dark Water set is well built, the aqua-and-orange accents are handled cleanly, and as a one-off spectacle for a night game it will get plenty of jersey sales. The matchup is right too, since the Bills are the rival worth circling. But a good execution of a bad idea is still a bad idea. A warm-weather team with no black in its colors, one whose entire brand is sun and white and bright water, going full monochrome black is the wrong look for Miami, and no amount of clean detailing fixes the premise. We grade it a **C+**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When will the Dolphins wear the Dark Water uniform in 2026?**

Once, on Sunday, January 3, at home against the Buffalo Bills. It is a Week 17 game at Hard Rock Stadium with a 1:00 PM ET kickoff on CBS.

**What is the Dolphins Dark Water uniform?**

It is Miami's Rivalries alternate from the Nike and NFL Rivalries program. The base is a near-black shade called pitch blue, with a blacked-out dolphin on the helmet, the "305" area code on the helmet bumper, aqua numbers, and an orange "MIAMI" wordmark and nameplate.

**Is the Dolphins Dark Water uniform any good?**

We grade it a C+. The execution is clean, but the concept is wrong for Miami: the Dolphins have no black in their palette, a monochrome blackout buries their aqua and orange, and a warm-weather team that has always used white at home has no real reason to go all-dark.

**Why do the Dolphins wear white at home?**

Tradition and climate. Miami has long used its home whites to make visiting teams wear dark jerseys in the South Florida heat, turning the weather into a competitive advantage. It is a big reason an all-black alternate feels off-brand for this franchise.

**Who do the Dolphins play in Week 17?**

The Buffalo Bills, at home, on January 3. It is a division game against Miami's biggest AFC East rival.
