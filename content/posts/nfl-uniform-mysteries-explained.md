Every NFL fan has stared at a helmet and wondered why. Why is the Steelers logo only on the right side? Why is the Browns helmet blank? Why do the Cowboys wear white at home when almost everyone else wears color?

We went looking for the real answers. For each one we used the team's own history, the Pro Football Hall of Fame, a museum, a court record or a major news outlet, and we name the source in every section. Some of the famous stories hold up. A few don't, and we say so.

This is about helmets, logos and colors. If you want to know where the team names came from, that's in [how every NFL team got its name](/stories/how-every-nfl-team-got-its-name). The colors themselves are in [how every NFL team got its colors](/stories/how-every-nfl-team-got-its-colors).

## Quick Answers: NFL Uniform Mysteries

- **Steelers logo on one side:** a 1962 test. The team only put the logo on one side to see how it looked, won, and never added the other side.
- **Browns no helmet logo:** no team logo has appeared on the shell in a game since 1946, per the team. The "CB" helmet logo you may have seen came from NFL ad drawings, not the field.
- **Bengals stripes:** Paul Brown said the old helmet looked like Cleveland's from a distance.
- **Cowboys white at home:** Tex Schramm wanted Dallas fans to see the visiting teams' colors. The "blue jersey curse" came later.
- **Packers G and Georgia G:** Green Bay had it first, in 1961. Georgia asked permission in 1964.
- **Eagles wings:** the coach liked the Rams' horns and asked a Rawlings salesman for something similar.
- **Rams horns:** a halfback with an art background painted them by hand in 1948.
- **Chiefs arrowhead:** Lamar Hunt was inspired by the 49ers' helmet.
- **Vikings horns:** real Vikings didn't wear them, and even the Vikings' own helmet horn has two families claiming credit.
- **Ravens' first logo:** a security guard drew it, a jury agreed, and the team changed logos.
- **Seahawks logo:** a real Kwakwaka'wakw eagle mask, now in a museum in Maine.
- **Bears GSH:** George S. Halas, the founder, on the left sleeve.

## Pittsburgh Steelers: Why Is the Logo Only on One Side of the Helmet?

Because it started as a test, and the test went well.

In 1962, Cleveland's Republic Steel suggested the Steelers use the Steelmark, the steel industry's logo, on their helmets. That's according to the American Iron and Steel Institute (AISI), which owns the Steelmark. The team had to petition AISI to change the word "Steel" inside the logo to "Steelers."

The Steelers tell the rest. Owner Art Rooney Sr. wasn't sure the logo would look good on a solid gold helmet, so equipment manager Jackie Hart was told to put it on just one side as a visual test, Steelers.com's Bob Labriola wrote in 2024. AISI says that side was the right side. The 1962 team went 9-5 and made the Playoff Bowl. For that game, Rooney switched the team to black helmets to mark the season, and the logo popped against the black. They liked the look, and it became permanent.

The three shapes in the logo are hypocycloids, not diamonds. AISI says the colors stand for the materials used to make steel: yellow for coal, orange for iron ore and blue for steel scrap. We covered more of that story in [hidden details in sports logos](/stories/hidden-details-sports-logos).

**Myth check:** you'll sometimes read that fans voted to keep one side blank. We found no team or AISI source for that. The team's own answer is the test. [Pittsburgh Steelers uniform schedule 2026](/stories/steelers-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Cleveland Browns: Why Is There No Logo on the Browns Helmet?

Because the Browns have never put one there, and the plain orange shell became the logo.

The team's history tells it this way. The first Browns, in 1946, wore plain white leather helmets. Orange helmets first appeared in 1950. In the late 1950s the Browns put player numbers on the sides, then took them off. What they have never done is put a team logo on the shell. ClevelandBrowns.com's 2015 myth-busting piece by Andrew Gribble says there are no photos showing a logo on Browns helmets since the team began play.

The stripe has done the work instead. A 1981 Christian Science Monitor story noted that the stripe down the middle was the only thing on the Browns helmet, and that the stripe alone kept it from being the league's one completely plain shell. Brown, orange and white striping is still the whole design on the orange shell.

**Myth busted: the "CB" helmet logo.** You may have seen a Browns helmet with "CB" on the side, dated 1965. The Browns say there is no photo of it on the field. The logo came from advertising drawings the NFL commissioned, and merchandise makers ran with it. [Cleveland Browns uniform schedule 2026](/stories/browns-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Cincinnati Bengals: Why Do the Bengals Have Stripes on Their Helmets?

Because their helmet looked too much like the Browns'.

The same man built both teams. Paul Brown coached the Browns, was fired in Cleveland, and then started the Bengals, who began play in 1968. The first Bengals helmet was plain orange with "BENGALS" in block letters on the side. Brown, then the team's general manager, wanted a helmet as recognizable as the Colts' horseshoe or the Chargers' lightning bolt, the Bengals say. His problem with the old one, in his words: "When you were far away, it looked like Cleveland's helmet."

The fix arrived in 1981. The Bengals replaced the lettering with six black tiger stripes running across the orange shell, and put the same tiger pattern on the jersey shoulders and down the pants, according to the team's uniform history. The Christian Science Monitor reported in April 1981 that Brown also said the league had pushed teams toward more distinctive uniforms.

The stripes have stayed on the helmet through every Bengals redesign since. [Cincinnati Bengals uniform schedule 2026](/stories/bengals-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Dallas Cowboys: Why Do the Cowboys Wear White at Home?

Because Tex Schramm wanted Dallas fans to see color.

In the 1960s, NFL home teams usually wore their dark jerseys and visitors wore white. Schramm, the Cowboys' longtime president, flipped it. The Cowboys' own site says he wanted home fans to see the different uniforms of teams from around the league, instead of every opponent showing up in white.

There's a second, more practical theory. Many early Dallas games were played in the heat of the Cotton Bowl, and a white jersey is cooler than a dark one. DallasCowboys.com describes that as a belief rather than a documented reason, so we treat it as a possible bonus, not the answer.

**Myth busted: the "blue jersey curse."** A lot of fans think Dallas wears white because the blue jerseys are cursed. The order is backwards. The white-at-home tradition came first, in the 1960s. The curse talk grew later, after big losses in blue, including Super Bowl V against the Colts, and some hosts were said to wear white just to put Dallas in blue. Schramm reportedly said he didn't believe in it. In 2026, all three of the Cowboys' navy jersey games are on the road. [Dallas Cowboys uniform schedule 2026](/stories/cowboys-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Green Bay Packers and Georgia: Why Do They Have the Same G?

They don't, quite. But it's close, and Green Bay had it first.

The Packers' G debuted on Aug. 5, 1961, at the team's intra-squad scrimmage at old City Stadium, Packers team historian Cliff Christl wrote on Packers.com in 2015. Vince Lombardi wanted a G in the shape of a football. Equipment manager Gerald "Dad" Braisher asked his assistant, John Gordon, to sketch it. The Packers and the Packers Hall of Fame credit the artwork to Gordon.

Georgia's G came three years later. When Vince Dooley took over as Georgia's coach in 1964, assistant coach John Donaldson volunteered his wife, Anne, who had a degree in commercial art from Georgia, to help redesign the helmet. Her G is a little different in shape. Because it looked so much like Green Bay's, Georgia cleared it with the Packers first, the Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported, citing the school's own history. In the school's telling, athletic director Joel Eaves made the call. Georgia has never paid the Packers for it, school spokesman Claude Felton told the AJC.

**Myth busted: the G stands for "greatness."** It stands for Green Bay. Christl traced the "greatness" line to Tiki Barber at Super Bowl XLV media day, as we covered in [more hidden details in sports logos](/stories/hidden-details-sports-logos-part-2). **Also busted:** Georgia didn't steal it. They asked. [Green Bay Packers uniform schedule 2026](/stories/packers-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Philadelphia Eagles: Why Are There Wings on the Eagles Helmet?

Because the Eagles' coach got beat by the Rams and liked their helmets.

Designer Todd Radom, who has researched the wings, tells the story. Jim Trimble was entering his third season as Philadelphia's coach in 1954 and wanted a helmet logo after a loss to the Rams. "I really liked the Rams' helmets with the horns," Trimble said in 1998. He asked Rawlings salesman Kenny Baldwin if the Eagles could get something similar, and general manager Vince McNally signed off.

The first wings, in 1954, were silver on green. The colors have flipped several times since. In 1969 the silver went away, and for a few seasons the Eagles wore white helmets on the road. By 1981 it was Kelly green helmets with silver wings outlined in white, which lasted through 1995. In 1996 the wings got bulkier and the green went darker, to midnight green. You can read more from Radom in [our interview with him](/stories/todd-radom-interview-sports-logo-design). [Philadelphia Eagles uniform schedule 2026](/stories/eagles-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Los Angeles Rams: Why Do the Rams Have Horns on Their Helmets?

Because one of their players painted them.

Fred Gehrke, a Rams halfback who had majored in art at the University of Utah, painted a helmet dark blue with yellow ram horns in 1948. Coach Bob Snyder took the idea to owner Dan Reeves, who approved it. Gehrke took 75 helmets home that summer and painted them all, at $1 a helmet, according to Todd Radom's research. Radom also found a Los Angeles Times clipping showing the Rams first wore the horns at an intra-squad scrimmage at Gilmore Stadium on Aug. 25, 1948.

The Rams were the first pro team with a helmet logo, Radom notes, and within about six years the Eagles and Colts followed. In 1972 the Pro Football Hall of Fame gave Gehrke its first Daniel F. Reeves Pioneer Award. So in a way, every NFL helmet logo on this list goes back to one Rams halfback with a paintbrush. [Los Angeles Rams uniform schedule 2026](/stories/rams-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Kansas City Chiefs: Where Did the Arrowhead Logo Come From?

From the 49ers' helmet.

Lamar Hunt founded the team in 1960 as the Dallas Texans and moved it to Kansas City in 1963, where it became the Chiefs. "Inspired by the look of the San Francisco 49ers helmets, Hunt created the arrowhead logo with the intertwined letters K and C," KCUR, Kansas City's public radio station, reported in 2025. You can see it: the 49ers' helmet logo is an interlocking SF inside an oval, and the Chiefs' is an interlocking KC inside an arrowhead.

**Myth check: the napkin.** You will often read that Hunt sketched the arrowhead on a napkin on a flight home. It's a good story, and it might be true, but we couldn't find a primary source for it, like a Hunt interview or team archive. We'd call it unconfirmed. ## Minnesota Vikings: Did Vikings Really Wear Horned Helmets?

No. And who drew the Vikings' horn is its own mystery.

Start with history. The Gjermundbu helmet, found on a farm in Norway in the 1940s, is one of the few complete Viking Age helmets ever found, and it has no horns. The horned helmets that do exist in Scandinavia, like the pair found in a Danish bog in 1942, are Bronze Age objects from around 900 B.C., about 2,000 years before the Vikings, Smithsonian magazine reported in 2022. The horned Viking look took off in the 1800s, and historians widely credit costume designer Carl Emil Doepler, who put horns on the singers in the first Bayreuth production of Wagner's Ring cycle in 1876.

Now the football part. Minnesota has worn horns on a purple helmet since its first season, 1961, and the horn on the side of the helmet has been the team's look ever since. The real Vikings never wore them, but nobody in Minnesota is giving them back. [Minnesota Vikings uniform schedule 2026](/stories/vikings-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Baltimore Ravens: Why Did the Ravens Change Their Logo After Three Seasons?

Because a security guard drew it first, and a jury agreed.

The Ravens' first logo, used from 1996 to 1998, was a winged shield with a B, often called the "Flying B." Frederick Bouchat, an amateur artist working as a security guard at a state office building in Baltimore, drew a similar shield design in 1995 as a proposed Ravens logo and faxed it to the Maryland Stadium Authority in April 1996. His drawing reached the people designing the logo. These facts are laid out in the federal appeals court opinions in the case.

Bouchat sued in 1997. In November 1998 a jury found that the Flying B infringed his copyright. The Ravens and the NFL switched to the raven-head logo they still use, starting with the 1999 season. Bouchat never got a payday: a later jury awarded him nothing in damages. Courts later allowed some historical uses of the old logo, such as in NFL videos and team exhibits, as fair use. ## Seattle Seahawks: What Is the Seahawks Logo Based On?

A real mask. It's a Kwakwaka'wakw transformation mask that depicts an eagle, made on the northeast side of Vancouver Island.

Robin K. Wright, curator of Native American art at Seattle's Burke Museum, traced it in 2014. Her colleague Bill Holm found the mask pictured in Robert Bruce Inverarity's 1950 book "Art of the Northwest Coast Indians." When the mask is closed, it's a bird. When it opens during a dance, it reveals a human face. The Seahawks later found a 1975 Seattle Post-Intelligencer story in their archives in which general manager John Thompson named the mask as the inspiration, the Burke says.

The mask itself turned up at the University of Maine's Hudson Museum. It was loaned to the Burke and shown in Seattle from November 2014 to July 2015, then went back to Maine. **Myth check:** the mask isn't a seahawk, and it isn't from the Seattle area. It's an eagle, made by Kwakwaka'wakw artists on Vancouver Island. [Seattle Seahawks uniform schedule 2026](/stories/seahawks-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Chicago Bears: What Does GSH on the Bears Jersey Mean?

George S. Halas. He founded the team, played for it, coached it and owned it, from 1920 until he died on Oct. 31, 1983.

The Bears wore a memorial patch for him during the 1983 season, the team says. His initials, GSH, have stayed on the left sleeve of the jersey since, a permanent tribute, as NBC Chicago has reported. In 2025 the Bears used a near-identical patch design to honor his daughter, longtime owner Virginia Halas McCaskey. [Chicago Bears uniform schedule 2026](/stories/bears-uniform-schedule-2026)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is the Steelers logo only on one side of the helmet?**

It started as a test in 1962. Owner Art Rooney Sr. wasn't sure the logo looked good on a gold helmet, so equipment manager Jackie Hart put it on one side only. The team had a winning season, switched to black helmets for its Playoff Bowl game, and kept the one-sided look.

**Which side of the helmet is the Steelers logo on?**

The right side, according to the American Iron and Steel Institute, which owns the Steelmark.

**What do the colors in the Steelers logo mean?**

The steel industry says yellow is for coal, orange is for iron ore and blue is for steel scrap.

**Why don't the Browns have a logo on their helmet?**

They never have. The Browns wore plain white helmets from 1946 to 1949, switched to orange in 1950 and have never worn a team logo on the helmet in a game, according to the team. The team says there are no photos of any logo on a Browns helmet.

**Did the Browns ever wear a CB logo on their helmets?**

No. The Browns say the "CB" helmet logo came from NFL-commissioned advertising drawings around 1965, and there's no photo of it on the field.

**Did the Browns ever have numbers on their helmets?**

Yes. In the late 1950s the Browns put player numbers on the sides of their helmets, then removed them.

**Why do the Bengals have stripes on their helmet?**

Paul Brown said the plain orange helmet with "BENGALS" on it looked like Cleveland's helmet from far away. The tiger stripes arrived in 1981.

**Who founded the Bengals and the Browns?**

Paul Brown. The Browns are named after him, and he founded the Bengals after Cleveland fired him.

**Why do the Cowboys wear white at home?**

Tex Schramm started it in the 1960s so Dallas fans could see the visiting teams' colors. Some also think the white jerseys helped in the Texas heat.

**Is the Cowboys' blue jersey cursed?**

That's fan lore. White at home came first. The curse idea grew after Dallas lost big games in blue, including Super Bowl V. Schramm said he didn't believe in it.

**Do the Cowboys ever wear blue at home?**

Rarely. In 2026, all three of their navy jersey games are road games. Check our [Cowboys uniform schedule](/stories/cowboys-uniform-schedule-2026) for every game.

**What does the G on the Packers helmet stand for?**

Green Bay. The "greatness" story is a myth.

**Who designed the Packers G logo?**

John Gordon, an assistant to equipment manager Gerald "Dad" Braisher, drew it for Vince Lombardi in 1961.

**Did Georgia copy the Packers' G?**

Georgia's G came in 1964, three years after Green Bay's. Georgia asked the Packers for permission first and got it. The designs are slightly different.

**Who designed the Georgia G?**

Anne Donaldson, wife of Georgia assistant coach John Donaldson, who had a commercial art degree from Georgia.

**Does Georgia pay the Packers for the G?**

No, a Georgia athletics spokesman told the Atlanta Journal-Constitution.

**Why do the Eagles have wings on their helmets?**

Coach Jim Trimble liked the Rams' horned helmets and asked a Rawlings salesman for something like it. The wings first appeared in 1954.

**Who painted the first NFL helmet logo?**

Rams halfback Fred Gehrke, a former art major, who painted horns on the Rams' helmets in 1948. He painted 75 helmets at $1 each.

**Were the Rams the first team with a helmet logo?**

Yes. The Rams' horns, from 1948, were the first logo on a pro football helmet. The Eagles' wings and the Colts' horseshoe came about six years later.

**Where did the Chiefs arrowhead logo come from?**

Lamar Hunt created it after the team moved to Kansas City in 1963, inspired by the 49ers' helmet logo, an interlocking SF in an oval.

**Did Lamar Hunt draw the Chiefs logo on a napkin?**

That story is often told, but we couldn't find a primary source for it.

**Did Vikings actually wear horned helmets?**

No. The few Viking Age helmets that survive, like the Gjermundbu helmet from Norway, have no horns. The horned look came mostly from 1800s art and an 1876 Wagner opera production.

**Why did the Ravens change their logo?**

A jury found in 1998 that the original "Flying B" logo infringed on a drawing by Frederick Bouchat, a Baltimore security guard. The Ravens switched to the raven-head logo in 1999.

**Did the Ravens pay for the old logo?**

No. A jury awarded Bouchat no damages.

**What is the Seahawks logo based on?**

A Kwakwaka'wakw transformation mask that depicts an eagle, pictured in a 1950 book. The real mask is at the University of Maine's Hudson Museum.

**Is the Seahawks logo a seahawk?**

The mask it's based on depicts an eagle.

**What does GSH on the Bears jersey mean?**

George S. Halas, the Bears' founder, who died in 1983. His initials are on the left sleeve.

**Which NFL team has no logo on its helmet?**

The Cleveland Browns.

**Which NFL team has a logo on only one side of the helmet?**

The Pittsburgh Steelers.

## The Bottom Line

Most NFL uniform quirks aren't grand design plans. They're a test that worked, a coach who liked another team's helmet, an owner who wanted his fans to see color, or a player with a paintbrush. The stories that fall apart are usually the ones that sound too perfect: the curse, the "greatness" G, the fan vote. The real ones are better anyway.
