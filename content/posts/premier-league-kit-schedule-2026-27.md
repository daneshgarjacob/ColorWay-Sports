Wondering what kit your club is wearing this weekend? Here is the thing most fans do not know: **the Premier League itself decides, and it tells both clubs no later than three working days before kickoff.** Kits are not a matchday surprise. They are scheduled.

This page is that schedule. Every fixture, every matchweek, who wears what, and why the away side sometimes has to go to a third kit. We update it as each matchweek is confirmed.

## Matchweek 2: Every Kit Matchup

Ten fixtures from Friday night to Monday night. The Premier League confirms kit colours to both clubs no later than three working days before kickoff, so the Friday fixture is the first to lock and the Monday one is the last. Six are played, four kick off today, and every one of them is below as the shirts themselves rather than a line of text.

We fill each fixture in as the club itself confirms it, not when a preview graphic guesses. That distinction has mattered before: graphics circulating ahead of matchweek 1 had two of these fixtures wrong.

### Today: What Every Club Is Wearing, Sunday August 30

Four fixtures and two forced changes. Chelsea's royal blue keeps Brighton out of the blue home pinstripe and into the inverted white away. At the Stadium of Light both of Fulham's usual options fail at once, the white home against Sunderland's white stripes and the red and black away against Sunderland red, so the sky blue third gets its first league outing of the season. Leeds against Brentford is the quieter change, and Old Trafford needs no intervention at all.

The trap here is that red and white stripes read as a striped shirt to a supporter and as a half-white shirt to a television camera. Against Leeds' all white that is too close, so Brentford travel in the navy with cream pinstripes, the Savile Row shirt, which is both the correct call and the best-looking one available to them. Leeds wear the horizontal pinstripe, a design the club has never worn before.

## How Premier League Kit Clashes Actually Get Decided

This is the part almost nobody explains properly, so here it is straight.

**The home club has priority.** Premier League rules put the home side's outfield players first: they wear their home strip. Second priority goes to the visiting club's outfield players. Goalkeepers are sorted last, which is why keeper kits end up in colors nobody would choose.

**Only the away club has to declare.** The visiting side tells the league which kit it intends to wear. The home club is not part of that process at all.

**The league decides disputes, not the clubs.** Where there is a disagreement, the Premier League, in consultation with PGMOL on behalf of the referee, determines the colors and notifies both clubs and the match officials. The rule is a contrast standard: the strips must be different enough that officials, spectators and television viewers can clearly tell the teams apart.

**The deadline is three working days before kickoff.** That is the single most useful fact on this page. By Wednesday, the kits for a Saturday fixture are settled and communicated. Clubs sit on the announcement for engagement, but the decision is already made.

**Third kits exist for one reason.** If the away club's second kit also clashes with the home shirt, they go to the third. That is the entire purpose of a third kit, and it is why a third shirt that is too close in color to either of the other two is a design failure rather than a style choice.

**Referees can still override late.** If officials look at the two kits at the ground and decide the contrast is not good enough, they can order a change on the spot, including to a third kit nobody expected.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What kit is my Premier League club wearing this weekend?**

Check the matchweek grid above. Home clubs wear their home shirt by default, and the away club changes only if the league rules there is a clash. Kits are settled three working days before kickoff, so by Wednesday the answer for a Saturday match already exists.

**Who decides which kit a Premier League team wears?**

The Premier League, in consultation with PGMOL acting for the referee. The home club has first priority and wears its home strip. The away club declares which kit it intends to wear, and the league resolves any dispute and notifies both clubs and the officials no later than three working days before the match.

**Why do away teams sometimes wear their third kit?**

Because their second kit clashes with the home side's shirt too. The third kit exists for exactly this case, which is why a third shirt should be visually distinct from both of the club's other kits.

**Can a Premier League club wear its home kit away from home?**

Yes, and it happens constantly. If there is no clash, the visiting club can wear its home shirt. Liverpool in red at Newcastle and Chelsea in blue at Fulham are both normal.

**Which Premier League teams are new for 2026/27?**

Coventry City, Hull City and Ipswich Town. Coventry are back in the top flight for the first time since 2001, Hull for the first time in a decade, and Ipswich after one season in the Championship.

**How many Premier League clubs wear red at home?**

Seven: Arsenal, Bournemouth, Brentford, Liverpool, Manchester United, Nottingham Forest and Sunderland all use red as a primary home color, with Bournemouth, Brentford and Sunderland wearing it in stripes. Seven of twenty is the main reason change kits come out so often.

## The Bottom Line

Premier League kits are not decided on matchday and they are not decided by the clubs. The league settles them three working days out, the home side gets first call, and the away side changes only when the contrast fails. This page tracks every one of those decisions, matchweek by matchweek, starting with an opening weekend where Crystal Palace turned up at Everton in their own home shirt because they have swapped their colours, Tottenham split their away shirt and their home socks to clear a contrast check, and Manchester United went past their away kit entirely and opened the season in an ivory third.
