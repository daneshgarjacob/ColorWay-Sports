The short answer is that the home team's equipment manager pulls the jersey, but the call gets made by committee. The coaching staff weighs in on superstition and momentum. The marketing department weighs in on giveaways, sponsor activations, and minimum-wear quotas the team owes the league. The general manager and the head of basketball operations get the final sign-off. The road team then matches the home pick with a contrasting uniform. The whole package gets submitted to the league for approval and pushed to the broadcast partners before tipoff. Here is how the NBA playoff jersey selection process works at every step, from the league rules through who actually pulls the uniform off the rack.

## The NBA's Playoff Uniform Rule

The home team picks first. The home team historically wears white or light, and the road team wears their primary color. Teams can reverse it. The home team can opt to wear a colored jersey at home, which forces the road team to flip into their Association whites or a light alternate. Either way, the home team makes the pick first and the road team adapts.

In the 2026 playoffs that has produced everything from perfect home-in-whites versus road-in-color matchups to the Boston Celtics putting on the Statement Edition black at home for an elimination night to the Dallas Stars running their alternate over the iconic Victory Green primary. The rule does not force a primary color. The rule only forces contrast. ## Step 1: The Equipment Manager Pulls From the Wardrobe

The equipment manager is the person who selects the jersey, sizes it, packs it for travel, and lays it out in the locker room before the game. Every team has the same standard wardrobe heading into the playoffs. Association whites. Icon primary color. Statement alternate. City Edition. Sometimes a Classic Edition throwback. Sometimes a fan-vote alternate or a partnership uniform.

Equipment managers know which uniforms perform on which courts, which ones travel well, and which ones the players prefer. The big-picture pick is rarely theirs alone, but they are the person who physically makes it happen and the person the head coach asks first.

## Step 2: The Coaching Staff and Superstition

Coaching staffs care about superstition more than fans realize. Win streaks in a specific uniform drive playoff wear decisions. If a team won Game 1 in the Icon primary, they will probably run it back for Game 2. If they lost Game 4 in a black alternate, they often flip to whites for Game 5 just to break the look. Coaches want their guys feeling good, and clothes are part of that.

The head coach also has the final say on whether the team wears a road alternate. Most road alternates only show up once or twice per playoff series. The coach can lean in or veto.

## Step 3: Marketing Quotas and Minimum-Wear Obligations

Every NBA team commits to a minimum number of wears per uniform across the regular season and playoffs combined. The City Edition usually carries the heaviest minimum because it is tied to a year-long marketing campaign and a sponsor activation. The Statement Edition is next. Classic Edition throwbacks and special-edition partnership uniforms are usually quotaed too.

Teams that did not hit their City Edition wear count in the regular season have to make it up in the playoffs. That is part of why fans see a City Edition pop up in the postseason that looks completely off-script for a high-stakes game. The team owes the wear. The marketing department reminds the equipment manager, and the City Edition gets pulled out of the bag.

The Los Angeles Lakers running a different home jersey for every home game in their 2026 first-round series against the Houston Rockets is the cleanest case study of the marketing pressure right now. Icon gold for Game 1 on the primary court, City Edition black for Game 2 on the alternate court, Statement purple for Game 5 back on the primary court. Three home games, three different uniforms, every Lakers minimum-wear bucket getting touched in a single playoff series at Crypto.com Arena. The basketball logic does not drive that pattern. The minimum-wear math drives that pattern.

## Step 4: Crowd Giveaways Are a Separate Marketing Decision

The crowd t-shirt and rally towel giveaway is a separate decision from the jersey, and the two do not always coordinate. Some teams sync the giveaway color with the home uniform for a fully coordinated arena look. The Atlanta Hawks gave out red shirts at State Farm Arena the same night they wore the Statement red at home. The Houston Rockets gave out red All Fire shirts the same night they wore Icon red. The Orlando Magic flipped from a Statement black home jersey to the Icon Edition blue specifically because they had a blue t-shirt giveaway scheduled at Kia Center for Game 4, which is the cleanest example of the marketing department driving the jersey pick rather than the other way around.

Plenty of other teams skip the coordination entirely. The Philadelphia 76ers gave out black towels at Wells Fargo Center while playing in their Icon royal blue. The Phoenix Suns gave out orange towels at Footprint Center while wearing the Statement black. The San Antonio Spurs ran black Puro San Anto Loud shirts for Game 5 in their home Association whites. The Knicks handed out white New York Forever towels at Madison Square Garden while wearing the Statement black. The marketing piece and the jersey piece are connected, but they are run by different parts of the front office. ## Step 5: Player Input and the Star Factor

Star players have weight. Not always at the macro level, but enough that their preferences get heard. A franchise face who wants to wear the Icon at home for a closeout night is usually going to get the Icon at home for a closeout night. The equipment manager talks to the captains, the coaching staff confirms, and the look gets locked in.

Some players are superstitious about specific uniforms. A jersey they wore for a career night, a road set with a winning record, a Classic Edition throwback that hit on a national broadcast. Equipment managers track that, players ask for it, and the call gets made.

## Step 6: The Road Team Matches

Once the home team submits the pick, the road team matches with a contrasting jersey and uniform. If the home team is in white, the road team wears a primary color. If the home team is in a colored alternate, the road team flips to their Association whites or a light alternate.

The road team has an easier job. Their main lever is which color to wear, not whether to be light or dark. A road team in red versus a road team in white at the same arena is the difference between a clean broadcast read and a flat one.

## Step 7: League Approval and the Broadcast Hand-off

The full uniform package gets submitted to the league office for approval. The NBA reviews each matchup against the contrast rule, the minimum-wear quotas, the broadcast clarity standards, and any league-wide initiatives. Once approved, it goes out to the broadcast partners so ESPN, NBC, ABC, Prime Video, and any international rights holders can prepare their on-air graphics and uniform overlays.

This is also where the league issues the official matchup graphic. Teams typically post their uniform reveal on social within an hour of clearance.

## Why You See "Wrong" Jerseys at Home

Every playoff round produces a few uniform decisions that fans and design writers question. A black alternate at home for an elimination game. A Statement at home instead of the obvious Icon. A City Edition for the third game in a row when Association whites are the clean pick.

Most of those calls are driven by the minimum-wear quota or by coaching superstition rather than by aesthetics. The equipment manager is not making the look bad on purpose. The team is balancing several pressures at once, and the ones fans see most clearly are basketball superstition and contractual wear obligations.

## Frequently Asked Questions About NBA Playoff Jerseys

**Who picks the jersey first, the home team or the road team?**

The home team picks first. The road team then chooses a contrasting color from their wardrobe. The NBA's contrast rule is the only hard requirement.

**Can the home team wear a colored jersey instead of white?**

Yes. The NBA changed the home and road uniform rule in 2017 so home teams can wear any uniform from their wardrobe as long as the road team can show contrast. Teams now use this every playoff round to wear City Editions, Statement Editions, and Icon primaries at home.

**How far in advance are NBA playoff jerseys decided?**

Most teams submit the uniform package 24 to 48 hours before the game. The league office and broadcast partners need the lead time to prepare matchup graphics, lower-third overlays, and uniform reveal posts. Teams typically post their own uniform reveal on social within an hour of league clearance.

**Can a team change jerseys last minute?**

Technically yes, but it is rare. A last-minute change requires the league office to approve a new contrast pairing, the broadcast partner to swap their uniform graphics, and the road team to confirm they can still meet the contrast requirement with what they brought to the arena. Equipment managers usually pack at least two jersey options per game just in case, but the submitted package almost always holds.

**Do NBA players have a say in which jersey the team wears?**

Yes, especially star players. Equipment managers consult with team captains about superstition, comfort, and momentum from previous games. Players who wore a specific uniform for a career night will often ask for it back. The head coach has the final veto.

**Why do teams wear City Edition jerseys in the playoffs?**

Most City Edition uniforms have a contractual minimum-wear count tied to sponsor activations and the league's marketing program. Teams that did not hit their City Edition wear count during the regular season often make up the difference in the playoffs, which is why a team will sometimes pull a City Edition in a closeout game when Association whites are the obvious pick.

**Who actually pulls the playoff jersey off the rack?**

The team's equipment manager. They are the person responsible for the uniform decision physically, even though the call is made by committee. They size it, pack it, lay it out in the locker room, and travel two sets to every road game in case the league rejects the contrast.

**Do crowd giveaway shirts always match the home jersey?**

No. Some teams coordinate the giveaway color with the home uniform for a unified arena look, like the Atlanta Hawks giving out red shirts on a red Statement night or the Orlando Magic flipping to Icon blue for a blue t-shirt giveaway. Plenty of teams run the giveaway and the jersey on separate tracks, like the Philadelphia 76ers handing out black towels while wearing the blue Icon, or the Phoenix Suns giving out orange towels while wearing the Statement black.

## The Bottom Line on Who Chooses NBA Playoff Jerseys

The home team's equipment manager is the person who pulls the jersey and uniform. The head coach has veto power. The marketing department drives the minimum-wear quotas. Star players get a voice. The road team matches the home submission. The league office approves the matchup. The broadcast partners get the package before tipoff. The crowd giveaway is a separate decision that may or may not coordinate with the jersey, depending on how the team's marketing department is run.

Every playoff jersey decision is a small committee decision dressed up as a fashion choice. The next time you see a Statement black at home for a closeout game and wonder who thought that was the right look, the answer is usually "five different people, and the math added up to that one."
