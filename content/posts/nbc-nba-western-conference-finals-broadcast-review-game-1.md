NBC owns the 2026 NBA Western Conference Finals under the league's new national broadcast deal. Game 1 between the San Antonio Spurs and Oklahoma City Thunder at Paycom Center was the network's first crack at carrying a Conference Finals game, and the broadcast did not feel like a Conference Finals broadcast at all. The scorebug is boring. The graphics package treats the round like a regular season Sunday Night Football game with NBA team logos dropped in. The Conference Finals identity is buried in tiny gray subtext below the scores. The whole night felt like a network that did not bother to make this special. ESPN has been setting the standard for how to brand a Conference Finals broadcast and NBC did not get within shouting distance. Even TNT's scorebug (which we have never loved) made the playoff round feel bigger than NBC managed to. **We graded NBC's broadcast a C.** Mike Tirico in the booth and the Roundball Rock theme are the only two reasons this is not lower.

## The Announcer Booth — The Strongest Part

Mike Tirico anchored the play-by-play and was the best part of the broadcast. Tirico has been one of the most consistent voices in sports television for two decades and he carried the rhythm of Game 1 from tipoff through the closing minutes with the kind of calm authority that gives a Conference Finals broadcast its weight. He never tries to manufacture moments. He lets the game come to him and calls it cleanly. NBC making him the lead NBA voice for the new rights deal is the right call.

Reggie Miller and Jamal Crawford split color duties. Miller's analysis was sharp on the Spurs offensive sets and the Thunder defensive switches, but he leaned too far into nostalgia at moments where current-game analysis would have served better. Crawford was effective in the pre-game and post-game, less so during live play where his cadence sometimes overlapped with Tirico's calls. The three-person booth is a difficult balance and the chemistry is not fully there yet. ESPN's Mike Breen, Doris Burke, and Richard Jefferson booth has years of reps. NBC is starting from scratch and it shows.

## The Scorebug — Boring and Built for a Random Tuesday Night

This is the part of the broadcast that bothered us the most. The NBC scorebug for the Western Conference Finals is one of the most generic scorebugs we have seen on a Conference Finals broadcast in years. The team marks are there. The scores are there. The shot clock is there. Conference Finals identity is buried in a tiny gray "WEST FINALS - GAME 1" subtext below the scores that you have to squint to read on a broadcast TV. That is not how a Conference Finals scorebug should work. The Conference Finals identity should be the loudest thing on the bug, not a footnote.

Now compare NBC's effort against what ESPN and TNT have been doing.

That is the hierarchy. ESPN has spent years building a Conference Finals scorebug that puts the playoff identity front and center. TNT, even though we have never loved their scorebug design, at least made the round feel like a round. NBC took the same broadcast moment, on the same round of the same playoffs, and put "West Finals - Game 1" in tiny gray subtext below the scores. The Conference Finals branding is an afterthought. The broadcast feels generic because the broadcast graphics treat it as generic. The standard ESPN set is the standard NBC should have hit and we hope ESPN delivers the same treatment for the [Eastern Conference Finals Cavs-Knicks broadcast tonight](/stories/nba-eastern-conference-finals-game-1-knicks-cavs-preview).

The shot clock placement is the second gripe. The clock sits below the score in a font that is too small for the playoff broadcast scale. ESPN's playoff scorebug puts the shot clock in a contrasting color block that pops on the screen. TNT's old scorebug had a distinct color treatment for the clock. NBC's just blends in. Small details and they add up.

NBC's top-right ticker carries the same energy problem.

Nowhere does the broadcast graphics package tell the viewer "this is the Western Conference Finals" in the kind of bold, branded way that the round demands. The "GAME 1" pill is fine. The lockup of the NBA logo with the NBC Peacock is fine. The Conference Finals identity is missing.

## Court Branding — The Biggest Miss

Here is what kills us: there is no "Western Conference Finals" branding on the Paycom Center court. None on the floor. None on the basket stanchion. None on the digital overlay. The NBA brought back the Finals logo on the court for the 2026 NBA Finals (a real win that the league should be commended for), but the Conference Finals courts are running the same regular season floor design with zero playoff signage. NBC is broadcasting one of the four most important games in the entire league calendar and the court looks like a midseason Thursday night game.

We covered this in detail in our [NBA Conference Finals court branding gap](/stories/nba-conference-finals-court-branding-no-logo) breakdown, but the short version is that the NBA needs to extend the on-court playoff branding back to the Conference Finals the way every other major American sports league brands its postseason. The MLB Championship Series has the LCS logo on the field. The NFL Conference Championships have the AFC/NFC logo on the field. The NHL Eastern and Western Conference Finals get the conference logos on the ice. The NBA Conference Finals get nothing.

NBC cannot fix this on its own — the court branding is the league's call. But NBC's graphics team should have leaned harder into supplementing the missing court branding with on-air bumpers, lower-third treatments, and broadcast overlays that establish the Conference Finals identity. They did not. The broadcast looked the way the court looked: clean, polished, and missing the moment.

## The Halftime and Studio Show

NBC's NBA studio show with Maria Taylor as host and Vince Carter, Carmelo Anthony, and Reggie Miller (when not in the booth) as analysts had a quiet halftime. The set design is sleek and the production is competent. The energy is not where it needs to be for a Conference Finals broadcast. TNT's Inside the NBA spent twenty-five years setting the bar for how to do a halftime show in the NBA postseason and that bar is brutally high. Inside the NBA moved to ESPN ahead of this season, which means TNT's NBA coverage is gone and ESPN is now running both lead play-by-play (Mike Breen) and the best studio show in basketball (Shaq, Charles Barkley, Kenny Smith, Ernie Johnson). NBC has to build their studio identity from zero in their first NBA playoff cycle and the gap is wide.

Vince Carter and Carmelo Anthony are both Hall of Fame-level talents with real basketball insight. The format is not letting them cook yet. Halftime needs to be more than two analysts and a host taking turns reading scripted segments. The chemistry needs reps and it might come together by next year. For Game 1, the studio show was the lowest-energy part of an already low-energy broadcast.

## The Music and Open

NBC opened the broadcast with the iconic Roundball Rock theme. Real win. The theme is the single most beloved piece of NBA broadcast music in league history and bringing it back for the 2026 NBA on NBC era is the easiest layup of the whole rights deal. We give the music package a flat **A**. Roundball Rock by itself moves the broadcast grade up half a letter.

Where the music package falls short is the in-game cues. NBC's playoff broadcast cues sound generic — recycled from their football and golf playbooks — and do not have the playoff weight that TNT's old NBA music package carried for a generation. The bumps coming out of timeout and into commercial are forgettable. The closing music package after the final buzzer was anonymous. Fix the in-game cues and you have a real broadcast identity. Right now it is Roundball Rock at the open and generic sports network music for the rest of the night.

## What NBC Needs to Fix Before Game 2

Game 2 is Tuesday night at Paycom Center. Three quick fixes NBC could make:

1. **Custom playoff scorebug.** Add a "Western Conference Finals" tag to the scorebug. Use a distinct color treatment for the playoff games. Pop the shot clock. Smallest production change with the biggest visual impact.
2. **On-air court branding bumpers.** If the league will not put the Conference Finals logo on the court, NBC's graphics team can paint it on the broadcast overlay with lower thirds, intro graphics, and timeout bumpers that establish the Conference Finals identity.
3. **Tighten the studio show.** Cut one segment from halftime and let Carter and Anthony actually talk basketball with each other instead of doing scripted reads. The talent is there. The format is the problem.

## The Bottom Line on NBC's NBA Conference Finals Debut

NBC's first Western Conference Finals broadcast did not feel like a Conference Finals broadcast. The scorebug is boring. The graphics package is generic. The studio show is low-energy. The court branding gap (which is the league's fault, not NBC's) was not papered over with any kind of broadcast overlay that signaled "this is the Western Conference Finals." ESPN has spent years setting the gold standard for NBA playoff broadcast identity with the "WESTERN CONFERENCE FINALS" wordmark stretched across the full scorebug. TNT cleared the bar with their version even though we never loved the design. NBC walked into the same round with a template that looks like a regular season Sunday Night Football game. The strongest parts of the broadcast are Mike Tirico in the booth and the Roundball Rock theme at the open. Those two carry the grade out of D territory. Everything else needs a rebuild. The grade is a **C** and the over-under on whether NBC's coverage improves before the NBA Finals starts on ABC is genuinely uncertain. The bar to clear is ESPN's. NBC is not close.

## FAQ: NBC NBA Western Conference Finals Broadcast

**Who calls the NBA on NBC?** Mike Tirico is NBC's lead play-by-play voice with Reggie Miller and Jamal Crawford as color analysts. The studio show is hosted by Maria Taylor with Vince Carter and Carmelo Anthony as primary analysts.

**Is the NBA Western Conference Finals on NBC?** Yes. Every game of the 2026 NBA Western Conference Finals airs on NBC and streams on Peacock. The Eastern Conference Finals air on ESPN with select Sunday games on ABC.

**Did NBC bring back Roundball Rock for the NBA?** Yes. NBC opens every NBA broadcast with the iconic Roundball Rock theme from the original NBA on NBC era. The theme is the single best part of the new NBA on NBC music package.

**Why does the NBA Conference Finals court not say Western Conference Finals?** The NBA has not extended the on-court playoff branding to the Conference Finals. The 2026 NBA Finals court will feature the Finals logo at center court, but the Conference Finals courts use the same regular season floor design. See our [NBA Conference Finals court branding](/stories/nba-conference-finals-court-branding-no-logo) breakdown for the full story.

**How does NBC's NBA coverage compare to ESPN and TNT?** NBC is the worst of the three. ESPN sets the gold standard with the "WESTERN CONFERENCE FINALS" wordmark stretched across the entire scorebug locked around the NBA logo. TNT cleared the bar with their version even though the design feels cramped. NBC put the Conference Finals identity in tiny gray subtext below the scores and looks like a regular season broadcast.

**Which network has the best NBA playoff scorebug?** ESPN. The Western Conference Finals scorebug ESPN ran in the 2024 playoffs put the round identity at the full width of the bug with the NBA logo centered and the ESPN brand block locked in the corner. It is the cleanest, most readable, most playoff-feeling NBA scorebug on television. We hope ESPN runs the same treatment for the 2026 Eastern Conference Finals broadcast.
