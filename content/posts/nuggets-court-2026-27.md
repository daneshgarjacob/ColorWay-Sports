The Nuggets have a new floor for 2026-27, and the easiest way to describe it is that Denver did not add navy or remove navy so much as move it. The color that used to frame the entire court now sits inside the paint, and that single relocation changes how the whole floor reads on television.

Four things are different from the floor Denver played on last season.

## The Navy Borders Are the Real Loss

Last season's floor was framed. A solid navy band ran the sidelines and the baselines, DENVER and NUGGETS sat in white inside it, and MILE HIGH BASKETBALL ran along the bottom in the same block. That band did the single most useful job a court border can do: it gave the wood an edge, so on a wide broadcast shot you saw a defined rectangle of playing surface rather than a pale field that ran out to the seats.

The new floor drops it. The out-of-bounds areas are natural wood now, with DENVER and NUGGETS in navy sitting directly on the grain. It is a lighter, airier look in a still image and a weaker one in motion, because the court no longer has a hard boundary. On a league-wide broadcast slate where the floors that stand out are the ones with real contrast at the edges, Denver just gave up its frame.

The navy did not disappear, though. It moved into the keys, which are now painted solid navy where they used to be wood with an outline. That is the trade at the center of this redesign: a color that used to define the shape of the floor now sits in the two zones where ten players stand, where it spends most of the broadcast partially covered by bodies. Same amount of navy, far less of it doing any work.

## The Mountains Are the Best New Idea

The genuinely good addition is the silhouette. A mountain range with a city skyline tucked under it now runs across the lower half of the floor in a tone-on-tone treatment, slightly darker than the surrounding wood.

This is the right kind of court graphic. It is specific to Denver in a way nothing else on the floor is, it does not fight the lines or the logos, and because it is rendered in wood tone rather than a printed color it reads as part of the surface instead of a sticker applied to it. The restraint is the reason it works. A full-color mountain range would have been a disaster; this version lets you notice it on a replay and forget it during live play, which is exactly the job.

If Denver had added the mountains and kept the navy borders, this would be a considerably better floor.

## The Center Logo Shrank, and It Should Not Have

The circular pickaxe logo at center court is meaningfully smaller than last season's. Center court is the most valuable real estate in the building, the shot every broadcast returns to between whistles, and the one place a team's mark gets shown at scale without competing with anything.

The Nuggets' circular logo is well suited to that spot, since a round mark fills a center circle better than a wordmark or a mascot ever will. Shrinking it leaves a ring of bare wood around the logo and makes the middle of the floor feel emptier than it did, especially now that the borders are wood too. Between the lost navy frame and the reduced center mark, the new court has more undifferentiated wood on screen than any Denver floor in recent memory.

## The Bigger Problem Is the Theme

The floor is downstream of a brand direction we have never been sold on. The Nuggets' current identity leans on navy, gold and a dull maroon, and that maroon is the piece that keeps causing trouble, showing up in the line work here without ever earning its place next to the gold. Denver has one of the great color stories available to it in the sport, a mountain-state palette that could be doing far more, and instead the visual identity keeps arriving muted.

That is the honest frame for this court. It is a competent floor for a brand that is not doing enough, and the two changes that hurt most, the borders and the logo scale, both reduce contrast on a design that already needed more of it.

## The Bottom Line

Denver added the best new element on the floor and removed the best existing one in the same redesign. The mountain and skyline silhouette is a smart, restrained piece of court graphics that belongs on an NBA floor. The navy borders that framed the whole surface are gone, the navy that replaced them sits in the paint where it does the least good, and the center logo lost size it did not need to lose.

The 2026-27 Nuggets court is lighter, flatter, and less defined than the one it replaces, and the one genuinely good addition is not enough to cover it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the new Nuggets court for 2026-27?**

Denver revealed a new Ball Arena floor with natural wood out-of-bounds borders, solid navy painted keys, a mountain range and city skyline silhouette across the lower half, and a smaller circular center logo. DENVER and NUGGETS now sit in navy directly on the wood.

**What changed on the Nuggets court this year?**

Four things: the navy out-of-bounds borders were replaced with natural wood, the keys went from wood to solid navy, a mountain and skyline silhouette was added, and the center logo was reduced in size. MILE HIGH BASKETBALL remains along the bottom, now in navy on wood instead of inside a navy band.

**Did the Nuggets get rid of the blue on their court?**

Not exactly. The navy moved rather than disappeared. It came off the sidelines and baselines and went into the two painted keys, so the floor carries a similar amount of navy but no longer uses it to frame the playing surface.

**Where do the Nuggets play?**

Ball Arena in Denver, Colorado, which the team shares with the Colorado Avalanche of the NHL.

**Is this the Nuggets' only court for 2026-27?**

This is the primary home floor. NBA teams frequently add a separate City Edition or alternate floor later in the season, and nothing further has been announced for Denver.
