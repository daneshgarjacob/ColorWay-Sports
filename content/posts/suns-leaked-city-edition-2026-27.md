The current Phoenix Suns Valley jersey runs the cursive "The Valley" wordmark over the iconic pixelated desert sun and mountain silhouette graphic, all set on an all-black base. The template debuted in 2020-21 and has been the franchise's strongest single uniform design since the Charles Barkley era. The pixel art is one of the most recognizable single elements in modern NBA uniform design and the wordmark is now permanently tied to Phoenix Suns culture. The drawback is the black colorway. Phoenix is not a black-jersey city. The Arizona desert palette is sun-orange and turquoise blue and purple, not black, and the all-black base shrinks the broadcast impact of the desert sun graphic against any opposing primary that is also dark. The template is great. The base color is the weak link. **Grade: B**

## Why Teal Works for Phoenix

Phoenix sits in the middle of the Sonoran Desert. The actual palette of Arizona is turquoise sky, sun-orange and yellow, copper, cactus green, and the soft pink of sandstone at sunset. Black is not on that list. The franchise's primary identity has rotated between Suns purple, orange, and the iconic 1990s teal-turquoise alternates from the Charles Barkley and Kevin Johnson era. Teal is in the Phoenix DNA in a way black never was. A 2026-27 teal Valley jersey reconnects the franchise to its strongest visual era while keeping the modern pixel art that made the current template a Phoenix Suns fan favorite.

The bigger move here is that Phoenix has the chance to build a whole secondary identity around teal again. A teal alternate court for Valley jersey nights. A teal crowd t-shirt takeover for the home playoff openers. Teal merchandise and concourse signage at Footprint Center. Once the jersey is the entry point, the rest of the in-arena package can build off it.
