The NBA All-Star logo is one of the most fun assignments in sports design, because unlike a team mark or even the Finals logo, it gets torn up and rebuilt from scratch every single year around a brand-new host city. That means seventeen completely different logos from 2010 to 2026, and it also means the All-Star mark has swung further stylistically than almost any recurring logo in the sport, from ornate hand-drawn city crests to a clean, standardized template you could set your watch to.

We tracked every one of them from 2010 through 2026 and sorted them into the two clear eras they fall into. Below is the full history, each era graded, and our case for the best and worst All-Star logos of the stretch. For the rest of the league's championship branding, see our [NBA Finals logo history](/stories/nba-finals-logo-history-1986-2025) and [World Series logo history](/stories/world-series-logo-history-1986-2025) breakdowns.

For eight straight years the NBA treated the All-Star logo like a bespoke crest, a one-off emblem hand-built around the host city with no shared template holding it together. This is the creative peak of the whole run. The 2015 New York mark is the boldest idea anyone has ever had for an All-Star logo, literally routing the wordmark through a subway map of the city. The 2016 Toronto logo tucks the entire badge inside a CN Tower silhouette, which is the cleverest single execution on this list. New Orleans got two of the richest crests in the set, the 2014 fleur-de-lis shield and the 2017 wrought-iron gate, and even the more conventional star badges in Dallas, Houston, and Orlando have a depth and craft to them that the league would later sand away.

The reason this era lands at A- instead of A is consistency. When every logo is its own animal, some are going to overcook it, and a few of these do get busy, with heavy bevels, gradients, and drop shadows that date them to their exact year. But we will take ambition that occasionally overreaches over safety every time, and this era swung big almost every year. It is the golden age of the All-Star logo, and it is not particularly close.

Starting in 2018 the league scrapped the bespoke approach and locked into a single template: the NBA logoman on the left, "ALL-STAR" and the year in clean flat type, and the host city on a strip underneath with one small local graphic doing the identifying. On its own terms it works. It is legible at any size, it looks sharp on a broadcast bug and on a hat, and it finally gives the All-Star brand the kind of year-over-year continuity the Finals logo has. As a system, it is a clear professional upgrade.

The problem is that a system is exactly what it is, and the personality got flattened out with the bevels. Most of these are the same lockup with the city name swapped and a token nod bolted on, and you could cover the wordmark on half of them and have no idea where the game was played. The best ones are the ones that push the local element hardest: Chicago in 2020 nails it with the four red stars straight off the city flag, and Indianapolis in 2024 is the most fun of the bunch with its checkered-flag racing star. Cleveland's 2022 mark carried the extra weight of the league's 75th anniversary, and San Francisco's 2025 Golden Gate strip is clean. The clear low point is Atlanta 2021, a stripped-down red-star wordmark thrown together for a hastily-scheduled pandemic All-Star Game that barely reads as a host logo at all. The era gets a B: cohesive and clean, but it traded the soul of the old crests for consistency.

## The Best and Worst NBA All-Star Logos, 2010 to 2026

If we are picking a single best, it comes down to the two New York area and Toronto swings. The 2015 New York subway-map logo is the most creative concept the program has ever produced, and the 2016 Toronto CN Tower badge is the most elegant execution. We give the slight edge to Toronto, because it is both a bold idea and a genuinely clean logo, while the New York mark is a brilliant idea that runs a little busy. Orlando 2012 and the two New Orleans crests round out the top tier.

At the bottom, Atlanta 2021 is the easy pick for worst, a placeholder mark for a placeholder All-Star Game. The rest of the weak spots are less about failure and more about anonymity, the flat-era logos that are perfectly fine and completely forgettable.

## The Bottom Line on the NBA All-Star Logo History

The All-Star logo has quietly become one of the best case studies in sports design, because the league has now clearly shown you both philosophies. From 2010 to 2017 it built an ornate, one-of-a-kind crest for every city and produced the most creative run of logos in the program's history, capped by the New York subway map and the Toronto CN Tower. Since 2018 it has run a clean, consistent template that looks more professional and says a lot less.

We grade the bespoke era an A- and the template era a B, and the gap is the whole point. The modern logos are better built and worse to look at. If the league ever wants to get the magic back, the blueprint is sitting right there in 2015 and 2016, and we would love to see All-Star design swing for the fences again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many different NBA All-Star logos are there from 2010 to 2026?**

Seventeen, one for each year and host city. The NBA rebuilds the All-Star Game logo from scratch every season around the new host, so no two are alike, from the Dallas 2010 star badge to the Los Angeles 2026 mark.

**What is the best NBA All-Star logo?**

We rank the 2016 Toronto logo, which tucks the whole badge inside a CN Tower silhouette, as the best of the 2010 to 2026 run, just ahead of the 2015 New York subway-map design. Both come from the bespoke city-crest era we grade an A-.

**What is the worst NBA All-Star logo?**

Atlanta 2021, a stripped-down red-star wordmark created for a hastily-scheduled pandemic-year All-Star Game. It barely reads as a host-city logo, and it is the clear low point of the modern flat-template era.

**When did the NBA All-Star logo change styles?**

In 2018. From 2010 to 2017 each logo was a unique, ornate city crest. Starting with Los Angeles 2018 the league adopted the flat horizontal template it still uses, with the NBA logoman, "ALL-STAR," the year, and the host city in clean type.
