## What Crucial Catch Is, and How It Changed

Crucial Catch is the NFL's cancer screening campaign with the American Cancer Society. It launched in 2009 as a breast cancer awareness push, which is why so many fans still picture a pink October.

In 2017 the league and the ACS broadened it. After nearly a decade focused on breast cancer, the campaign expanded to early detection and risk reduction for multiple cancers, starting with breast, colorectal and cervical, under the name "Crucial Catch: Intercept Cancer." Today every club supports the league's early detection mission and each year picks one or more cancers to highlight. The league's apparel now carries a multi-colored logo meant to represent all cancers.

Pink has not vanished. The Bears still pair their game with Bears Care's Real Bears Fans Wear Pink, and pink is one of the two main colorways on the 2026 retail line. It is just no longer the whole story. The Steelers describe 2026 as the 18th consecutive season of the partnership.

Every team-confirmed game except Jacksonville's falls on one of those three weekends.

## What Teams Wear for Crucial Catch

Crucial Catch does not change the jersey. Teams wear their normal home or road uniform, which is why every uniform note above is simply the club's regular look for that week. What changes is everything around the jersey.

In its 2025 release, the NFL listed the on-field elements as Crucial Catch game balls, goal post wraps, field-wall banners, and Nike and New Era apparel for players, coaches and officials carrying the multi-colored all-cancers logo. The sideline hats and hoodies are the most visible pieces on a broadcast. In 2024 the league's hats went to a rainbow plaid.

In the breast cancer years the list ran much longer and was all pink: cleats, gloves, wristbands, chin straps, captains' patches, helmet decals and towels. Some players still work pink or multi-colored accessories into their Crucial Catch games, but the league has not published a 2026 equipment list.

Game-worn Crucial Catch gear goes up on NFL Auction after the games, with proceeds going to the American Cancer Society.
