The Rockets finished the job. After [rebranding the uniforms and logos in June](/stories/houston-rockets-2026-rebrand-graded), Houston revealed the floors that go with them: a **Core Court** and a **Statement Court**, both at Toyota Center for 2026-27. One of them earns the rebrand. The other one does not.

The color is the good part. Red lanes, red baselines, and real Championship Yellow doing structural work rather than sitting there as a pinstripe. Bright courts are better than beige ones, and after years of NBA floors drifting toward tasteful nothing, Houston committing to saturated red and mustard is the correct instinct. **More yellow is the right call and they should push it further.**

Then there is the logo, and the logo is the problem.

The **R** rocket mark takes up an enormous share of the center circle, and blown up to floor scale it stops working. The trail of exhaust lines under the rocket reads as clean at jersey size and reads as clutter at forty feet across. The yellow outline around the whole thing softens the edge exactly where the mark needed to stay sharp against pale hardwood. What should be the most confident object in the building instead looks like a decal that got scaled past its comfort zone.

That is the trouble with putting a mark this detailed at center court. A floor logo gets seen from the 400 level, on a broadcast wide shot, and upside down from the opposite bench. It has to survive all three. This one only really works in the overhead press photo.

**D-.** The palette is genuinely good and the yellow is a real improvement. The centerpiece undoes it.

<img src="/images/posts/houston-rockets-court-2026-27-graded/statement-court.jpg" alt="Houston Rockets Statement Court at Toyota Center for 2026-27, light hardwood with a large black Dunkstronaut astronaut logo outlined in red at center court" style="width:100%;display:block;margin:1.4em auto;border-radius:12px;border:1px solid #ececec;" />

The **Dunkstronaut at center court is the best idea in this entire rebrand.** A dunking astronaut, rendered as a single bold silhouette, is exactly what a floor logo should be: one shape, readable instantly, impossible to confuse with any other team in the league. It is the rare mascot mark that gets *better* the larger you print it, which is the opposite of the R.

The execution is where it slips. The astronaut is rendered almost entirely in black, and that much black on pale hardwood turns the middle of the floor into a hole. From a broadcast camera the center circle reads as an absence rather than a graphic. Houston's identity is red and yellow, and this is the one surface in the building where neither of them shows up in the element everyone is looking at.

Swap that black fill for red with a black outline, or even keep the silhouette black but let Championship Yellow carry the detailing, and this is comfortably the best floor in the conference. As delivered it is a great mark in a palette that fights it.

**C+.** The idea earns a much higher grade than the execution does.

## Why One Court Is So Much Better Than the Other

The two floors make an unusually clean case study, because they fail and succeed for the same underlying reason: **scale**.

The R is a badge mark. It was designed to be understood at the size of a chest logo, where the exhaust trails and the yellow keyline read as craft. Enlarged to center court, that craft becomes visual noise, and the mark loses the clarity it had at three inches tall.

The Dunkstronaut is a silhouette. Silhouettes scale, which is why leagues build logos out of them, and why the astronaut still lands from the upper bowl. Houston already owns a mark that was built for this job. On the Core Court, they used the other one.

## The Bottom Line

Houston did the hard part right. The rebrand gave them a genuine color story, mustard yellow is back after thirty years, and the Dunkstronaut is a legitimately great piece of design. The floors do not fully cash that in. The Core Court pairs a good palette with a center logo that does not survive the scale, and the Statement Court pairs a great center logo with a fill color that drains the floor of everything the rebrand was about. **Core Court D-, Statement Court C+**, and both problems are fixable without touching the color scheme.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Did the Houston Rockets get a new court for 2026-27?**
Yes. The Rockets revealed two new Toyota Center floors in August 2026, a Core Court and a Statement Court, completing the rebrand they started with new uniforms and logos in June.

**What is the Dunkstronaut?**
The Dunkstronaut is Houston's astronaut-dunking secondary mark, made a permanent part of the identity in the 2026 rebrand. It appears at center court on the Statement Court floor.

**What is on the Rockets Core Court?**
Light hardwood with the primary red **R** rocket logo outlined in Championship Yellow at center, red painted lanes, and red baselines with yellow accents.

**How many courts do the Rockets have?**
Two for 2026-27: the Core Court for most home games, and the Statement Court that pairs with the Statement Edition uniform.

**What grades did the new Rockets courts get?**
The Core Court gets a D- and the Statement Court gets a C+. The color direction is strong on both. The Core Court's center logo is too detailed to work at floor scale, and the Statement Court's Dunkstronaut is rendered in a black that reads as a hole in the middle of the floor.
