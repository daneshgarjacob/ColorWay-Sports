The Colorado Avalanche were supposed to be burgundy and green. A color printer in 1995 turned the green into a muted blue, the owners and the commissioner liked it, and the mistake has been on the sweater for 30 years.

The San Jose Sharks picked teal by calling clothing stores. Neiman Marcus, L.L. Bean, J. Crew, Bloomingdale's and Starter were each asked which color would still sell in five to 10 years, and every one of them said teal.

And the Boston Bruins started out brown, because the owner wanted the team to match his grocery stores.

Those are three of the 32. We went back to the start for every NHL team, using NHL.com, the clubs' own histories and press releases, The Hockey News and newspaper archives. Where the famous story doesn't hold up, we say so. Where a team never explained its choice, we say that too. This is the follow-up to [How Every NHL Team Got Its Name](/stories/how-every-nhl-team-got-its-name) and the hockey version of [How Every NFL Team Got Its Colors](/stories/how-every-nfl-team-got-its-colors), [How Every MLB Team Got Its Colors](/stories/how-every-mlb-team-got-its-colors) and [How Every NBA Team Got Its Colors](/stories/how-every-nba-team-got-its-colors).

## Quick Facts: NHL Team Colors

- **From a printing mistake:** the Avalanche. The blue was meant to be forest green.
- **From a store survey:** the Sharks' Pacific Teal.
- **From a grocery chain:** the Bruins' first brown and gold.
- **From a college football team:** the Flyers' orange, credited to co-founder Bill Putnam, a former University of Texas quarterback.
- **From a city's other teams:** the Penguins switched to black and gold in 1980 to match the Steelers and Pirates. The Bruins protested and lost.
- **From the military:** the Jets' Polar Night Blue matches Royal Canadian Air Force aircraft paint, and the Blue Jackets' Union Blue honors Ohio's Civil War soldiers.
- **From a movie studio:** the Mighty Ducks' eggplant and jade, from Disney in 1993.
- **Never explained:** the Panthers, Lightning and Hurricanes have no recorded reason for their original palettes.

## Atlantic Division

### Boston Bruins

The Bruins began in brown. When grocery owner Charles F. Adams started the team in 1924, NHL.com says he dressed it in "brown and yellow – the colors of owner Charles F. Adams' First National grocery store chain." The Boston Globe describes the stores as trimmed in brown and gold. Brown gave way to black in the mid-1930s, and the Globe dates the switch to November 1935. Some later accounts say the supermarkets were black and gold. They were not. Black, gold and white have been the Bruins' colors ever since, and the same history made the Bruins the target of a famous protest against Pittsburgh in 1980 (see the Penguins below). ### Buffalo Sabres

The Sabres have been blue and gold since brothers Seymour and Northrup Knox launched the team in 1970. The Buffalo News has reported that the colors came from the Knox family's polo pinnies, the blue, gold and white the family called its "Aurora colors," after the East Aurora polo club. We could not see the original document, so treat that as the reported story. In 1996 the team switched to black and red with a buffalo-head logo, and it never explained why. Blue and gold returned in 2006, first in navy. In 2020 the team announced a "Return to Royal," bringing back the royal blue, gold and white of 1970. ### Detroit Red Wings

Red was already in Detroit before the Red Wings were. The 1926 Detroit Cougars wore red and white with an Old English D, and the Falcons that followed kept red and white. When James Norris bought the club in 1932, he renamed it the Red Wings and brought the winged wheel from the Montreal amateur athletic club he had played for, the "Winged Wheelers." NHL.com confirms the Montreal source of the crest. A Detroit Historical Society curator has suggested the red resembled the flags of Norris's shipping business, but that is a theory, not a record. So Norris gave Detroit the name and the wheel. The red was there first. Red and white are still the only two colors. ### Florida Panthers

The Panthers have worn red, navy and gold since their first season in 1993, under owner Wayne Huizenga. The team has never given a reason for those colors, and the explanations online about passion and sunshine have no source behind them. The palette survived the 2016 rebrand under owner Vincent Viola, when the gold went flatter and a new shield logo arrived. That shield draws on the 101st Airborne's eagle patch, the division Viola served in, and the shoulder patch carries the Florida state flag. Executive Doug Cifu said the team "really wanted to establish our own identity." The colors are red, navy and gold. ### Montreal Canadiens

The Canadiens were not always bleu-blanc-rouge. Uniform historians describe the first sweater, in 1909-10, as blue and white with no red. The next season the team wore a red sweater with a green maple leaf. The red, white and blue look settled in around 1912-13, and NHL.com says the C with an A inside appeared in 1913, with the A swapped for an H in 1917. The H stands for hockey, from Club de hockey Canadien. It does not stand for "Habitants." The team has never published a reason for adding red. ### Ottawa Senators

The modern Senators borrowed their colors from the original Ottawa club, one of the oldest in hockey. Historian Paul Kitchen's history of that club says it wore red and black as early as the 1884 Montreal Winter Carnival, and its famous red, black and white barber-pole sweaters date to the late 1890s. When Ottawa returned to the NHL in 1992, it went back to red, black and white, with gold in the Roman centurion logo. In 2020 owner Eugene Melnyk brought back that original 1992 logo and called it a "return to our heritage." The colors today are red, black and white with gold trim. ### Tampa Bay Lightning

The Lightning started in 1992 in black, blue and silver, and the team never recorded why. Everything changed on Jan. 31, 2011, under new owner Jeff Vinik and general manager Steve Yzerman. NBC Sports reported that the team "decided to shelve the black and silver in the logo in favor of a simple blue and white scheme." The goals were a look that was bold, classic and hockey-first, and for the first time the crest had no wordmark. Many fans saw a Red Wings influence in the new striping, given Yzerman's career in Detroit, but no one on record has said that was the plan. The colors are blue and white. ### Toronto Maple Leafs

Toronto's team was green before it was blue. Conn Smythe's group bought the St. Patricks in February 1927 and renamed them the Maple Leafs, and NHL.com says Smythe "soon changes the team colors to blue and white from green and white." The team's longtime publicist, Stan Obodiac, used to say Smythe chose blue for the Canadian sky and white for the snow. That is the club's favorite story, but we found no record of Smythe saying it himself. His real reason is unrecorded. Blue and white have been the colors for almost 100 years. ### Carolina Hurricanes

The Hurricanes' red and black came with the move from Hartford. The Whalers wore blue, green and silver. Karmanos said the team wanted "a traditional look." The colors match NC State, which shared the team's arena, but no team source says that was the reason. ### Columbus Blue Jackets

The Blue Jackets' blue is a Civil War uniform. The team's own history says the name, picked in a 1997 fan contest, honors Ohio's role in the war. Ohio sent a larger share of its people to the Union Army than any other state, and many Union blue coats were made in Columbus. Founding owner John H. McConnell, a Navy veteran of World War II, insisted on blue. The base look was red, white and blue, and the 1997 identity also had a lime green that was later dropped. The colors are usually listed as Union Blue, Goal Red, Capital Silver and white, though the team's own history does not use those names. ### New Jersey Devils

The Devils spent their first 10 seasons in red and green. The franchise arrived from Colorado in 1982, where the Rockies wore blue, red and yellow. The Devils' history page says only that the team "found new colors to match their new home" in red, green and white. Owner John McMullen's wife, Jackie, came up with the logo idea. Fans called the look the Christmas colors, but that was never a design reason. Green was replaced by black for 1992-93, and the team never made a public statement about why. ### New York Islanders

The Islanders have worn blue and orange since 1972. The usual explanation is Nassau County, home of their first arena, and the county's colors do match. Nassau's official flag, adopted in 1899, is orange, and its seal shows a gold lion on a blue field, both tied to the Dutch House of Nassau. We found no team or newspaper record that says the club chose its colors because of the county, so we call it a match, not a proven reason. One popular claim is simply wrong: New York State's colors are blue and gold, not blue and orange. The colors are royal blue, orange and white. ### New York Rangers

The Rangers have worn blue, red and white since 1926. Madison Square Garden boss Tex Rickard founded the team, and the Garden already had a tenant, the New York Americans, who wore star-spangled red, white and blue. Hockey historian Stan Fischler writes that Rickard wanted his team to stand apart, so the Rangers put "RANGERS" diagonally across a blue sweater. No record gives Rickard's reason for the colors themselves. The diagonal lettering has outlasted almost everything else in the league, and the Penguins copied it in 1967. ### Philadelphia Flyers

The Flyers' orange came from Texas. Ed Snider usually gets the credit, but the colors were co-founder Bill Putnam's idea. The owners wanted a "hot" color, and red was already taken by the Red Wings and Canadiens. Putnam had played quarterback at the University of Texas, and Hall of Fame hockey writer Jay Greenberg says the Longhorns inspired the orange. The team started with orange and white and added black. Sam Ciccone of the Philadelphia ad firm Mel Richman Inc. designed the logo and first uniforms, and the Philadelphia Inquirer reports that three models showed off the colors at the 1967 naming event. ### Pittsburgh Penguins

The Penguins started in light blue and white in 1967. In January 1980, looking for more fan support, they switched to black and gold to match the Steelers and the Pirates, who had just won the Super Bowl and World Series. Both of those teams' colors come from the city flag. The first game in black and gold was Jan. 30, 1980. The Bruins formally protested. "I protested like hell over it because we'd had those colors for 60 years," Boston general manager Harry Sinden said. Pittsburgh answered that its 1920s hockey Pirates had worn black and yellow, and the league sided with the Penguins. As the Boston Globe tells it, the league's research also turned up that the Bruins had started in brown. Today's colors are black, Pittsburgh Gold and white. ### Washington Capitals

The Capitals began in 1974 in red, white and blue, and the team's own jersey timeline calls the look patriotic. No designer or reason is on record beyond the obvious fit for the capital city. On June 22, 1995, the team switched to blue, black and bronze with the Screaming Eagle logo, again with no stated reason. Exactly 12 years later, on June 22, 2007, owner Ted Leonsis brought back red, white and blue, and the team said the change strengthened its ties to Washington, D.C. ## Central Division

### Chicago Blackhawks

The Blackhawks' first sweater was designed by a famous ballroom dancer. NHL.com and Cornell University credit Irene Castle, the wife of founding owner Frederic McLaughlin, with designing the 1926 logo and uniforms. The team started in black and white and won its first Stanley Cup in 1934 in those colors. Red came in during the 1930s, and the red home sweater the team still wears has been in place since 1955, by the club's own count. No record explains why Castle picked black and white. The colors are red, black and white, and the 2025-26 Centennial sweater added gold trim based on the earliest sweaters. The Avalanche's blue was a printing mistake. When the Quebec Nordiques moved to Denver in 1995, marketing executive Shawn Hunter said burgundy "will be the next great color in sports." ### Dallas Stars

The Stars' green goes back to Minnesota. The North Stars started in green and gold in 1967. President Walter Bush Jr. said at the time that the team ruled out any combination that looked like other NHL teams or the local Gophers, Twins and Vikings. Green and gold moved to Dallas in 1993. On June 4, 2013, the team dropped gold, added silver and introduced Victory Green, a mix of kelly green and forest green that no other NHL team wears. The team's reasons were that stars look silver in the night sky, not gold, and that gold was hard to match across materials. The colors are Victory Green, silver, black and white. ### Minnesota Wild

The Wild's colors are named after the state. The four colors are Iron Range Red, Forest Green, Harvest Gold and Minnesota Wheat. The names point to northern Minnesota's iron mining region, its forests and its farms. The team has not published a color-by-color explanation, so that is as far as the record goes. The palette has not changed since 1998. ### Winnipeg Jets

The Jets' colors are Air Force colors. When the team returned in 2011, its logo was built on the Royal Canadian Air Force roundel with a CF-18 Hornet silhouette. The CBC and the Globe and Mail report that Polar Night Blue matches the paint on many current RCAF aircraft and Aviator Blue reflects the Air Force's historical colors. The uniforms were unveiled at CFB Winnipeg, with players walking out of an RCAF Hercules, and a contract with the Department of National Defence governs the use of the roundel. The original WHA Jets of 1972 wore blue, red and white. The colors today are Polar Night Blue, Aviator Blue, silver and white, with red accents. ### Los Angeles Kings

The Kings started in purple and gold because their owner also owned the Lakers. Jack Kent Cooke founded the team in 1967 and put both clubs in the same colors at his Forum. Purple came back in the late 1990s and was gone again by about 2011. The colors are black, silver and white. ### San Jose Sharks

The Sharks picked teal by asking clothing stores. Before the team's 1991 debut, creative lead Matt Levine wanted a color that did not look traditional. He asked Neiman Marcus, L.L. Bean, J. Crew, Bloomingdale's and Starter which color would stay in style for five to 10 years, and all of them said teal. The team then chose one shade out of about 20. Jersey maker CCM wrote to urge a standard blue, because teal needed new yarn, and a burgundy, silver and black scheme was also rejected. The idea that teal stands for the Pacific Ocean came later. The Sharks did $150 million in merchandise sales in their first year. The colors are Pacific Teal, black, white and orange. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why are the Flyers orange?**

Co-founder Bill Putnam picked it. The owners wanted a "hot" color and red was taken, and Putnam, a former University of Texas quarterback, was inspired by the Longhorns, according to hockey writer Jay Greenberg.

**Why are the Penguins black and gold?**

In January 1980 the team switched from blue to black and gold to match the Steelers and Pirates, whose colors come from the Pittsburgh city flag. The Bruins protested, and the NHL ruled for Pittsburgh.

**Why do the Kings wear black and silver instead of purple?**

Founder Jack Kent Cooke put the Kings in the same purple and gold as his Lakers in 1967. Owner Bruce McNall switched to silver and black in August 1988, the month Wayne Gretzky arrived.

**Why are the Bruins black and gold?**

The team started in brown and gold in 1924 to match owner Charles Adams's grocery stores. Brown became black in the mid-1930s.

**Why are the Avalanche burgundy and blue?**

Burgundy was the team's pick in 1995. The blue was meant to be forest green, but a printer rendered it as blue, and the owners and Gary Bettman liked it.

**Why are the Sharks teal?**

In 1991 the team asked major clothing retailers which color would stay in style, and all of them said teal. The Pacific Ocean link came later.

**Why are the Maple Leafs blue and white?**

Conn Smythe changed the team from the St. Patricks' green and white to blue and white in 1927. The team's story is blue for the sky and white for the snow, but Smythe's actual reason is not recorded.

**Why were the Devils red and green?**

The team chose red, green and white when it arrived from Colorado in 1982. Green was replaced by black for the 1992-93 season.

**Why are the Winnipeg Jets' colors blue?**

The 2011 identity is built on the Royal Canadian Air Force. Polar Night Blue matches current RCAF aircraft paint, and Aviator Blue reflects the Air Force's historical colors.

**Are the Islanders' colors from Nassau County?**

The colors match. Nassau County's flag is orange and its seal is blue and gold, but we found no record that the team picked them for that reason.

**Which NHL team has changed colors the most?**

The Kings, Canucks and Sabres have each left their original colors and come back. The Kings went from purple and gold to black and silver, the Canucks went to the Flying V and back, and the Sabres went to black and red and back.

## The Bottom Line

Hockey colors are older than most, and a lot of them came from one person: a grocer in Boston, a Texas quarterback in Philadelphia, a dancer in Chicago, a logo designer in Vancouver. The newer teams explain every shade in a press release. The older ones mostly never explained anything, and the stories filled in later. And when a team leaves its colors, it tends to come back, as Buffalo, Vancouver, Washington and Calgary all did.
